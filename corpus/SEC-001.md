# Security Incident Response Runbook

## 1. Purpose

When something smells wrong — a phishing click, a lost laptop, an alert that will not explain itself — this runbook tells you exactly what to do, in order. It is written for every employee; the deeply technical playbooks used by the response team live separately in the security workspace.

## 2. What counts as an incident

Anything that could affect the confidentiality, integrity or availability of company or customer data. Typical examples: a clicked phishing link, credentials possibly exposed, a lost or stolen device, malware warnings, an unknown login notification, a vendor telling you they were breached. When unsure, treat it as an incident — false alarms are free.

## 3. Step one: report

Report a suspected security incident to security@kranich.example within 24 hours of noticing it — sooner is always better. One paragraph is enough: what you saw, when, on which device or system, and what you already did. Reporting is also the rule when the mistake was yours; a prompt report is explicitly a good look at Kranich, never a bad one.

## 4. Step two: contain what you safely can

- Phishing click: disconnect from networks, leave the device on, report.
- Lost or stolen device: report immediately — remote wipe is triggered by IT; also rotate any credential you typed on that device recently.
- Suspicious login alert: change the affected password in 1Password and enable/verify MFA, then report.

Do not investigate on your own beyond this list — well-meant digging destroys evidence.

## 5. What happens next

The on-call responder from the security team acknowledges your report, classifies severity, and owns communication from there. You may be asked follow-up questions; incident channels are named `#inc-…` and are invite-only while the incident runs.

Example: when Deniz reported a convincing "shared invoice" phish that three people had received, the responder blocked the sender domain, checked click logs, and closed the incident the same afternoon — with a thank-you note in #security.

## 6. Customer and personal data

If customer data or personal data may be affected, the responder brings in the privacy team; regulator and customer notifications are their call, on their timelines (see POL-010). You never notify customers about an incident yourself.

## 7. After the incident

Every real incident gets a short, blameless write-up: what happened, what worked, what we change. The write-ups are readable by everyone in the security workspace — the point of an incident is to make the next one boring.
