# Acceptable Use Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software provides its employees with devices, accounts, and access to internal tools so that everyone can do their best work, wherever they are. This policy explains how we expect those resources to be used — and how we protect each other, our customers, and the company in the process.

This policy applies to all company-issued devices, corporate accounts, network access, and any system you use for work purposes, including personal devices when they are used to access company resources.

We trust you to use good judgment. This policy is not a list of every possible rule. If you are unsure whether something is appropriate, ask your manager or write to security@kranich.example.

## 2. Do

Use company resources primarily for work. Occasional personal use is fine as long as it does not interfere with your responsibilities, consume excessive bandwidth, or put company data at risk.

Keep your accounts secure. Use 1Password to generate and store strong, unique passwords. Enable multi-factor authentication wherever it is offered. Never share your passwords or passphrases with anyone, including colleagues or IT support.

Protect customer data and internal information. Only access data you need to do your job. When you handle customer information, follow the access rules of your team and the safeguards described in our information security policies, including POL-004.

Lock your screen whenever you step away from your device, even at home. Use the screen lock feature on your phone and laptop.

Report security concerns promptly. If you suspect a phishing message, a lost device, or any unusual activity on a company account, contact security@kranich.example right away. It is always better to report and be wrong than to stay silent.

Be thoughtful about what you install. Only install software that you need for work and that is approved through our normal procurement process. If you need something unusual, ask it-help@kranich.example first.

Use company communication tools for work-related conversations. When you discuss sensitive topics, prefer channels our team has chosen for their security properties.

Respect the privacy of your colleagues. Do not record meetings or capture screenshots of conversations without asking everyone involved.

When you leave the company or change roles, return or reassign company resources as instructed by Workplace & Operations and People & Culture.

## 3. Don't

Do not use company resources for illegal activity, for personal financial gain, or to run an outside business.

Do not access, store, or distribute content that is discriminatory, harassing, or sexually explicit. This includes material that would violate our workplace conduct expectations.

Do not bypass security controls. This includes disabling antivirus software, removing device management profiles, or attempting to circumvent network protections.

Do not share your credentials or let anyone else use your accounts. If a colleague needs access to a system you use, direct them to the appropriate owner or to it-help@kranich.example.

Do not send company data to personal accounts unless you have an explicit business need and approval from your manager. This includes forwarding work email to personal addresses or uploading files to personal cloud storage.

Do not use unapproved file-sharing services for customer data or internal confidential information. When in doubt, use the tools provided by Platform & Infrastructure.

Do not connect unauthorized devices to the company network. If you need to connect a personal device for a specific purpose, check with it-help@kranich.example first.

Do not use company resources to access or distribute pirated software, media, or other copyrighted material without permission.

Do not make purchases or enter contracts using company accounts unless you have the authority to do so under our purchasing and expense policies, which are available on Grove.

Do not ignore security warnings. If your browser, email client, or device shows a warning about a site, an attachment, or a certificate, stop and report it.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, receives an email that appears to come from a client's procurement team, asking her to open a document and log in with her Kranich credentials. The sender address looks slightly wrong. Deniz does not click the link. She forwards the message to security@kranich.example and notifies her manager. This is exactly the right response.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, sometimes uses a shared workstation at a client site. He always logs out of his Kranich accounts at the end of the session and never saves passwords in the browser. When he is unsure whether the client's network is trustworthy, he uses his company laptop's VPN.

## 4. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, or about whether a particular use is acceptable, ask your manager or write to security@kranich.example. For questions about the tools mentioned here, such as 1Password or Perch, contact it-help@kranich.example.

For questions about how this policy interacts with employment expectations, contact people@kranich.example. Information Security reviews this policy periodically and may update it as our tools and ways of working evolve. You will be notified of significant changes through Grove.
