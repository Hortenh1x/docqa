# Budget Guidelines

## 1. Purpose

This document explains how budget planning works at Kranich Software GmbH. It covers the annual planning cycle, how we monitor spending against the plan, and when we reforecast. It is written for everyone who owns a budget or contributes to one — from team leads to department heads.

A budget is a promise we make to each other. It lets the company invest in the right things and gives teams clarity on what they can spend. This guide helps you plan realistically, track your spending, and raise concerns early.

## 2. Planning cycle

Our fiscal year is the calendar year. Planning for the next year starts in the autumn of the current year. Finance coordinates the cycle and publishes a timeline on the Grove intranet ahead of each planning season.

Each team prepares a budget proposal for the coming year. Proposals are based on the team's goals, expected headcount, and planned initiatives. Finance reviews proposals with each team lead and consolidates them into a company-wide plan.

The leadership team approves the final plan before the start of the new year. Once approved, the plan is the baseline against which we measure actual spending. Budgets are set at the cost-center level. Each cost center has a named owner who is responsible for managing spending within that center.

If you are a cost-center owner, you will receive guidance from Finance during the planning cycle. You do not need to build the plan alone — Finance is there to help you estimate costs and challenge assumptions where needed.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, owns the cost center for client workshops. During planning, she works with Finance to estimate travel and material costs for the coming year based on the expected number of workshops.

## 3. Variance monitoring

Access: Finance only

Once the year starts, we compare actual spending against the plan on a regular basis. Finance produces a variance report each month. The report shows, for each cost center, the planned spend, the actual spend, and the difference between the two.

Cost-center owners are alerted when spend deviates by more than 7% from plan. The alert is sent by email from Finance. It includes a short summary of where the variance comes from and a request to review.

When you receive an alert, please respond promptly. You do not need to prepare a full analysis. A short reply explaining the reason for the variance — for example, a delayed hire or an unexpected invoice — is enough. Finance will follow up if more detail is needed.

If you expect a variance to continue for the rest of the year, mention that in your reply. This helps Finance decide whether a reforecast is needed, as described in the next section.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, notices that his travel costs are higher than planned because of an unusually busy quarter. He receives an alert from Finance and replies that the increase is temporary and should level out by mid-year.

## 4. Reforecasts

A reforecast is an updated version of the plan for the current year. It is not a new budget — it is a revised expectation of what we will actually spend. Reforecasts help us keep the plan realistic without changing the accountability of cost-center owners.

Finance decides when a reforecast is needed. Typically, this happens once or twice a year, or sooner if there is a significant change in circumstances — for example, a major new client project or a change in team structure.

When a reforecast is triggered, Finance asks cost-center owners to review their remaining spend for the year and submit updated figures. The process is lighter than the annual planning cycle. You will receive clear instructions and a deadline of a few days.

The reforecast is reviewed by Finance and approved by the leadership team. Once approved, the reforecast becomes the new baseline for variance monitoring for the rest of the year.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, learns that her team will hire two additional engineers in the second half of the year. She submits a reforecast that reflects the higher personnel costs. Finance approves the update, and variance monitoring continues against the new baseline.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this guide, please reach out to Finance. For questions about your specific cost center, contact your Finance business partner. For general budgeting questions, the Grove intranet has a frequently asked questions page that we keep up to date.

If you notice something in this guide that seems out of date, please let us know. We review our policies regularly and update them when our ways of working change.
