# Parental Leave Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH wants to support you and your family during one of life’s most meaningful transitions. This policy explains how parental leave works at our company: what you can expect from us, what we ask of you, and how we will stay connected while you are away.

We believe that welcoming a child is a personal event, and we aim to be flexible and respectful of your individual circumstances. This policy covers leave for the birth of a child, for adoption, and for placement into foster care.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of your location or your employment contract type, as long as you are on the active payroll. It covers employees working from our Berlin office, our Lisbon office, and those working remotely from Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom.

The policy applies to all parents: birth parents, adoptive parents, and foster parents. It also applies regardless of gender, marital status, or family structure. We want every parent at Kranich to feel equally supported.

Where local law provides more generous rights than this policy, the local law applies. Where this policy is more generous, this policy applies. Your manager or the People & Culture team can help you understand which rules apply in your specific situation.

## 3. Telling us

Please tell People & Culture at least 7 weeks before the planned start of the leave. This gives us enough time to arrange your cover, plan your handover, and make sure all administrative steps are complete.

You do not need to share sensitive medical information with us. A simple note confirming that you expect to start parental leave on a certain date is enough. If you prefer, you can first speak informally with your manager or with a member of the People & Culture team to explore your options before you make a formal request.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, told his manager and People & Culture about his planned leave shortly after the medical confirmation. He sent a short email with his expected start date, and People & Culture responded with the next steps.

Once you have told us, we will confirm your leave dates in writing and send you a summary of what happens next. If your plans change, just let us know as soon as you can. We understand that due dates and adoption processes can shift.

## 4. Leave and pay

You are entitled to parental leave in line with this policy and with the local statutory framework in your country of employment. We ask that you take a minimum continuous period of leave after the child arrives, so that you and your family have time to settle.

During your leave, you will receive pay according to the rules that apply in your country. In Germany, for example, you may be eligible for state parental allowance, and we will top this up where our policy is more generous. In Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom, similar arrangements exist. People & Culture will explain the exact financial flow for your situation before your leave starts.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, asked People & Culture whether her pay during leave would come from the state or from Kranich. Lea, the people operations specialist, walked her through the process and confirmed that Kranich would handle the paperwork with the relevant authorities.

If both parents work at Kranich, each parent is entitled to their own leave under this policy. The leave is individual and does not need to be shared.

## 5. Before the leave

We want your leave to start smoothly. About a month before your planned start date, please arrange a handover conversation with your manager and your team. Together, you will decide how to distribute your ongoing tasks and who will cover your responsibilities while you are away.

Please make sure that your projects, documents, and code repositories are in a clear state. Write short handover notes where helpful, and update your calendar and your status in our internal tools. If you use Perch for desk bookings or Fern for personal data, you do not need to do anything special, but you may want to set an out-of-office message on your email.

Your manager will also let you know about any important deadlines or events that might occur during your leave, so that you are not surprised when you return. We do not expect you to do any work during your leave, and we will not contact you about routine matters.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, prepared a handover document for her team two weeks before her leave. She listed the open pull requests, noted where she had left her current work, and scheduled a short walkthrough with Ines Duarte, her engineering manager. Ines confirmed that the team would handle everything in Anna’s absence.

## 6. During the leave

During your parental leave, you are off work. You should not check work email, attend meetings, or respond to messages. We will respect your time away, and we ask you to respect it too, so that you can fully focus on your family.

You will remain an employee of Kranich throughout your leave. Your health insurance and other benefits continue as before, to the extent permitted by local law. You will still have access to your personal accounts, such as 1Password for passwords and Ledgerly for expenses, but you should not need to use them for work purposes.

If you wish, you can stay loosely connected with your team in a social way. Some parents enjoy an occasional coffee chat with a colleague, while others prefer no contact at all. Both are fine. We will follow your lead.

If an urgent matter arises that truly requires your input, your manager will contact you first and ask whether you are willing to help. You are never obligated to say yes. In practice, urgent matters are rare because we plan properly before your leave starts.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, told her team that she would prefer no work contact during her leave. Her team respected this completely. When a question came up about a roadmap item she had owned, her colleague handled it without reaching out to Sofia.

## 7. Returning to work

We will agree on your return date before your leave starts, and we will confirm it in writing. If you want to change that date, please let People & Culture know as soon as possible so we can adjust plans.

About two weeks before your return, your manager will reach out to schedule a reintegration conversation. This is a chance to catch up on what happened while you were away, to discuss any changes to the team or the product, and to plan your first weeks back. You will also receive a short update from your team so that you do not feel lost on day one.

Your first days back should be gentle. We suggest that you start with a half day or a short week, if that helps, and we encourage you to ease into your workload. Your manager will help you prioritize and will make sure you are not overwhelmed.

When you return, you will find that your accounts and access are still active. If you notice any issue, contact it-help@kranich.example and we will resolve it promptly.

Example: Sofia returned from her leave on a Wednesday. Her manager had scheduled a reintegration meeting for Thursday morning, and her team had prepared a written summary of recent decisions. By the following week, Sofia felt up to date and ready to take back her responsibilities.

## 8. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, our people operations specialist, can help you understand the process, and Aylin Demir, our VP People & Culture, is available for more complex conversations.

For questions about how your leave interacts with local law in your country, we can connect you with the relevant information or, where needed, with external advice. We want you to feel confident and supported throughout your parental leave journey.

If you have concerns about confidentiality or fairness, you can also contact security@kranich.example, and we will treat your query with discretion. We are here to help, and we thank you for being part of the Kranich community.
