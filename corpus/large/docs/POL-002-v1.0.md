# Remote Work Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we believe that great work happens when people have the flexibility to do their best work in the environment that suits them. This policy explains how remote work operates across our company, whether you are based near our Berlin or Lisbon offices or working from home in one of the countries where we employ people.

We are a remote-first company. Most of us work from home within our country of employment, and we come together in person when it helps collaboration, team bonding, or client meetings. This policy sets clear expectations so that everyone, regardless of their location, knows what is available to them and what we ask in return.

This policy applies to all employees. Your manager and the People & Culture team are happy to discuss how it applies to your specific role and situation.

## 2. Where you may work

Employees may work up to 60% of their working time remotely. This means that, over a typical month, you can spend the majority of your working days outside the office — at home or in another suitable location of your choosing — as long as your role allows it and you remain productive and reachable.

The remaining time is expected to be spent in the office: either at our headquarters in Berlin (the Ritterstraße office) or at our second office in Lisbon (the Rua do Alecrim office), depending on your contractual location. We value the time we spend together in person, whether for team meetings, planning sessions, or informal exchanges that build trust and camaraderie.

If you work remotely from a location outside your country of employment for more than a few days, you must speak with your manager and the People & Culture team in advance. There may be tax, social security, or data protection implications that we need to consider. In general, working from your home within your country of employment — Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom — is straightforward and requires no special approval beyond what is described here.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, works from home on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday. On Wednesday and Friday, she comes into the Rua do Alecrim office to pair with her colleagues and join team discussions. This arrangement fits within the 60% remote allowance and requires no formal request.

For employees whose role requires them to be client-facing or on-site at a customer location, such as field solutions engineers, the remote work allowance applies to the time not spent at client sites. If you are unsure how this applies to you, please speak with your manager.

## 3. Working hours and availability

Remote work does not change the expectations around your working hours. We trust you to structure your day in a way that lets you do your best work while also being available for collaboration with your team and colleagues.

As a general principle, we expect you to be reachable during the core hours that your team agrees upon. Many teams have colleagues across different time zones, from Lisbon to Warsaw, so we ask everyone to be thoughtful about scheduling meetings and to respect each other's focus time.

When you work remotely, you should:

- Keep your calendar up to date, including your working hours and any breaks.
- Respond to messages and meeting invitations promptly during your working hours.
- Let your team know if you are unavailable for an extended period during the day.
- Join meetings with your camera on when possible, so that remote participants feel equally included.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, starts her day at eight in the morning so she can support clients in earlier time zones. She blocks an hour for lunch and finishes at four in the afternoon. Her calendar reflects these hours, and her team knows when to expect her.

We do not prescribe specific start or end times, because we know that personal circumstances differ. What matters is that you deliver on your responsibilities and that your team can rely on you. If your working pattern changes, update your calendar and let your manager know.

## 4. Equipment and workspace

When you work remotely, you are responsible for setting up a workspace that allows you to work effectively and comfortably. Kranich Software provides the equipment you need to do your job, and we expect you to take good care of it.

For your remote workspace, we recommend:

- A quiet area where you can take calls and focus without frequent interruptions.
- A desk or table at a comfortable height, and a chair that supports good posture.
- Good lighting, ideally natural light, to reduce eye strain.
- A reliable internet connection that supports video calls and access to our cloud tools.

If you need additional equipment to make your home workspace suitable, such as a monitor, keyboard, or headset, please reach out to the Workplace & Operations team. They will arrange for the equipment to be shipped to you or, where possible, for you to collect it from the office. The Head of Workplace & Operations, Rui Almeida, and his team are your first point of contact for any questions about equipment.

You are expected to keep your Kranich-issued devices secure and in good condition. If a device is lost, stolen, or damaged, report it to it-help@kranich.example immediately so that we can help you and take the necessary security steps.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, requested an additional monitor and a noise-cancelling headset for his home office. The Workplace & Operations team arranged delivery within a few days, and Marek could continue supporting his clients without interruption.

## 5. Security at home

Working remotely means that company data and systems are accessed from outside our office networks. Protecting that data is everyone's responsibility, and the measures described here help keep Kranich and our clients safe.

When working from home, please follow these guidelines:

- Use the company-approved tools for all work-related communication and file storage. Do not use personal email accounts or personal cloud storage for company information.
- Lock your screen whenever you step away from your device, even for a short moment.
- Ensure that your home Wi-Fi network is protected with a strong password. Avoid working on public or unsecured networks.
- Use the password manager (1Password) to store and manage all work-related credentials. Never share passwords through chat or email.
- Be aware of your surroundings when taking calls or sharing your screen. Make sure that confidential information is not visible to others in your home.
- If you suspect a security incident, such as a phishing attempt or a lost device, report it to security@kranich.example without delay.

The Information Security team, led by Priya Nayar, regularly shares guidance and updates through the Grove intranet. Please familiarise yourself with the relevant security policies, including POL-004, which covers information security in more depth. If you have any doubt about the security of your home setup, contact security@kranich.example before proceeding.

Example: Sofia, a product manager working remotely from Spain, noticed an unusual email asking her to verify her account credentials. She did not click on any links. Instead, she forwarded the email to security@kranich.example and deleted it. The security team confirmed it was a phishing attempt and thanked her for reporting it promptly.

## 6. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, your first point of contact is your manager. They can help you understand how remote work applies to your role and team.

For questions about your employment contract, working time, or personal circumstances, please contact the People & Culture team at people@kranich.example. Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture, and her team are here to support you.

For questions about equipment, your home workspace, or office access, contact it-help@kranich.example or the Workplace & Operations team.

For any security-related questions or concerns, contact security@kranich.example.

We want remote work to be a positive experience for you and for Kranich. If something is not working, please speak up. We will do our best to find a solution together.
