# Internal Data Protection Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we handle personal data every day — of our employees, of our customers' employees, and of the drivers and dispatchers who use our products. Treating that data with care is not only a legal obligation; it is part of how we build trust with each other and with the logistics operators who rely on Kranich Route Cloud.

This policy explains the principles we follow when processing personal data internally. It applies to everyone at Kranich, whether you work from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from your home in any of the countries where we employ people.

We process personal data only where we have a lawful basis to do so, only for purposes we can explain, and only to the extent necessary. When in doubt, ask. The Legal team owns this policy and is happy to help you think through a specific situation.

The key principles are simple. Collect what you need, use it only for the purpose you collected it for, keep it accurate, protect it from unauthorised access, and delete it when you no longer need it. The sections below describe how these principles apply in practice.

## 2. Personal data we handle

Personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. In our day-to-day work, this includes several categories.

Employee data is held in Fern, our HRIS. This includes contact details, emergency contacts, bank account information for salary payments, leave records, and information needed for tax and social security reporting in the countries where we operate. It also includes performance-related notes kept by managers and the People & Culture team.

Customer contact data lives in our CRM and in the systems used by Customer Success and Go-to-Market. This includes names, business email addresses, phone numbers, and company details of the people we speak with at our customers and prospects.

Operational data is generated when our customers use Kranich Route Cloud. This can include the names and employee identifiers of dispatchers who log in, as well as location data and delivery schedules that may reveal where individual drivers are at a given time. Even if this data is provided by our customers, we process it on their behalf and must protect it accordingly.

Correspondence data includes the content of emails, chat messages, and meeting recordings that contain personal information about colleagues, customers, or other individuals.

We also process special categories of data in limited circumstances, for example health-related information that employees share with People & Culture in the context of sick leave. Access to such data is restricted to those who need it to fulfil legal or administrative duties.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, works on a feature that stores the home addresses of delivery drivers. She treats those addresses as personal data, ensures they are encrypted in transit and at rest, and does not copy them into local files or personal notes.

## 3. Retention

We keep personal data only as long as we need it for the purpose for which it was collected, or as long as the law requires us to keep it. Once the purpose has been fulfilled and no legal obligation to retain the data remains, we delete it or make it anonymous.

Employee data is deleted when an employment relationship ends, except where we must keep records for tax or social security purposes. Applicant data is deleted 8 years after the hiring decision, whether the application was successful or not.

Customer contact data is reviewed periodically. If a customer has not engaged with us for a while and there is no active project or contract, we remove their contact details from our CRM. Operational data that we process on behalf of customers is deleted when the underlying contract ends, unless the customer asks us to return it first.

Correspondence data, including emails and chat messages, is deleted according to the retention schedules of the tool in which it is stored. When you leave Kranich, your accounts are deactivated and your files are transferred or deleted in line with our offboarding process.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, keeps notes about her customer calls in the CRM. When a customer ends their subscription, she reviews her notes, deletes anything that is no longer needed, and flags the remaining records for deletion at the end of the retention period.

If you are unsure whether a piece of data can be deleted, keep it and ask Legal. It is better to retain data briefly longer than to delete something we are required to keep.

## 4. Requests from data subjects

Individuals whose personal data we process have rights. They may ask us to give them a copy of their data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict how we use it, or to transfer it to another organisation. They may also object to processing based on legitimate interests.

Data subject requests are answered within 20 calendar days. This deadline applies from the day we receive a complete request with enough information to identify the person. If we need more time because the request is complex or we have received many requests at once, we inform the requester before the deadline passes and explain why we need an extension.

If you receive a data subject request — by email, phone, or in person — do not respond substantively on your own. Forward it promptly to security@kranich.example and to the Legal team. We maintain a register of requests and coordinate the response centrally.

When we respond to a request, we first verify the identity of the requester. We do not hand out personal data to someone who cannot prove they are the person concerned. Where a request concerns data we process on behalf of a customer, we coordinate with that customer before responding, because the customer is the controller for that data.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, receives an email from a dispatcher at a client company asking for a copy of all personal data the client holds about her. Marek recognises this as a data subject request. He does not reply to the dispatcher directly. Instead, he forwards the email to the Legal team and security@kranich.example, and notes in the client account that a request has arrived.

We never charge a fee for responding to a data subject request unless the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive. If you believe a request is abusive, raise it with Legal before taking any action.

## 5. Working with processors

A processor is an external organisation that handles personal data on our behalf. We use processors for many parts of our business: cloud hosting, email and collaboration tools, customer support software, and payroll services, among others.

Before we engage a new processor, we conduct a review. We check that the processor provides sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures. We also ensure that a data processing agreement is in place. This agreement sets out the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data involved, and the obligations of the processor.

Our data processing agreements require processors to process personal data only on our documented instructions. Processors must ensure that persons authorised to process the data have committed themselves to confidentiality. They must assist us in responding to data subject requests and in ensuring the security of the processing. They must also delete or return all personal data at the end of the engagement.

When a processor engages a sub-processor, it must inform us in advance and give us the opportunity to object. Our internal register of processors is maintained by the Legal team and is available on request through Grove.

If you are considering a new tool or service that will involve personal data, do not sign up for it on your own. Contact Legal and the Information Security team first. They will help you assess the tool and, if appropriate, put the necessary agreements in place. Using an unapproved tool that processes personal data is a violation of this policy.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, wants to use a new survey tool to collect feedback from customer dispatchers. She checks Grove, sees that the tool is not in the approved list, and raises a request with Legal before collecting any responses.

## 6. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, about a specific data handling situation, or about a request you have received, please reach out. The Legal team owns this policy and can be contacted through security@kranich.example. For questions about your own employee data, contact people@kranich.example.

You can also speak to your manager or to the People & Culture team. They will help you find the right contact if they cannot answer your question themselves.

We review this policy regularly and update it when our practices or legal obligations change. The current version is always available on Grove under the policy library. Please familiarise yourself with this policy and follow it in your daily work. Protecting personal data is everyone's responsibility.
