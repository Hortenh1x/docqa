# Expense Rates & Per-Diems 2025

## 1. Purpose

This document sets out the per-diem rates that Kranich Software GmbH reimburses for business travel during the calendar year 2025. It applies to all employees in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom.

Per-diems cover meals and incidental expenses while traveling for work. They are paid in addition to directly reimbursable travel costs such as accommodation and transport, which are handled through Ledgerly. If a policy in the Grove library conflicts with this sheet, this sheet takes precedence for travel expenses incurred in 2025.

## 2. Per-diem rates by country

The following table shows the daily rate for each country where Kranich employees are based. The rate applies per full calendar day of business travel within that country.

| Country | Per-diem rate |
|---|---|
| Germany | €68 per day |
| Portugal | €41 per day |
| Spain | €42 per day |
| Poland | €18 per day |
| France | €33 per day |
| United Kingdom | €51 per day |

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, travels to a client in Hamburg for a full day. Her reimbursement for that day follows the German rate in the table above.

## 3. Partial travel days

When a business trip does not cover a full calendar day, the per-diem is adjusted based on the time spent traveling. Trips under eight hours pay 50% of the country rate. This applies whether the trip starts early in the morning or ends late in the evening.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, visits a client in Warsaw for a few hours and returns the same day. Because his trip is under eight hours, he receives 50% of the Polish rate.

If a trip spans two calendar days but totals less than eight hours of travel, the partial-day rule applies to the entire trip rather than to each day separately. For trips that cross midnight, each calendar day is assessed on its own.

## 4. Meals provided by others

When a meal is provided by a client, a partner or Kranich itself — for example, at a conference lunch or a working dinner — the per-diem for that day is reduced. The reduction is proportional to the number of meals covered. If all meals are provided, no per-diem is paid for that day.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Spain, attends a customer workshop where the client provides breakfast and lunch. She pays for her own dinner, so she claims a per-diem that reflects only the meal she covered.

Employees should use their judgment and record in Ledgerly whether meals were provided. Finance may ask for clarification if a claim appears inconsistent with the travel itinerary.

## 5. Questions

If you are unsure which rate applies or how to handle a partial day, ask your manager or the Finance team before submitting an expense report. For general expense questions, contact People & Culture through the usual channels. For security-related concerns about submitting receipts, refer to POL-004.

This sheet is valid for travel expenses incurred in 2025 only. Expense reports for travel in later years should follow the guidance that Finance publishes at that time.
