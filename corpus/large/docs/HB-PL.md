# Employee Handbook — Poland

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of our team in Poland. This handbook describes how we work together, what you can expect from us, and what we ask of you in return. It is written for employees who are employed in Poland, and it complements our global policies and the local agreements that apply to you.

Our company builds route-optimization software for logistics operators. We value clarity, care, and reliability — in our product and in how we treat one another. We are remote-first, which means you have flexibility in where you work, while staying connected to your team and our mission.

This handbook is owned by People & Culture. If anything here is unclear, or if your situation differs from what is described, please reach out to us at people@kranich.example. We update this handbook periodically, and the version in force is the one published on Grove, our intranet and policy library.

## 2. Your employment in Poland

Your employment relationship is governed by your individual employment contract, the Polish Labour Code, and the internal policies of Kranich Software GmbH. In case of any conflict, the applicable law and your contract take precedence.

When you join, you will receive an onboarding checklist through Fern, our HRIS. There you will find your personal data, your org chart position, and how to submit leave requests. Please make sure your details are current, especially your address and emergency contact information.

We are committed to a respectful and inclusive workplace. Our policies on conduct, confidentiality, and data protection apply to all employees regardless of location. If you have questions about which policy applies in a given situation, contact People & Culture or consult Grove.

Your employment contract specifies your role, your working time, and your place of work. Any changes to these terms require an agreement in writing. Your manager will discuss changes with you in advance.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in Poland receive 26 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This is your full annual leave entitlement, combining the statutory minimum with our additional company leave. It is available on a pro-rata basis for the calendar year in which you join or leave.

You request vacation through Fern. Please submit your request as early as possible so your team can plan. Your manager will approve or decline the request, taking into account business needs and fairness across the team.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, plans his vacation around client implementation milestones. He submits his requests a few weeks ahead, which allows his colleagues to cover urgent matters while he is away.

We encourage you to take your full entitlement each year. Vacation is meant for rest, and we expect you to disconnect during your time off. If you have unused days at the end of the year, please discuss them with your manager. The rules for carrying over or paying out unused days follow local law and are described in the relevant policy.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in Poland is 1 month. This applies to new hires and reflects the introductory phase of your employment. During this time, both you and Kranich get to know each other and assess whether the role is a good fit.

Your probation period is stated in your employment contract. It is a normal part of joining, and it does not affect your access to our tools, benefits, or support. You participate in team meetings, receive onboarding, and are expected to perform your duties just as you would after probation.

During the probation period, you and your manager will have regular check-ins. These conversations are an opportunity to give and receive feedback. If either side decides that the employment should not continue, the notice rules for probation apply as described in your contract and in §5 of this handbook.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined Kranich through a similar probation arrangement in her country. Her manager scheduled weekly one-on-ones, which helped her ramp up quickly and clarify expectations early.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in Poland is 3 weeks. This applies when you or Kranich terminates your employment after the probation period has ended, unless a different period is stated in your contract or required by law.

During the notice period, you remain employed and are expected to continue your duties and support a smooth handover. Your manager will work with you to transfer knowledge, document your tasks, and introduce your colleagues to ongoing projects.

If you resign, please submit your resignation in writing and confirm receipt. If Kranich terminates your employment, you will receive written notice with the reasons and the effective date. In both cases, People & Culture is available to answer questions about your final pay, unused vacation, and any other entitlements.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, decided to move to another company. She gave notice in line with her contract, and during her notice period she documented her key accounts and introduced her colleagues to the clients, so the transition was seamless.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in Poland observe 15 days of public holidays in the reference year. These are the national holidays recognized in Poland, and they are paid days off.

When a public holiday falls on a weekend, the rules for an additional day off follow local law. Your local team calendar in Grove shows the holidays for Poland, and your manager will confirm which days are observed in a given year.

If you are scheduled to work on a public holiday because of business needs, your manager will discuss this with you in advance and arrange a replacement day off in accordance with local rules. We aim to respect your time off and to plan ahead so that holiday coverage is fair and transparent.

Example: Marek coordinates with clients in Poland who often pause operations during public holidays. He aligns his project milestones around these days so that deployments do not fall on a holiday.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in Poland is 36.5 hours per week. This is your contractual working time, and it is distributed across the working days of the week in agreement with your manager.

We are a remote-first company, which means you have flexibility in how you structure your day. What matters is that you fulfill your responsibilities, collaborate with your team, and are reachable during the core hours agreed with your manager. Your working time is recorded in the way described in your contract and in our internal tools.

We do not expect you to be available around the clock. Please communicate your working hours to your team, set your status in our communication tools, and respect the working hours of your colleagues in different time zones.

Example: Sofia, a product manager working remotely from Spain, coordinates with her team across Europe. She agrees on a core window for meetings and does focused work outside that window, making sure her total working time stays within her contractual hours.

Breaks are part of your working day. You are entitled to a rest break as provided by law, and you should take it. If you work longer than your standard hours occasionally, discuss this with your manager. Systematic overtime is not expected, and any arrangements follow local law and company policy.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are unwell and cannot work, please inform your manager as soon as possible, preferably before your working day starts. You should also submit your sick leave in Fern, so that People & Culture and payroll have the information they need.

For short absences, you may not need a medical certificate. The rules for when a sick-note is required are in POL-007. Please follow that policy and your manager's guidance. If you are sick for longer, or if the absence recurs, your manager and People & Culture will stay in touch with you to support your recovery and plan your return.

During sick leave, you receive your statutory sick pay as provided by Polish law. Your contract and the relevant policies describe how sick leave interacts with your vacation and other entitlements.

Example: Anna woke up with a fever and could not work. She messaged her manager early in the morning, submitted her sick leave in Fern, and rested. A few days later, when she was still unwell, she followed POL-007 regarding the sick-note and kept her manager informed.

Your health comes first. Please do not work while you are sick, and do not feel pressured to check messages or join calls. We trust you to use sick leave responsibly and to return when you are well.

## 9. Remote work in Poland

Kranich is remote-first, and we support you in working from home or from another suitable location in Poland. Remote work is part of how we operate, and we have designed our tools and practices around it.

You should have a workspace that allows you to work effectively and securely. This includes a reliable internet connection and a quiet environment for calls. Our IT team provides the equipment you need, and you can reach them at it-help@kranich.example if you have technical issues.

When working remotely, please follow our information security policies. Use company-approved tools, keep your devices updated, and protect confidential data. If you travel or work from a location outside your usual place of work, please inform your manager in advance.

We also have offices in Berlin and Lisbon. If you are visiting one of these locations, you can book a desk or a meeting room through Perch. Please check the office guidelines on Grove before your visit.

Example: Deniz occasionally visits the Berlin office for team workshops. She books her desk through Perch a few days ahead, and she uses the time to meet colleagues in person and collaborate on cross-team topics.

Remote work does not change your employment terms. Your working hours, vacation, and other entitlements remain as described in this handbook and in your contract. If you plan to work from abroad for an extended period, please discuss this with People & Culture in advance, as tax and social security implications may arise.

## 10. Benefits in Poland

We offer a benefits package that supports your well-being and professional growth. The specific benefits available to you in Poland are described in the benefits section of Grove and in your onboarding materials.

Our benefits include private medical care and a group life insurance plan. You will receive details about enrollment and coverage when you join, and you can update your choices during the annual enrollment window or as described in the plan documents.

We also provide a Multisport or similar sports and recreation benefit, which gives you access to a network of fitness and leisure facilities. This is part of our commitment to your health and work-life balance.

Professional development is important to us. You can access learning resources through our internal platforms, and you may discuss training opportunities with your manager as part of your growth conversations.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, uses her sports benefit to attend yoga classes twice a week. She finds it helps her stay active and manage stress, and she appreciates that Kranich supports this.

If you have questions about your benefits, please contact People & Culture. We review our benefits regularly, and we communicate any changes through Grove and Fern.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly to your Polish bank account, on the last working day of the month, unless your contract states otherwise. Payroll is processed by our payroll provider, and you will receive a payslip each month through the provided system.

Taxes and social security contributions are withheld from your salary according to Polish law. Your payslip shows the breakdown of your gross salary, the deductions, and your net pay. If you have questions about a specific item, please contact People & Culture or our payroll contact.

You are responsible for providing accurate personal and tax information, and for notifying us of any changes, such as a new address or a change in your tax status. This helps us keep your payroll correct and compliant.

Example: Marek moved to a new apartment in Warsaw. He updated his address in Fern promptly, so that his payslip and any correspondence reached him without delay.

Reimbursement of business expenses is handled through Ledgerly, our expenses and travel tool. Please submit your expenses with receipts in a timely manner, following the expense policy on Grove. If you travel for work, you book through Ledgerly, and the travel policy describes what is covered.

Our fiscal year is the calendar year. At the end of the year, you will receive the tax documents you need for your annual tax return. People & Culture can point you to the relevant documents, but we do not provide individual tax advice. If you are unsure about your tax situation, please consult a tax advisor.
