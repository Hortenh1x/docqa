# Expense Rates & Per-Diems 2023

## 1. Purpose

This document sets out the per-diem rates for business travel in 2023. It applies to all Kranich Software employees when they travel for work in the countries listed below. The rates cover meals and incidental expenses for full travel days.

Per-diems are paid in addition to reimbursable travel costs such as accommodation and transport, which are handled separately through Ledgerly. When you submit an expense report, Ledgerly will prompt you for the country and the number of travel days, and apply the correct rate automatically. If you have questions about a specific trip, reach out to Finance.

## 2. Per-diem rates by country

The following rates apply for full travel days in each country.

| Country | Per-diem rate |
|---|---|
| Germany | €46 per day |
| Portugal | €23 per day |
| Spain | €20 per day |
| Poland | €55 per day |
| France | €53 per day |
| United Kingdom | €67 per day |

These rates are fixed for the calendar year 2023. They apply regardless of whether you travel for a client meeting, a team workshop, or a conference. If your trip takes you to more than one country, use the rate for the country where you spend the night. If you do not spend a night, use the rate for the country where you spend the most time during the day.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, travels from his home in Poland to a client site in Germany for a single day. Because he returns home the same evening and spends most of the day in Germany, the German rate applies.

## 3. Partial travel days

Not every trip fills a full day. For trips under eight hours, the per-diem is 50% of the country rate. This applies to the day of departure and the day of return separately, so a trip that starts early and ends late on the same day counts as one partial day, not two.

A travel day begins when you leave your home or the Kranich office and ends when you arrive at your destination for the night. If your trip includes an overnight stay, both the departure day and the return day are treated as travel days. Each of those days is assessed on its own: if the day is under eight hours of travel time, it qualifies for 50% of the rate; if it reaches eight hours or more, it qualifies for the full rate.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, travels to a client in Warsaw for two nights. On the departure day she travels for most of the day, so she claims the full rate. On the return day she travels for only a few hours, so she claims 50% of the Polish rate.

## 4. Meals provided by others

When a meal is provided as part of your trip — for example, by a client, at a conference, or during a team event — the per-diem for that day is reduced. The reduction applies per meal: if breakfast, lunch, or dinner is provided, you deduct the corresponding portion from your daily per-diem. If all meals are provided, you should not claim a per-diem for that day.

The purpose of this rule is to keep per-diems fair and to avoid double reimbursement. When you submit your expenses in Ledgerly, indicate which meals were provided so the system can apply the reduction. If you are unsure whether a meal counts as provided, use your judgment: a coffee or a light snack does not count, but a full meal does.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, attends a two-day partner event in Porto. The event includes lunch on both days. She claims the full Portuguese per-diem for the first day and the second day, but deducts the lunch portion for each day.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about per-diems or expense rates, please contact Finance through Ledgerly or write to the people team at people@kranich.example. For questions about a specific reimbursement, include the relevant expense report reference so we can help you promptly.
