# Employee Handbook — Portugal

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of our team in Portugal. This handbook brings together the essentials of working with us: your rights, our shared expectations, and the practical details of employment under Portuguese law and Kranich practice.

We are a remote-first company. Whether you work from the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon or from your home elsewhere in Portugal, you are part of a distributed team that spans several countries. What connects us is not a building but the way we work: with care, clarity, and respect for one another.

This handbook is written for employees in Portugal. It complements our internal policies, which you can find on Grove, our intranet and policy library. Where this handbook refers to a policy by its document id, please read the policy itself for full detail. If anything here conflicts with Portuguese law, the law applies.

Our leadership team is here to support you. CEO Marta Lindqvist leads the company; CTO Jonas Weber heads our engineering; CFO Tomasz Zieliński oversees finance; VP People & Culture Aylin Demir looks after the people side of Kranich; Head of Information Security Priya Nayar keeps our data safe; and Head of Workplace & Operations Rui Almeida makes sure our offices and tools work well for everyone.

People & Culture is your first stop for questions about employment, leave, or anything in this handbook. Write to people@kranich.example. For technical issues with your laptop or internal tools, write to it-help@kranich.example. For security concerns, contact security@kranich.example.

## 2. Your employment in Portugal

Your employment with Kranich Software GmbH is governed by your individual contract, Portuguese labour law, and the policies referenced in this handbook. Your contract states your role, your team, and your place of work. Most employees in Portugal are employed through our Lisbon entity, and your contract reflects that.

You belong to one of our teams: Routing Core, Fleet Insights, Platform & Infrastructure, Customer Success, or Go-to-Market. Your manager will confirm your team and your responsibilities when you join. Our org chart is available in Fern, our HRIS, alongside your personal data and leave requests.

We expect every employee to act with integrity, to treat colleagues with respect, and to protect Kranich data and systems. Our security policies, including POL-007, describe specific obligations. Please read them and follow them in your daily work.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, works closely with Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon. Their collaboration happens over video calls and written updates. Anna knows which policies apply to her because she read this handbook and the linked policies when she joined.

If your personal circumstances change — your address, your family situation, or your bank details — update your records in Fern promptly. Keeping your data current helps People & Culture and Finance serve you well.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in Portugal receive 22 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This is your annual leave entitlement, and we encourage you to use it. Rest is part of doing good work.

You request vacation through Fern. Your manager approves requests, and you can see your remaining balance in the same tool. We ask that you plan your leave with some advance notice, especially when your team has shared commitments or client-facing deadlines.

Vacation days are calculated on a calendar-year basis. When you join mid-year, your entitlement is prorated according to Portuguese law. When you leave Kranich, unused vacation is settled in your final payment.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, lives in Spain and collaborates with colleagues across Europe. She plans her vacation around the product release calendar, which her team publishes quarterly. She enters her requests in Fern at least a few weeks ahead so her manager can plan coverage.

You may carry over unused vacation into the next year only where Portuguese law allows it. If you have questions about your balance or carry-over, contact People & Culture.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in Portugal is 5 months. During this time, you and Kranich get to know each other and confirm that the role is a good fit.

Your probation period starts on your first working day. During probation, your manager will hold regular check-ins with you. These conversations cover your progress, your learning, and any support you need. Feedback flows both ways; we want to hear how you are experiencing Kranich too.

If either side decides that the employment is not working out, the contract may be terminated during probation with the notice required by Portuguese law. People & Culture can explain the process and answer your questions.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, joined Kranich during a busy season. His manager scheduled weekly calls with him throughout probation, and Marek received clear feedback after each client visit. By the end of the period, both sides were confident the role was right.

Probation is not a test you pass alone. Your manager, your team, and People & Culture are all invested in your success. Ask questions early, and use the check-ins to raise anything that feels unclear.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in Portugal is 8 weeks. This applies when you resign from Kranich, and it also applies when Kranich terminates your employment for reasons that require notice under Portuguese law.

When you decide to leave, please inform your manager and People & Culture in writing. Your notice period starts on the date specified in your contract and runs according to Portuguese rules. During your notice period, we ask you to continue your work as usual, to hand over your responsibilities, and to support your team through the transition.

If Kranich initiates a termination, People & Culture will explain the process, the reasons, and the next steps. We aim to handle every departure with dignity and fairness.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, decided to move to a different industry. She told her manager and People & Culture, and together they agreed on a handover plan for her accounts. Deniz used her notice period to document her work and introduce her colleagues to her clients.

During your notice period, you remain entitled to your normal salary and benefits. You also remain bound by your confidentiality obligations. If you have questions about notice, your contract, or your final settlement, contact People & Culture.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in Portugal observe 10 days of public holidays in the reference year. These are the national holidays recognised in Portugal, and you are free from work on those days.

The specific dates of public holidays vary from year to year because some fall on fixed dates and others are movable. Your calendar in Fern and the tools you use for scheduling will reflect these days. If a public holiday falls on a weekend, Portuguese law determines whether you receive a compensatory day off. People & Culture will communicate any such arrangement in good time.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, checks the holiday calendar at the start of each year. She knows that on public holidays her office is closed and her colleagues across Europe may still be working. She plans her meetings accordingly, so she does not schedule recurring calls on those days.

Public holidays are separate from your vacation entitlement. They do not reduce the 22 days described in §3 of this handbook.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in Portugal is 35.5 hours per week. This is your full-time schedule, and your contract reflects it.

We are a remote-first company, and we trust you to manage your time. What matters is that you deliver on your responsibilities, collaborate with your team, and are reachable during the core hours your team agrees on. Your manager will clarify expectations for your role.

We do not expect you to work evenings or weekends as a rule. If your role occasionally requires extra effort, for example during a product release or a critical client incident, we expect that to be the exception, not the norm. Talk to your manager if you feel the workload is consistently too high.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, starts her day in the morning and finishes in the late afternoon. Her team in Routing Core overlaps with colleagues in Berlin for a few hours each day. Anna schedules her deep work for the morning and her meetings for the overlap window.

You are responsible for tracking your own time in the way your manager and Finance require. If you have questions about your schedule, flexible arrangements, or time tracking, ask your manager or People & Culture.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are unwell and cannot work, please inform your manager as soon as you can. Do not wait until you are well to write; a short message in the morning is enough for your manager to plan around your absence.

For absences due to illness, you must follow the sick-note rules in POL-007. That policy explains when you need a medical certificate and how to submit it. Please read it so you know your obligations before you are in the situation.

While you are on sick leave, your focus is on recovering. You do not need to check work messages or attend meetings. Your team will cover your responsibilities in your absence.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, woke up with a fever on a Tuesday. She wrote to her manager before her first meeting and stayed in bed. On the third day of her absence, she submitted the medical certificate required by POL-007. Her manager had already rebooked her client calls with a colleague.

If your illness lasts longer than expected, stay in touch with your manager and People & Culture. We will support you and make sure you have what you need to recover.

## 9. Remote work in Portugal

Kranich is remote-first, and that includes our employees in Portugal. You can work from home, from the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or from another location in Portugal, as long as it suits your role and your team.

When you work from home, we expect you to have a suitable workspace and a reliable internet connection. Kranich provides the equipment you need for your role, and our Platform & Infrastructure team supports you with setup and troubleshooting.

If you prefer to work from the Lisbon office, you can. The office has desks and meeting rooms, which you can book through Perch, our internal tool for room and desk reservations. The office is a place for focused work, team gatherings, and the occasional in-person meeting with colleagues.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, visits clients in person. His colleagues in Portugal, like Anna, rarely travel for work. Anna works from home most days and goes to the Rua do Alecrim office once or twice a week to meet her Lisbon-based colleagues and to have a change of scenery.

If you plan to work from a location outside Portugal for an extended period, please discuss this with your manager and People & Culture in advance. Tax and social security implications may apply, and we need to handle them properly.

## 10. Benefits in Portugal

Kranich offers a benefits package for employees in Portugal that goes beyond the statutory minimum. The details are described in your contract and in the relevant policies on Grove.

Your benefits include the vacation entitlement described in §3, sick leave as described in §8 and POL-007, and the other leave types required by Portuguese law. You also have access to our internal tools: Fern for leave and personal data, Ledgerly for expenses and travel booking, and Perch for office reservations.

We provide a meal allowance as part of your monthly compensation, in line with common practice in Portugal. The specific amount is stated in your contract and is updated according to the applicable legal framework.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Spain, uses Ledgerly to book her flights when she travels to Berlin for a team gathering. Her colleague Anna, in Lisbon, uses the same tool when she books a train to meet a client. Both receive their meal allowance with their monthly salary, without needing to submit receipts for everyday meals.

For a full overview of your benefits, please speak with People & Culture. We review our benefits regularly and will communicate any changes through the appropriate channels.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly, in euros, to the bank account you have registered in Fern. Payment is made on the last working day of the month, or on the preceding working day if the last day falls on a weekend or public holiday.

Kranich withholds income tax and social security contributions from your salary and remits them to the Portuguese authorities on your behalf. Your payslip is available in Fern and shows the breakdown of your gross salary, deductions, and net payment.

If your personal or financial situation changes — for example, your marital status, the number of dependants, or your bank account — update your data in Fern and, where necessary, inform People & Culture. Tax withholding is based on the information you provide, and it is your responsibility to keep that information accurate.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, checks her payslip in Fern at the end of each month. She notices that her withholding rate matches the information she provided when she joined. When she moved to a new apartment, she updated her address in Fern within a few days.

For questions about your payslip, tax withholding, or social security, contact People & Culture or Finance. They can explain the details and help you resolve any discrepancies. We cannot provide individual tax advice; for that, you may wish to consult a tax professional.
