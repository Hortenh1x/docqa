# Training & Development Policy

*Superseded by version 2.0.*

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we believe that growing our people grows our product. This policy describes how we support your professional development through a learning budget, conference attendance, and internal learning opportunities. It applies to all employees, regardless of their role, location, or whether they work from the office or remotely.

We encourage you to take ownership of your development. Your manager is your first conversation partner for identifying skills you want to build and areas where you want to deepen your expertise. Together, you can decide which learning activities make the most sense for your current role and for your longer-term aspirations within the company.

This policy is meant to be simple and practical. We trust you to use the resources described here in good faith, and we ask that you keep your learning goals aligned with the needs of your team and the company.

## 2. Learning budget

Every employee has a learning budget of €500 per year. This budget is available for activities that help you grow in your role or prepare you for future responsibilities at Kranich. Examples include online courses, books, professional memberships, certification exams, and subscriptions to learning platforms.

The budget resets at the beginning of each calendar year. Unused funds do not carry over to the next year, so we encourage you to plan your learning activities across the year rather than leaving everything to the final months.

You do not need pre-approval for an expense that fits within the budget. Use the standard expense process in Ledgerly, attach your receipt, and select the training category. If you are unsure whether a particular expense qualifies, reach out to People & Culture before you purchase.

For expenses that exceed the budget, speak with your manager first. In some cases, your team or department may have additional funds available. Approval for such requests is at the discretion of your manager and the relevant department head.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to take an advanced course on distributed systems. The course costs less than her annual budget, so she books it directly and submits the receipt through Ledgerly the same day.

We expect that learning activities funded by the budget take place during your working hours when they are part of your role. If you prefer to study in the evening or on a weekend, that is your choice, but you are not required to do so.

## 3. Conferences

Conferences are a valuable way to learn about industry trends, meet peers, and bring fresh ideas back to Kranich. We support attendance at conferences when there is a clear benefit to your role and to the company.

If you would like to attend a conference, discuss it with your manager as early as possible. Conference costs, including the ticket, travel, and accommodation, are covered separately from your personal learning budget. However, attendance requires approval in advance, and we ask you to consider the overall cost to the company before submitting a request.

When you attend a conference on behalf of Kranich, you represent the company. We expect you to behave professionally and to follow our internal policies, including those on confidentiality and respectful communication.

After you return, we encourage you to share key takeaways with your team. This can be a short written summary on Grove, a presentation at a team meeting, or an informal conversation. Sharing helps the whole company benefit from your experience.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, attends a logistics industry conference each year. She discusses the agenda with her manager beforehand, submits her travel request through Ledgerly, and afterwards posts a brief summary on Grove with links to the talks she found most relevant.

If you speak at a conference, please let your manager and the communications team know. Speaking engagements can be a great way to build your personal brand and raise Kranich's profile. We are generally supportive of such opportunities, provided they do not conflict with your work commitments.

## 4. Internal learning

Learning does not only happen through external courses and events. Much of our best knowledge lives inside Kranich, and we want to make it easy for you to learn from your colleagues.

The Routing Core, Fleet Insights, and Platform & Infrastructure teams regularly hold technical talks and demos. These sessions are open to all employees, and recordings are made available on Grove for those who cannot attend live. If you have a topic you would like to present, reach out to your engineering manager or to the Platform & Infrastructure team.

Customer Success and Go-to-Market run regular sessions on customer insights and industry topics. These are particularly useful if you want to understand how our product is used in the field or if you are considering a move toward a customer-facing role.

We also encourage cross-team mentoring. If you want to learn a new skill that exists in another team, talk to your manager and to the relevant team lead. Many colleagues are happy to share their expertise through a short pairing session or a structured series of conversations.

The People & Culture team coordinates an internal learning calendar at the beginning of each year. If there is a topic you would like to see covered, you can suggest it through the Grove suggestion box or directly to your People Partner.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, wants to learn more about the Fleet Insights data model. He arranges a few short sessions with Sofia, the product manager for Fleet Insights, who walks him through the key concepts and points him to the relevant documentation.

Internal learning activities do not draw on your personal learning budget. They are part of your normal working time, and you do not need to submit any expenses.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, the first point of contact is your manager or your People Partner. They can help you think through what learning activities make sense for your situation.

For administrative questions about the learning budget, expenses, or the approval process, contact people@kranich.example. The People & Culture team will respond promptly.

If you believe an expense should qualify under this policy but was declined, you can appeal by writing to people@kranich.example with a brief explanation. Appeals are reviewed by the VP People & Culture.

We want this policy to be easy to use. If something is unclear, or if you have ideas for how we could better support learning at Kranich, please share them. We review our policies regularly and value your input.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, receives a question from a colleague about whether a professional association membership counts as a learning expense. She confirms that it does, as long as it fits within the annual budget, and points the colleague to the relevant section of this policy.
