# Travel & Expense Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how to book business travel and how to get reimbursed for expenses you incur while working for Kranich. We want travel to be straightforward: you focus on the work, and we make sure the practical side is clear and fair.

This version supersedes version 2.0. It reflects how we work today, including our remote-first setup across Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom. Whether you are based in Berlin, Lisbon or working from home, the same principles apply.

We trust you to spend company money as if it were your own. That means choosing reasonable options, not the cheapest possible option at the expense of your wellbeing, and not the most lavish option either. When in doubt, ask your manager or the Finance team before you book.

## 2. When to travel

Travel is part of many roles at Kranich, but it is not the default. Before you plan a trip, ask yourself whether the goal can be achieved just as well through a video call, a shared document or a workshop in our internal tools. If the answer is yes, skip the trip.

You should travel when being physically present genuinely matters. Examples include client workshops, on-site implementation work with a customer, team offsites, conferences where you represent Kranich, and visits to our offices in Berlin or Lisbon that require in-person collaboration.

Your manager approves the business purpose of a trip before you book anything. For client travel, the Customer Success or Go-to-Market lead coordinates with the client to confirm dates and agenda. For internal travel, your manager confirms the reason and the budget line.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, plans to visit a client in Hamburg. She confirms the agenda with the client first, then discusses the trip with her manager. The visit has a clear purpose: reviewing the rollout of a new routing feature. The trip is approved.

## 3. Booking

Once your manager approves the business purpose, you book through our travel tools in Ledgerly. Ledgerly handles flights, trains and hotels. If you need something Ledgerly cannot book, contact it-help@kranich.example and we will help you arrange it.

Book early. Trips should be booked at least 7 days before departure. Booking early gives you better options and lower prices, and it gives the team time to adjust plans if something changes. If you need to book with shorter notice, add a brief note explaining why.

Choose the most sensible mode of transport for the journey. Trains are preferred over flights for routes under 5 hours door to door. Door to door means from your home or office to your destination, including transfers. For longer routes, compare train and flight options and pick what is reasonable considering cost, total time and your comfort.

When booking a flight, choose economy class. When booking a train, choose standard class unless your manager approves a higher class for a specific reason, such as a medical need. Hotels should be mid-range and located reasonably close to where you are working. You do not need to stay at the cheapest place in the city if it adds an hour of commuting each day.

Accommodation and transport must be booked in your own name. Do not book for family members or friends on the company account, even if they accompany you. If you extend a business trip for personal reasons, the company pays only for the business portion. You are responsible for the extra nights, the changed return fare and any personal costs.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, needs to visit a client near Warsaw. The door-to-door journey by train is under 5 hours, so he books a standard-class train ticket through Ledgerly. The trip is two days long, so he books a mid-range hotel near the client's office. He books everything a week and a half before departure.

## 4. Per-diems

Instead of collecting receipts for every small meal, we pay a per-diem to cover meals and incidental expenses while you are traveling on business. The per-diem applies to full days of business travel and to partial days according to the rules in the current Expense Rates sheet (FIN-RATES-2026).

The per-diem is meant to cover your costs, not to be a bonus. You do not need to submit receipts for meals covered by the per-diem. If a meal is already covered by an event, a conference ticket or a client, you should not claim the per-diem for that meal.

Per-diem rates may differ by country and by whether the trip is domestic or international. Always check the current Expense Rates sheet (FIN-RATES-2026) before you travel so you know what to expect. The Finance team updates this sheet when rates change.

Example: Sofia, a product manager working remotely from Spain, travels to Berlin for a product workshop. The trip lasts three full days. She checks the current Expense Rates sheet (FIN-RATES-2026) to understand her per-diem for Germany, and she claims the per-diem for the three days in her expense report. Her breakfasts and dinners are covered by the per-diem, so she does not keep those receipts.

## 5. What is reimbursable

We reimburse reasonable and necessary expenses that you incur for business purposes while traveling. These include:

- Transportation to and from the airport, train station or hotel, such as taxis or public transport
- Baggage fees for one checked bag when needed for business
- Work-related communication costs, such as roaming charges for business calls
- Laundry for trips that last several days
- Visas and entry permits required for the destination

We do not reimburse personal expenses. Examples include minibar charges, movies, personal shopping, entertainment for friends, or costs for family members who travel with you. Alcohol is not reimbursable, even with a business meal.

If you are unsure whether an expense is reimbursable, ask Finance before you spend the money. A quick question is easier than a rejected report.

Expenses for clients or external partners, such as a client lunch, follow a separate approval process through your manager. These are not part of a standard travel expense report.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, travels to Berlin for a team offsite. She takes a taxi from her apartment to the airport in Lisbon, and another taxi from the airport to her hotel in Berlin. She keeps both taxi receipts. During the offsite, she buys a small gift for a friend in Berlin; that gift is a personal expense, so she does not include it in her report.

## 6. Submitting expenses

Submit your expense report through Ledgerly. Attach a receipt for every expense above the threshold described in the current Expense Rates sheet (FIN-RATES-2026). For expenses below that threshold, a receipt is not required, but you must still list the expense and its purpose.

Expense reports are due within 30 calendar days after the trip ends. If you submit later, we may not be able to reimburse you, and we will ask you to explain the delay. Submitting on time keeps our accounting accurate and helps Finance close the books each month.

For each expense, add a short description that explains the business purpose. Write something like "Taxi from Lisbon airport to hotel for Routing Core offsite" rather than just "Taxi". If an expense is unusual or the purpose is not obvious, add a sentence of context.

Your manager approves the report first, and Finance reviews it after. If Finance has questions, they will reach out to you through Ledgerly. Once approved, reimbursement is paid through payroll on the next scheduled run.

Keep your receipts for your own records even after you submit. Ledgerly stores the attachments, but it is good practice to keep a copy until the reimbursement lands in your account.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, returns from a two-day recruiting event in Lisbon. Within a week of returning, she submits her report in Ledgerly with receipts for her taxi rides and a note explaining the purpose of each. Her manager approves the report, and Finance processes it in the next payroll run.

## 7. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start by checking the current Expense Rates sheet (FIN-RATES-2026) for rates and thresholds. For questions about a specific trip or expense, contact Finance through Ledgerly.

For questions about booking tools or technical issues with Ledgerly, contact it-help@kranich.example. For questions about how this policy interacts with your employment terms, contact people@kranich.example.

If you notice that this policy is unclear or that a situation is not covered, tell us. Policies work best when they reflect how we actually work, and your feedback helps us keep them useful.

Example: Deniz is planning a trip to a client in Poland and is unsure whether a train or a flight is more appropriate for the route. She checks the door-to-door travel time and finds it is just over the threshold where trains are preferred. She sends a question to Finance through Ledgerly with the route details, and Finance helps her choose the most reasonable option.
