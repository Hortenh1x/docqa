# Training & Development Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we believe that helping you grow in your role also helps our products and our customers grow. This policy describes the support we offer for your professional development, whether you want to deepen your technical expertise, learn more about the logistics industry, or build leadership skills.

This version of the policy supersedes version 1.5. We have updated it to make the rules clearer and to reflect how we work today, including our remote-first setup across Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom.

This policy applies to all employees, regardless of your team, your location, or whether you work from home or from one of our offices in Berlin or Lisbon. Your manager is your first point of contact for any questions about your development goals. For questions about the budget or the approval process, please reach out to People & Culture via people@kranich.example.

We encourage you to think of learning as part of your job, not something you do on top of it. The sections below explain the budget available to you, how conferences fit in, and what internal learning opportunities exist.

## 2. Learning budget

Every employee has a learning budget of €1,100 per year. This budget is yours to use for activities that help you develop professionally. It resets at the start of each calendar year, which is also our fiscal year, and it does not carry over.

You can use the budget for a wide range of learning opportunities. Common examples include online courses, books, certifications, and subscriptions to learning platforms. You can also use it for coaching or for materials that support a conference talk you are giving. If you are unsure whether an expense qualifies, please ask your manager or People & Culture before you spend.

The budget is intended for individual learning. If your team wants to attend a training together or buy a shared resource, please discuss this with your manager; team-level expenses are handled separately.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to take an advanced course on distributed systems. She checks with her manager that the course aligns with her goals for the year, then books it. She submits the receipt through Ledgerly, and the expense is approved against her learning budget.

To use your budget, follow this simple process:

- Discuss your learning goals with your manager during your regular check-ins.
- Choose a course, book, or other resource that fits those goals.
- Purchase it, or ask People & Culture to invoice you if the provider requires a company purchase order.
- Submit the expense through Ledgerly within a few days of the purchase.

If the cost of a single learning activity exceeds your annual budget, you may still pursue it, but you will need to cover the difference yourself. Your manager can help you decide whether the investment makes sense for your role.

You are welcome to use your learning budget from your first day at Kranich Software. There is no waiting period.

## 3. Conferences

Conferences are a valuable way to learn about new trends, meet peers from other companies, and represent Kranich Software to the wider community. We support your attendance at conferences that are relevant to your work.

Up to 5 working days per year may be spent at conferences without using vacation. This time includes the days of the conference itself plus reasonable travel time. If you want to attend more than one conference in a year, the total days you take must stay within this limit. If you need more time, you can combine conference attendance with vacation days, as long as you follow the normal vacation request process in Fern.

Conference attendance is funded from your learning budget. This means the registration fee, travel, accommodation, and meals are paid from your €1,100 per year, unless your manager agrees that the conference is so important to your team that the cost should be shared differently. Please clarify the funding arrangement before you book anything.

You do not need to be a speaker to attend a conference. However, if you are selected to speak, we are especially supportive. Speaking at a conference is a great way to share what you have learned and to raise the profile of Kranich Software. If you are speaking, please let your manager know as soon as you are accepted, so we can plan for your absence.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, wants to attend a conference about customer onboarding in the logistics software space. She checks the conference dates against her team calendar, confirms with her manager that the topic is relevant, and submits the registration cost through Ledgerly. She spends three days at the conference, which counts against her 5 working days for the year.

When you attend a conference, we ask you to share your key takeaways with your team when you return. This can be a short update in a team meeting or a post on Grove, our intranet. Sharing what you learned helps others benefit from your experience.

If a conference is cancelled or you cannot attend for a reason outside your control, please contact the conference organizers about refunds before you contact us. If a refund is not possible, we will discuss with you how to handle the cost.

## 4. Internal learning

Beyond the individual budget, we invest in internal learning opportunities that are available to everyone. These are funded by Kranich Software and do not count against your personal budget.

We run regular internal talks and workshops, often during working hours. Topics range from deep dives into our routing algorithms to sessions on communication, feedback, and working effectively in a remote-first company. Some sessions are recorded, so you can watch them later if you cannot attend live. Recordings are available on Grove.

We also encourage informal learning within and across teams. For example, a team might run a lunch-and-learn about a new technology they have adopted, or two colleagues might pair on a problem to share knowledge. If you would like to organize a session, please speak to your manager or to People & Culture. We are happy to help you find a slot and an audience.

The Platform & Infrastructure team maintains a library of technical documentation and recorded talks from past internal events. The Customer Success team shares lessons learned from client engagements, which can be valuable for engineers who want to understand how our products are used in the field.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, learns from a Customer Success talk that a particular client workflow is causing confusion. He shares this insight with the Routing Core team, and together they organize a workshop to explore how the product could better support that workflow. The workshop is open to anyone who wants to join.

Mentoring is another form of internal learning. If you would like a mentor, or if you would like to mentor someone else, please reach out to People & Culture. We maintain a list of colleagues who have volunteered to mentor, and we can help match you based on your goals and interests.

Finally, we encourage you to learn from your daily work. Taking time to reflect on what went well and what could be improved is part of our culture. Your manager can help you identify learning opportunities within your current projects.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, please start with your manager. They can help you think about what learning makes sense for your role and your goals.

For questions about the budget, eligible expenses, or the approval process, please contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. For questions about submitting expenses through Ledgerly, please contact it-help@kranich.example.

If you believe an exception to this policy is warranted, please discuss it with your manager and People & Culture. We review exceptions on a case-by-case basis, and we will let you know our decision promptly.

We want learning to be a natural part of your experience at Kranich Software. Please use this policy as a guide, and do not hesitate to ask if anything is unclear.
