# Access Control & Identity Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software builds and operates the Kranich Route Cloud for our customers. Access to that platform, and to the internal systems that support it, must be controlled carefully. This policy explains how we manage identities and access rights across the company, so that every person can do their job without exposing data or systems to unnecessary risk.

We want access to feel effortless when you need it and tightly controlled when you do not. This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and anyone else who holds an account on Kranich systems, regardless of whether they work from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from home.

## 2. Least privilege

Every account is granted the minimum access needed to perform the person's role. We call this the principle of least privilege. You should be able to reach the systems and data your work requires, and nothing more. This keeps the impact of a compromised account small and makes it easier to understand who can do what.

Access is tied to your role within a team, not to you as an individual. When your responsibilities change, your access changes with them. For example, Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, has access to customer account data but not to the source code of the Routing Core algorithms. Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, has broader read access to configuration data but does not hold administrative rights on the platform.

Administrative privileges are treated with particular care. Accounts with administrative rights on production systems, on identity providers, or on our password manager are limited to a small set of people whose job requires them. Administrative actions are performed with a separate account where technically possible, so that everyday browsing and email stay separate from privileged operations.

You may not share your account credentials with anyone, including colleagues. If you need someone else to perform a task on your behalf, request the appropriate access through the normal process. When you use 1Password to store secrets, keep them in the shared vaults that are relevant to your team and do not copy them into personal vaults or unsecured notes.

## 3. Access reviews

Access rights are reviewed every 4 months. The review is led by Information Security and involves each team's manager. The goal is to confirm that every active account still matches the person's current role and that no access has drifted beyond what least privilege allows.

During a review, managers go through the list of accounts and permissions for their team. They confirm who still needs what and flag anything that looks unusual. Information Security then acts on those flags, which may mean revoking access, changing a role, or asking the manager for more context.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, changed teams last year. Her access review caught that she still held a permission from her previous team on the internal analytics dashboard. The manager revoked it during the review cycle, and Sofia received a short note explaining the change.

Access reviews also cover service accounts and integrations. These are checked against the same least-privilege standard. Any service account that is no longer used is deactivated promptly. If you are a manager, you are responsible for responding to review requests within the window given in the review invitation. If you do not respond, Information Security will follow up with you directly.

## 4. Joiners, movers, leavers

Access: Managers only

When a new person joins Kranich, their manager requests access through the standard onboarding process. The request goes to the teams that own the relevant systems, and each system owner grants only the access listed in the request. New joiners receive their accounts for the core tools — email, Grove, Fern, Ledgerly, and 1Password — on their first day. Access to additional systems, such as the Kranich Route Cloud production environment, is granted as the person's role requires.

Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined the Routing Core team through this process. Her manager requested access to the code repositories, the development environment, and the team's collaboration channels. She did not receive access to customer billing data, because her role does not require it.

When a person changes roles within the company, their manager follows the mover process. The old manager confirms which access should be removed, and the new manager requests what the new role requires. Until the change is complete, the person keeps the access they had before so that their work is not interrupted. The access review in §3 catches any permissions that should have been removed but were overlooked.

Leavers follow a clear process. When a person leaves Kranich, their manager notifies People & Culture, who coordinate the offboarding. Standard departures have their access revoked on the person's last working day. For a departure for cause, all access is revoked within 1 hour of the manager's notification. This applies to every account the person holds, including email, the HRIS Fern, the expense tool Ledgerly, 1Password, and any production systems.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, had to let a contractor go for cause. She notified People & Culture and Information Security immediately. Within 1 hour, the contractor's accounts were deactivated across all systems. Ines then confirmed with her team that no shared credentials had been used by the contractor in a way that would require a broader reset.

When a person leaves, their manager is also responsible for ensuring that company equipment is returned. The process for returning hardware is governed by POL-006 §5. Information Security coordinates the revocation of access to customer environments, and the Customer Success team is informed when the leaver had direct contact with customers, so that the customer relationship is handled smoothly.

For all leavers, the manager should also consider whether the person had access to shared accounts or vaults that need to be rotated. When in doubt, rotate the credential. It is better to change a password than to wonder later whether a former employee still knows it.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, or about your own access, start by speaking with your manager. For technical questions about accounts, permissions, or the access review process, contact it-help@kranich.example. For questions about how this policy applies to a specific person or situation, contact security@kranich.example.

If you believe you have access you should not have, or that someone else has access they should not have, report it. You can raise it with your manager, with Information Security, or anonymously through the channel described on Grove. We would rather review a false alarm than miss a real issue.

This policy is owned by Information Security and is reviewed regularly. The version published on Grove is always the current one. If you are reading a printed copy, check the effective date in the front matter to make sure you have the latest version.
