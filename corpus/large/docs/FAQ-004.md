# Passwords FAQ

## 1. Questions

**How long must my password be?**
Passwords must be at least 10 characters long.

**How often do I need to change my password?**
You do not need to change your password on a fixed schedule. Instead, change it promptly if you suspect it has been compromised or if you are asked to do so by the Information Security team.

**Can I reuse a password I have used before?**
No. When you reset your password, you cannot choose one that you have used previously. Choose something new that you have not used for any Kranich account before.

**What should I use as a password?**
A passphrase works well — for example, a short sentence or a combination of words that is easy for you to remember but hard for others to guess. Avoid using your name, your team name, the company name, or anything that appears in your public profile.

**Where should I store my passwords?**
Store all work-related passwords in 1Password, our company password manager. Do not keep passwords in plain text files, sticky notes, or shared documents. If you need to share a credential with a colleague, use 1Password's secure sharing feature rather than email or chat.

**What if I forget my password?**
Use the self-service reset option on the login page. If you are locked out, contact it-help@kranich.example for assistance. You will need to verify your identity before receiving help.

**What about multi-factor authentication?**
Multi-factor authentication is required for all accounts that support it. If you receive a prompt to enroll, please do so promptly. If you lose access to your authenticator app or hardware key, contact it-help@kranich.example.

**What do I do if I think my password has been leaked?**
Change your password immediately, then report the incident to security@kranich.example. The Information Security team will guide you through the next steps. See §4 of POL-004 for the full incident reporting procedure.

**Are there different rules for service accounts or shared mailboxes?**
Yes. Service accounts and shared mailboxes follow the same minimum length requirement, but their passwords are managed by the Platform & Infrastructure team. If you need access to such an account, request it through your manager rather than asking a colleague to share the password directly.

**Example:** Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, once received a phishing email that looked like a login alert. She did not click the link. Instead, she reported it to security@kranich.example and changed her password as a precaution. That is exactly the right response.
