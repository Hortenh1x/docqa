# Access Control & Identity Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software builds and operates the Kranich Route Cloud for our customers. Protecting that service starts with controlling who can access what, both inside our offices and across our remote-first environment. This policy explains how we grant, review, and revoke access to Kranich systems, data, and tools.

We want every employee to do their best work without unnecessary barriers. At the same time, we hold ourselves to a high standard of security because our customers trust us with their operational data. This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and anyone else who receives access to Kranich resources, whether they work from Berlin, Lisbon, or from home in any of the countries where we employ people.

Access control is a shared responsibility. Information Security owns this policy and the technical controls behind it. Managers own decisions about what their team members need. Every individual owns the security of their own credentials and reports suspicious activity promptly.

## 2. Least privilege

We follow the principle of least privilege. Every person receives the minimum access needed to perform their role, nothing more. Access is granted based on job function, not on seniority, tenure, or curiosity.

When you join Kranich, your manager requests access for you. The request goes through our internal tools, and the relevant system owners approve it. For example, a backend engineer on Routing Core needs access to the code repositories and development environments for their service. They do not need access to the customer relationship system used by the Go-to-Market team. A customer success manager who works with a specific set of logistics operators needs to see those customers' data in the product, but not the source code behind it.

Access is granted per system and per role. We do not hand out broad "everything" access to make administration simpler. Where a system supports fine-grained permissions, we use them. Where it does not, we restrict access to the smallest group that can do the job.

Temporary needs get temporary solutions. If you need elevated access for a specific task, such as debugging a production incident, request it for that task only. The elevation expires automatically after the task is complete. You do not keep elevated rights because you once used them.

Credentials follow the same rule. You use your Kranich identity to sign in to company systems. You do not share accounts, passwords, or tokens with anyone, including colleagues who say they need help. If a tool does not support individual accounts, inform your manager and Information Security so we can find a safer alternative.

## 3. Access reviews

Access is not a one-time decision. People change roles, teams reorganize, and projects end. To keep access aligned with reality, we review access rights regularly.

Information Security coordinates an access review of all Kranich systems every 8 months. The review covers production systems, internal tools, code repositories, and administrative accounts. Each system owner receives a list of who holds access to their system. They confirm whether every person on that list still needs the access, and they flag anyone who should lose it.

Managers participate in the review for their direct reports. They confirm that the access their team members hold still matches what those people need for their current work. If someone changed teams within the last review period, the manager checks that old access was removed when the person moved.

The review is not a formality. When a system owner flags an access right as unneeded, we remove it promptly after the review closes. If a system owner does not respond to the review request, Information Security follows up. If there is still no response, we revoke the access rather than leave it in place.

Everyone plays a part between reviews, too. If you notice that you have access you no longer need, tell your manager. If you notice that a colleague has access that seems wrong for their role, tell Information Security. It is always better to ask than to stay silent.

## 4. Joiners, movers, leavers

Access: Managers only

Access decisions belong to managers. Your manager decides what you need, and your manager is responsible for requesting changes when your needs change. Information Security and the Platform & Infrastructure team implement the technical steps, but they do not decide what access a role should have.

When you join Kranich, your manager submits access requests as part of the onboarding process. People Operations coordinates with your manager to make sure you have what you need on your first day. You receive your Kranich account, access to the tools listed in your request, and a seat in the relevant team channels. You set up multi-factor authentication on your personal device before you can sign in.

When you move to a new role within Kranich, your new manager reviews what you currently have and what you need going forward. The old manager participates as well. Access that belonged to your previous role is removed as part of the move, not left in place "just in case." This applies to team changes, location changes, and changes in responsibility, such as when an engineer becomes an engineering manager.

When you leave Kranich, whether voluntarily or not, your manager starts the offboarding process. For a voluntary departure, your access remains active until your last working day so you can finish your work. On that day, your access is revoked. For a departure for cause, all access is revoked within 1 hour of the manager's notification. There is no grace period and no exception for unfinished tasks.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, has a team member who is leaving under difficult circumstances. Ines notifies People Operations and Information Security as soon as the decision is made. The departing person's access to all Kranich systems is cut off within the hour, before the person is informed of the decision. Ines then works with People Operations to collect any company property and to plan the handover of the person's work to the rest of the team.

When access is revoked, we also recover company property such as laptops and security keys. Your manager coordinates the return of hardware with Workplace & Operations. If you work remotely, Rui Almeida's team arranges a shipping label and a pickup. Until the hardware is returned, your access stays revoked regardless.

Leavers are reminded of their ongoing confidentiality obligations. They may no longer sign in to Kranich systems, but their duty to protect customer data and company information continues after their last day.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager. They can help you understand what access you need and how to request it.

For questions about the technical side of access, such as how to reset a password or set up multi-factor authentication on a new device, contact it-help@kranich.example.

For questions about whether a specific access right is appropriate, or to report a concern about someone's access, contact security@kranich.example. You can report anonymously if you prefer. Information Security reviews every report.

For questions about how access decisions relate to your employment, such as what happens when you go on parental leave, contact people@kranich.example. People Operations works with your manager to make sure your access matches your situation while you are away and when you return.

This policy does not cover every system or every edge case. When you are unsure, ask. A quick question is always cheaper than a security incident.
