# Speak-Up & Anti-Harassment Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we want every person to feel safe, respected, and able to do their best work. This policy explains what we do not tolerate, how you can raise a concern, and what happens after you do. It applies to all employees, contractors, and anyone else who works with us, whether you are at the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or working remotely from home in any of the countries where we employ people.

This version supersedes version 0.9 and clarifies our commitment to listening, protecting, and acting. Speaking up is not always easy. We want you to know that raising a concern will never be held against you, provided you are acting in good faith. Even if an investigation does not confirm what you reported, you will not face retaliation for having come forward.

We are a remote-first company with people in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom. Our teams — Routing Core, Fleet Insights, Platform & Infrastructure, Customer Success, and Go-to-Market — work across time zones and cultures. This policy is one way we keep our shared standards clear, no matter where you sit.

## 2. What we do not tolerate

We do not tolerate harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation in any form. This includes conduct that occurs during work hours, at work-related social events, on business travel, or in digital spaces such as Slack, video calls, or email.

Harassment includes unwelcome verbal, physical, or visual conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment. It can be based on any protected characteristic, including age, gender identity, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, disability, or family status. We also do not tolerate sexual harassment, which includes unwanted advances, requests for favors, or other conduct of a sexual nature.

Bullying is repeated, unreasonable behavior that demeans, humiliates, or undermines a person. It does not need to be illegal to be unacceptable here. Examples include persistent belittling, public humiliation, excluding someone from work-related communication in a way that harms their performance, or spreading malicious rumors.

Retaliation is any adverse action taken against someone because they raised a concern, participated in an investigation, or supported a colleague who did. Retaliation is a separate violation of this policy, even if the original concern is not confirmed. We protect people who speak up in good faith, and we also expect that reports are made honestly. Knowingly making a false report is itself a violation.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, notices that a colleague from another team repeatedly makes dismissive comments about Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland. Deniz is unsure whether this rises to a formal complaint, but she feels the behavior is wrong. She can raise the concern without fear, and she will not be treated differently for doing so.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, receives unwanted private messages from a peer that make her uncomfortable. She is worried that reporting it will affect her standing on the Routing Core team. Under this policy, Anna can report the behavior confidentially, and any adverse reaction to her report would itself be a policy violation.

## 3. How to raise a concern

If you experience or witness behavior that may violate this policy, you have several ways to speak up. Choose the path you are most comfortable with; all are taken seriously.

- **Talk to your manager or a leader you trust.** You can start by speaking with your engineering manager, your team lead, or any member of the leadership team, including CEO Marta Lindqvist, CTO Jonas Weber, CFO Tomasz Zieliński, VP People & Culture Aylin Demir, Head of Information Security Priya Nayar, or Head of Workplace & Operations Rui Almeida.
- **Contact People & Culture.** You can reach out to the People & Culture team directly at people@kranich.example. Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, and her colleagues can guide you through the process and answer questions about this policy.
- **Use the security channel.** If your concern involves a potential legal or security issue, or if you prefer a route outside People & Culture, you can write to security@kranich.example. This mailbox is monitored by the Information Security function under Priya Nayar.
- **Ask for a different contact.** If the person you would normally report to is involved in the situation, or if you simply prefer to speak with someone else, say so. People & Culture will arrange an alternative contact for you.

You do not need proof to raise a concern. A good-faith description of what you experienced or observed is enough to start the process. You can raise a concern on your own behalf or on behalf of a colleague who is unable or unwilling to do so.

If you are unsure whether a situation falls under this policy, please ask. You can contact People & Culture or the security team for confidential guidance without triggering a formal process. We would rather you ask than stay silent.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, sees a pattern of comments in a group chat that she finds degrading toward a teammate. She is not directly involved and wonders if she should say anything. She can raise the concern through any of the channels above, and People & Culture will treat it with the same seriousness as a report from the affected person.

## 4. What happens next

Once you raise a concern, People & Culture will acknowledge receipt and assess the situation. Every concern receives a first response within 7 business days. This first response may be a confirmation that an initial review is underway, a request for more information, or an outline of next steps.

Depending on the nature of the concern, People & Culture may conduct an internal review, involve the Information Security team under Priya Nayar, or engage an external investigator. The process is designed to be fair to everyone involved: the person who raised the concern, the person named in the concern, and any witnesses.

During the process, we will keep the matter as confidential as reasonably possible. Only those who need to know will be informed. You will be updated on progress, and you can ask for a status update at any time.

If the review finds that this policy was violated, we will take appropriate action. This may include coaching, a formal warning, changes to working arrangements, or, in serious cases, termination of employment. The response will be proportionate to the behavior and consistent with prior cases. If the review does not confirm the concern, no action will be taken against the person who reported it, provided the report was made in good faith.

Retaliation against anyone involved in raising or investigating a concern is prohibited and will be treated as a separate violation. If you believe you are experiencing retaliation, report it promptly using the same channels described in §3.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, receives a report from a member of her team about a colleague's conduct. She forwards the concern to People & Culture the same day. Within 7 business days, People & Culture responds to the team member with an outline of the review process and a point of contact. Ines continues to manage the team as usual, and she takes care not to treat the reporting team member differently in any way.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, how to raise a concern, or what happens after you do, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. You can also speak with your manager, or with any member of the leadership team. For questions about confidentiality or the security of the reporting channels, contact security@kranich.example.

We also encourage you to review related policies in the Grove policy library, including POL-004, which covers respectful workplace conduct more broadly. This policy is not a substitute for local law; where national law in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France or the United Kingdom provides additional protections, those protections apply.

Speaking up takes courage. We are grateful to everyone who helps us keep Kranich Software a place where people can be themselves and do their best work. If you are ever in doubt, please ask. We would rather answer your question than miss a chance to make things right.
