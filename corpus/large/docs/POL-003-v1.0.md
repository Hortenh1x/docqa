# Workation Guideline

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we believe that great work can happen from many places. A workation — a period when you combine work with a temporary stay in another location within the EU/EEA — can be a wonderful way to refresh your perspective while staying fully productive. This guideline explains how you can make use of a workation, what to consider before you go, and which steps are required to make it a smooth experience for you, your team, and the company.

We are a remote-first company with team members across Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom. Many of us already enjoy the flexibility of working from home. A workation extends that flexibility a little further, letting you experience a change of scenery for a limited period while remaining connected to your colleagues and your work.

This policy is written for everyone at Kranich. It applies regardless of whether you work from our Berlin office, our Lisbon office, or remotely from home. If you have any questions after reading this guideline, please reach out to People & Culture or check the contact details in §5.

## 2. How much workation is allowed

Workation is limited to 30 working days per calendar year within the EU/EEA. This means that, in any given calendar year, you may work from a location other than your usual place of employment for up to 30 working days in total. You do not need to take these days in one block; you can split them across several shorter stays, as long as the total does not exceed the yearly limit.

A single workation stay may not exceed 21 calendar days. If you wish to stay longer in a location, you would need to take regular leave or annual vacation for the remaining time, or arrange a different type of absence with your manager. The workation portion of your trip must fit within the 21 calendar days, and any additional days are your own time.

Please note that the limit applies to working days, while the maximum length of a single stay is counted in calendar days. For clarity, a stay that spans weekends or public holidays still counts toward the 21 calendar days, but only the working days within that stay count toward the 30 working days per calendar year.

Workation is available to all employees, regardless of your team or your home location. Whether you are a backend engineer in Lisbon, a customer success manager in Berlin, or a product manager working remotely from Spain, the same rules apply. You are responsible for tracking your own usage, and your manager will help you confirm that your plans fit within the limits before you go.

## 3. Approval

A workation requires approval from your manager before you travel. You should discuss your plans with your manager at least a few weeks in advance, so that your team can plan around your absence and any time-zone differences. This is not a formality — it is a chance to align on expectations, confirm that your work can be done from the new location, and ensure that your project commitments will not be affected.

To request approval, you should have a conversation with your manager and then confirm the arrangement in writing, for example by email. Your manager will check that the proposed dates fit within the yearly limit and the maximum stay length described in §2. They will also consider whether your role can be performed effectively from the chosen location, taking into account client meetings, on-site requirements, and collaboration with your team.

For some roles, particularly those that involve regular client interaction, your manager may need to consult with others before approving. For example, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland may need to ensure that no client visits are scheduled during the workation period. Similarly, if you work in Customer Success and are expected to be available during certain hours, you should confirm that your workation location allows you to meet those expectations.

Approval is given on a case-by-case basis. Even if you have taken a workation before, each new request is considered individually. Your manager may decline a request if it conflicts with business needs, but we encourage open conversations and hope that most reasonable requests can be accommodated.

Once approved, you should inform your team and anyone who might need to reach you. Please also make sure that your calendar reflects your location and availability. If your plans change after approval, you must notify your manager promptly.

## 4. Tax and insurance

Working from another country, even temporarily, can have implications for tax and social security. Because we are a company with employees in several countries, we must ensure that every workation complies with local regulations. You are not expected to become a tax expert, but you need to be aware that a workation is not simply a personal choice — it has legal dimensions that we take seriously.

Before you travel, you must inform People & Culture about your planned workation. People & Culture will review your request in light of your employment country and the country you plan to visit. In some cases, a short stay may be covered by existing arrangements between countries. In other cases, additional steps may be needed. We will guide you through any requirements and let you know what documentation, if any, is needed.

Insurance is another important consideration. Your usual health and accident coverage may not apply in the same way when you work from another country, even within the EU/EEA. Please check with People & Culture or consult the relevant information on our intranet, Grove, to understand what coverage applies during a workation. You may need to arrange additional insurance for the duration of your stay, and any such cost is your responsibility.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, would like to spend a few weeks working from France. Before she books anything, she contacts People & Culture to confirm that her planned stay is permissible and to understand any tax or insurance implications. People & Culture reviews her situation and advises her on the steps she needs to take. Anna then discusses the dates with her manager, receives approval, and proceeds with her travel plans.

We know that tax and insurance topics can feel complex. Please do not let that discourage you from considering a workation — simply involve People & Culture early in your planning. We will do our best to make the process clear and straightforward. If a particular destination would create complications that we cannot resolve, we will let you know before you make any commitments.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this guideline, please reach out to People & Culture. You can contact us by email at people@kranich.example, or find us on Grove, our intranet and policy library. We are happy to discuss your situation and help you understand how this policy applies to you.

For questions related to tax, insurance, or other legal aspects of working from another country, People & Culture will coordinate with the appropriate internal teams. You do not need to contact external advisors yourself.

If you are unsure whether a planned trip qualifies as a workation, or whether it fits within the limits described in §2, please ask before you travel. It is much easier to clarify things in advance than to resolve issues after the fact.

We hope this guideline helps you make the most of the flexibility we offer. A workation can be a wonderful way to combine work with a change of scenery, and we want to support you in doing so safely and compliantly.
