# Remote Work Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH is a remote-first company. We believe great work happens when people have flexibility over where they do their best thinking, while staying connected to their teams and our mission.

This policy explains how remote work operates at Kranich. It covers where you may work, how we coordinate hours, what equipment we provide, and how you keep company data safe outside our offices.

This version supersedes version 1.0 of this policy. It reflects how we have matured as a remote-first organization since our founding, and it clarifies expectations for employees across all our countries of employment.

We wrote this policy for everyone — whether you joined us recently or have been here since the early days. If something is unclear, reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example.

## 2. Where you may work

You may work up to 100% remotely within your country of employment. There is no minimum number of days you must spend in an office each week or month.

If you are employed in Germany, you may work from home, from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, or from any other location within Germany. If you are employed in Portugal, you may work from home, from the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or from anywhere else in Portugal. The same applies for colleagues employed in Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom.

Working from another country — even temporarily — is not covered by this policy. Cross-border work raises tax, social security and data-protection questions. If you are considering such an arrangement, speak with People & Culture before making any plans.

We encourage you to visit our offices when it suits you. Both offices have space for focused work, meetings and socializing. You can book a desk or a meeting room through Perch. If you are visiting an office that is not your usual one, let your manager know in advance so they can plan around your availability.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, lives close to the Ritterstraße office and comes in twice a week. Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, prefers to work from home and visits the Rua do Alecrim office about once a month. Both arrangements are fully supported.

## 3. Working hours and availability

Kranich does not prescribe fixed working hours. We care about outcomes, not about when you log in or log off.

However, remote work only functions when teams can rely on each other. Each team defines its own core collaboration hours — the window during which members are expected to be reachable for meetings and quick questions. Your manager will share your team's core hours with you.

Beyond those core hours, you are free to structure your day as you see fit. Many colleagues start early, take a long midday break, and finish later. Others prefer a continuous day. Choose what works for you and your team, and communicate your schedule clearly in your calendar.

If your role involves direct client contact, you may need to align with client time zones. Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, schedules his availability around their business hours. Your manager will help you find a sustainable rhythm if your role requires such alignment.

We trust you to manage your time responsibly. If you need to step away during core hours for a medical appointment or a personal matter, let your team know and make up the time as needed.

## 4. Equipment and workspace

Kranich provides the equipment you need to do your job well. When you join, you receive a laptop, a monitor, a keyboard, a mouse and a headset. If your role requires additional equipment, discuss this with your manager.

You are responsible for setting up a productive workspace in your home. This means a quiet area where you can take calls, a comfortable chair, and sufficient lighting. We do not prescribe a specific setup — what matters is that you can work effectively and without strain.

Your home workspace is reviewed with your manager every 18 months. This review is a conversation, not an inspection. Its purpose is to check that your setup still supports your work, to identify any equipment that needs replacing, and to discuss ergonomic concerns before they become problems.

If you need something for your workspace — a different chair, a second monitor, a cable — ask your manager or submit a request through Ledgerly. We want you to work comfortably, and we will approve reasonable requests promptly.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, works remotely from Spain. During her workspace review, she mentioned that her dining chair caused back pain. Her manager approved a new ergonomic chair, which was delivered within a few days.

When you visit an office, you can use the equipment available there. You do not need to carry your home setup back and forth. If you work from an office regularly, let Workplace & Operations know through Rui Almeida's team, and they will ensure you have a permanent spot.

## 5. Security at home

Working remotely means company data lives in your home. Keeping that data secure is everyone's responsibility.

Always connect through the corporate VPN when accessing Kranich systems. Do not use public Wi-Fi networks for work without the VPN active. If your home network is unsecured, change the router password before you start working.

Your laptop must remain locked whenever you step away, even for a moment. Do not leave screens visible to housemates or visitors. If you live with others, consider a privacy screen if you work in a shared space.

Use 1Password for all passwords and never share your credentials with anyone, including colleagues. The IT team will never ask for your password. If someone claims to be from IT and requests credentials, report it to security@kranich.example immediately.

Physical security matters too. Do not leave company equipment unattended in public places. When traveling, keep your laptop with you or in a locked safe. If your laptop is lost or stolen, report it to IT Help and to security@kranich.example within a few hours.

Dispose of confidential documents properly. Shred paper documents that contain client or company information rather than throwing them in household recycling.

Example: Anna works from her apartment in Lisbon and shares the space with a roommate. She keeps her laptop in a drawer when not in use and uses a privacy screen during video calls. When her roommate had guests over, Anna moved her work to the bedroom for the afternoon.

If you have questions about securing your home network or devices, contact IT Help at it-help@kranich.example. Priya Nayar's team publishes guidelines on the Grove intranet, and they are happy to advise on specific setups.

## 6. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager. They can help with practical matters like your workspace review or your team's core hours.

For questions about eligibility, cross-border work, or any other employment-related aspect, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, our people operations specialist, handles most remote work queries and will respond promptly.

For security-related questions, contact security@kranich.example. For equipment or office questions, contact Workplace & Operations through Rui Almeida's team.

We review this policy periodically. When we update it, we will announce the changes on Grove and give you time to read them. Your feedback is welcome at any time — remote work evolves, and so should our policies.
