# Sick Leave & Medical Absence

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich, we want you to focus on getting better when you are ill. This policy explains what to do when you are sick and cannot work, how we support you financially, and what we expect from you so the process is smooth for everyone.

We trust you to take the time you need to recover. This policy applies to all employees, whether you work from home, from one of our offices in Berlin or Lisbon, or while travelling for work. It covers physical illness, injury, and medical appointments that prevent you from working during your scheduled hours.

If you are unsure about anything in this policy, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. We are here to help.

## 2. Telling us

As soon as you know you will not be able to work due to illness, tell your manager and the People & Culture team. Please do this on your first day of absence, before your usual start time if possible. A quick message is enough; you do not need to provide details about your symptoms.

You can notify us by email or through Fern, our HRIS. If you are unable to reach your manager directly, you may ask a colleague to pass along the message, or contact People & Culture instead. The important thing is that we hear from you promptly.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wakes up feeling unwell on a Tuesday. She sends a short message to her manager and logs her absence in Fern before her normal start time. Later that day, when she feels a bit better, she replies to any questions from her team about her planned work.

If your absence continues beyond what you initially expected, keep your manager updated. You do not need to report daily, but please let us know if your return date changes. For absences that last more than a few days, we will agree with you on a simple update rhythm that works for both sides.

## 3. Doctor's certificate

For short absences, we trust your word. You do not need to provide a doctor's certificate for the first couple of days of illness.

A doctor's certificate is required from the 3rd calendar day of absence. This means that if you are sick on a Monday and still sick on Wednesday, we need a certificate covering your absence. The certificate must state that you are unable to work and, where possible, the expected duration of your incapacity.

Please submit the certificate through Fern or send it to people@kranich.example. We will confirm receipt. If you cannot get a certificate because you are too ill to visit a doctor, or because of circumstances beyond your control, let us know as soon as you can. We will handle these situations with understanding.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, feels ill on a Wednesday and stays home. She expects to recover quickly and does not see a doctor. On Friday she is still unwell, so she visits her doctor and obtains a certificate covering Wednesday through the weekend. She uploads it to Fern on Friday afternoon.

If your doctor advises that you need to stay home longer than initially expected, please obtain an updated certificate before the current one expires. This helps us plan and ensures you have enough time to recover fully.

## 4. Pay during illness

We want you to recover without worrying about your income. Kranich continues full pay for the first 6 weeks of illness. This applies to each period of illness separately, in line with local legal requirements in the country where you are employed.

After the first 6 weeks of a continuous illness, your pay will follow the statutory rules of your country of employment. In Germany, for example, health insurance typically provides sick pay thereafter. For colleagues in Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom, the local social security system applies. Your manager or People & Culture can point you to the relevant information, but please note that we cannot give tax or legal advice.

If your illness is due to an accident at work or an occupational disease, different rules may apply. Please inform your manager as soon as possible so we can support you and, where relevant, involve our insurance provider. Your pay during such absence follows the applicable statutory provisions.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, falls ill for several weeks. For the first 6 weeks, Kranich pays him in full as usual. After that, his pay continues according to Polish social security regulations. People & Culture helps him understand what to expect and when.

Please note that sick pay is calculated based on your regular salary. Variable components, such as commissions or bonuses, are handled according to your employment contract and the local rules. If you have questions about your specific situation, please contact People & Culture.

## 5. Returning to work

When you are ready to return, please let your manager know at least a day in advance. This gives your team time to prepare and helps you ease back in.

You do not need a doctor's certificate to confirm you are fit to work after a short illness. For longer absences, your doctor may recommend a gradual return, for example reduced hours or modified duties for a period. Please discuss this with your manager and People & Culture. We will do our best to accommodate medical recommendations where operationally possible.

If you return from an absence that lasted several weeks, we suggest scheduling a short catch-up with your manager. This is an opportunity to review any changes that happened while you were away, prioritise your work, and agree on how to handle anything that accumulated. We do not expect you to catch up on everything at once.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, returns after a two-week absence. Her manager schedules a thirty-minute video call to bring her up to speed. Together they agree that she will focus on one key deliverable for the first week rather than trying to cover everything.

If you have a chronic condition that causes recurring absences, please let People & Culture know if you feel comfortable doing so. We can discuss workplace adjustments that might help you manage your health while working. Any information you share is treated confidentially.

## 6. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, your pay during illness, or how to report an absence, please contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. For matters related to data protection or the secure handling of your medical information, please contact our Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar, via security@kranich.example.

We also encourage you to speak with your manager if you have concerns about your workload or wellbeing. It is always better to raise worries early than to wait until you are unwell.

For practical questions about using Fern to log your absence, or about how your absence affects your leave balance, please reach out to the People Operations team. Lea, our people operations specialist in Berlin, is happy to help.

We want Kranich to be a place where you feel supported when you are ill, and where returning to work feels manageable. Please do not hesitate to ask for help when you need it.
