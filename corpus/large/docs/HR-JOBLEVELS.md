# Job Levels Framework

## 1. Purpose

The Job Levels Framework gives every employee at Kranich Software a clear, shared understanding of how roles grow. It describes the levels we use across the company, what we expect at each level, and how we make leveling decisions.

This framework is not a list of titles. It is a tool for conversation — between you and your manager, between teams, and between People & Culture and the wider organization. It helps us hire fairly, review consistently, and develop people in a way that makes sense for their work and for the company.

We keep the framework deliberately simple. We want you to recognize yourself in it, not to feel that you need a manual to find your place.

## 2. Who this is for

This framework applies to everyone at Kranich Software, regardless of team, location, or employment type. Whether you are a backend engineer in the Routing Core team in Lisbon, a customer success manager in Berlin, or a product manager on Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, the same levels and the same principles apply.

Managers use this framework when they onboard new team members, hold development conversations, and prepare promotion proposals. Employees use it to understand where they are and what growth looks like next. People & Culture uses it to ensure fairness across teams and countries.

If you work in a role that is not easily mapped to a level — for example, because your team has a specialized function — your manager will work with People & Culture to find the closest fit. We expect this to be the exception, not the rule.

## 3. Process

The framework defines levels along a single progression. Each level builds on the one before it. We describe levels in terms of scope, complexity, and impact — not in terms of time served or tenure.

A level is a combination of what you do and how you do it. What you do is the substance of your work: the projects you lead, the problems you solve, the decisions you own. How you do it is about behavior: how you collaborate, how you communicate, how you handle ambiguity, and how you support others.

When we assess a level, we look at demonstrated behavior over a sustained period. A single project, however impressive, is not enough. We look for patterns.

The framework has three broad bands:

- **Individual contributor levels** — for people who focus on deep execution within their domain.
- **Lead levels** — for people who guide others, set technical or functional direction, and take responsibility for outcomes beyond their own work.
- **Management levels** — for people who build and lead teams, and who own the health and delivery of a whole area.

You do not have to move into management to grow. The individual contributor and lead tracks are equally valued, and progression within them is a normal, supported path.

Promotions are decided by your manager together with the relevant team lead, and are reviewed by People & Culture to ensure consistency. We look at your work against the level description, not against your colleagues. There is no quota and no ranking against others.

If you believe your level does not reflect your work, talk to your manager. If you are not comfortable doing that, you can reach out to People & Culture directly. We take leveling questions seriously and will review them promptly.

## 4. Timelines

We review levels as part of the regular performance cycle. There is no fixed rhythm for promotion — people grow at different speeds, and we want the framework to reflect that.

When you join Kranich Software, your initial level is set during the hiring process. It is based on your experience, the needs of the team, and the expectations of the role. Your manager will explain your level to you during your first weeks, together with what growth might look like from there.

You can expect a leveling conversation at least once a year, during the review cycle. Outside that cycle, you can raise a leveling request at any time. There is no need to wait for a specific date.

When a leveling request is raised, we aim to give you a clear answer within a few weeks. If the answer is not a promotion, we will explain what would need to be different, and we will support you in working toward that.

Levels are not permanent. A level reflects your current work and impact. If the nature of your role changes — for example, because a team restructures — your level may be reassessed. We will always communicate such changes clearly and in advance.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this framework, start with your manager. They know your work best and can help you see where you sit within the levels.

If you prefer not to ask your manager, or if you want a second perspective, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Our People Operations team, including Lea in Berlin, is happy to walk you through the framework and to help you understand your level.

For questions about how levels relate to compensation, please refer to the relevant finance and people documents, such as FIN-RATES-2026. For questions about how levels are used in hiring, talk to your recruiter or to People & Culture.

We want this framework to be a living tool. If something in it is unclear, or if you see a gap that affects your work, tell us. The best improvements to this framework have come from employees who used it and found a way to make it better.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, was unsure whether her level matched the scope of the platform work she had been leading. She asked her manager, Ines Duarte, for a leveling review. Together they looked at Anna's projects over the past year, discussed the level descriptions, and agreed on a path forward. The conversation was straightforward because both of them were working from the same framework.
