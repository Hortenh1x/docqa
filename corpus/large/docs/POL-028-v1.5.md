# Office & Meeting Room Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how to use our offices in Berlin (Ritterstraße) and Lisbon (Rua do Alecrim), how to book desks and meeting rooms, and what to expect when hosting guests. It applies to everyone at Kranich Software GmbH, whether you work from an office regularly, occasionally, or not at all.

This version supersedes version 1.0. It reflects how we have grown, what we have learned about hybrid work, and the tools we now use every day. If you have questions after reading this policy, please reach out to Workplace & Operations or your manager.

We are a remote-first company. Most of us work from home in our country of employment, and the offices exist to support collaboration, focus time, and team gatherings. No one is required to come into an office, and no one is penalized for working remotely. At the same time, when you do visit an office, we ask everyone to follow the same simple rules so that the space works well for all.

## 2. Desks

Both offices offer open-plan seating with a mix of dedicated team areas and flexible desks. You do not need to request a permanent desk. Instead, book a desk for the day or for a half day through Perch, our booking tool.

Desks can be booked up to 8 days ahead in Perch. This gives everyone a fair chance to plan their office days while keeping the calendar flexible enough for spontaneous visits. If your plans change, please cancel your booking so a colleague can take the desk.

When you book a desk, you will see the floor, the zone, and the desk number. Please sit at the desk you booked. If you arrive and find someone else at your desk, it is usually a simple mistake — check with them politely, and if the desk is truly occupied, pick another available desk and update your booking in Perch.

Please keep your workspace tidy. At the end of the day, take your belongings with you, clear any rubbish, and leave the desk ready for the next person. We do not have assigned storage lockers at either office, so please do not leave personal items overnight.

Quiet focus is important. If you need to take a call or join a video meeting, please use a meeting room or a phone booth rather than staying at your desk. Headphones are welcome at any time.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, comes to the Ritterstraße office every Tuesday. She books her desk on the Monday before, which is well within the booking window. When she arrives, she checks the desk number in Perch and settles in for the day.

If you have accessibility needs or require a specific setup, please let Workplace & Operations know in advance. We will do our best to arrange a suitable desk.

## 3. Meeting rooms

Meeting rooms are available at both offices for conversations, workshops, video calls, and interviews. You can book them through Perch, just like desks. Each room shows its capacity, equipment, and location on the floor plan.

Room bookings work on a first-come, first-served basis. For recurring meetings, please be considerate: each person may hold at most 3 slots of recurring room bookings. This ensures that no single team or individual blocks a room for the whole week, and that others can find space when they need it.

If you need a room for a one-off meeting, book it as soon as you know the date. If the room you want is taken, check the floor plan for an alternative. Many rooms have similar setups, and a slightly different room often works just as well.

Please start and end your meeting on time. If you finish early, release the room in Perch so others can use it. If you are running late and someone else is waiting, they may knock and ask about the remaining time — please be gracious.

Video calls from a meeting room: check that the camera and microphone work before you start. If you are the remote participant, be aware that the room may have several people. Use the room display to see everyone clearly.

Meeting rooms are not storage spaces. Please remove your materials, notes, and personal items when your meeting ends. If you notice a room is left untidy, you can report it through Perch or to Workplace & Operations.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, visits the Berlin office for a workshop. He books a room for the afternoon through Perch. His team also has a weekly sync that recurs, but he holds only one recurring booking, so he is well within the limit.

If you need a room outside normal office hours, please check with Workplace & Operations first. Some rooms may be available, but we need to ensure the building is accessible and that cleaning schedules are respected.

## 4. Guests

You are welcome to invite guests to our offices, whether they are clients, partners, candidates, or colleagues from another Kranich office. A guest is anyone who does not normally work at the office you are visiting.

Guest registration is described in OPS-VISITORS. Please follow that process before your guest arrives. It covers who needs to be registered, how to register them, and what information we need for security and safety purposes.

When your guest arrives, please meet them at the reception area or the entrance and escort them for the duration of their visit. Guests should not wander through the office unaccompanied. If you need to step away, ask a colleague to look after your guest or bring them to a meeting room where they can wait comfortably.

Guests may use the guest Wi-Fi network. Please share the network name and password from the welcome card at reception. Guests do not have access to internal tools or systems, and they should not be left alone with access to unattended screens or documents.

If your guest is visiting for a meeting, please book a meeting room in advance and let reception know the expected arrival time. If your guest arrives early and you are not yet there, reception will contact you.

Please make sure your guest signs out at reception when they leave, if the reception process requires it. This helps us keep track of who is in the building, especially in an emergency.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, visits the Lisbon office for a client workshop. She registers her two guests through the visitor process described in OPS-VISITORS. On the day, she meets them at reception, brings them to the booked room, and walks them out at the end.

If you are hosting a guest from another Kranich office, the same registration process applies. Even though they are colleagues, they are still a guest at that office and need to be registered for safety reasons.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about anything in this policy, please contact Workplace & Operations at the usual channels. For practical issues on the day — a broken screen, a room that is locked, or a desk that is double-booked — you can also reach out through Perch or speak to reception.

For questions about guest access or security, please contact the security team at security@kranich.example. For IT-related questions about Perch or your office equipment, write to it-help@kranich.example.

If you believe this policy needs an update, please let us know. Workplace & Operations reviews it regularly, and we welcome feedback from everyone who uses the offices.

We want our offices to be welcoming, productive places for focused work and meaningful collaboration. Thank you for helping us keep them that way.
