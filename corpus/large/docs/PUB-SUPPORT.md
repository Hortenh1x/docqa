# Support Handbook

## 1. Scope

This handbook describes how the Customer Success team at Kranich Software GmbH handles support for the Kranich Route Cloud. It applies to every employee who interacts with customers on support matters, whether you are a customer success manager, a field solutions engineer, or someone covering the queue for a colleague.

Our goal is simple: when a customer reaches out, they should feel heard, informed, and confident that their issue is being handled. This handbook gives you the framework for doing exactly that — where to focus, which channels to use, and when to escalate.

We work with mid-size logistics operators across Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom. Customers may contact us in their local language. You do not need to be fluent in every language we serve, but you should always confirm understanding and, when needed, loop in a colleague who can communicate comfortably with the customer.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, frequently receives calls directly from dispatchers. He uses this handbook to decide whether an issue stays with him or moves up the priority ladder.

## 2. Priorities

Every ticket receives a priority based on how severely it impacts the customer's operation. Assign priority at intake, then revisit it whenever new information arrives. A ticket's priority can change — for example, a workaround may reduce the impact, or an outage may widen.

- **Priority-1** — The customer cannot operate. Route planning or dispatching is fully blocked, or data is at risk. These tickets receive a first response within 1 hour. We treat every Priority-1 ticket as a team effort.
- **Priority-2** — A major feature is impaired, but the customer has a workaround. The customer can still run their daily operation, though with noticeable friction.
- **Priority-3** — A minor issue or a general question. The customer can operate normally; the issue is an inconvenience or a request for information.
- **Priority-4** — A low-impact request, such as a documentation suggestion or a nice-to-have enhancement.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, reports that the route-calculation service returns errors for one customer's entire depot. Because the customer cannot dispatch at all, Deniz, the customer success manager in Berlin, marks the ticket Priority-1 and responds within 1 hour.

When you assign a priority, record the reasoning in the ticket. This helps the next person understand why the ticket is urgent and what the customer has already been told.

## 3. Channels

Customers can reach support through several channels. All channels feed into the same ticketing system, so a conversation may start on one channel and continue on another without losing context.

- **Email** — The primary channel for detailed issues and for customers who prefer a written record.
- **Phone** — Best for urgent matters and for building rapport. If a customer calls about a Priority-1 issue, create the ticket while on the call or immediately after.
- **In-app chat** — For quick questions and for customers already working inside the Kranich Route Cloud.
- **Video call** — Useful for screen-sharing and for complex configuration questions.
- **Customer portal** — Where customers can track their open tickets and review past resolutions.

Choose the channel that best serves the customer, not the one that is most convenient for you. If a customer prefers a channel we do not offer, explain the alternatives and help them move forward.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, receives a chat message from a customer who cannot export a report. She answers in chat, then offers a video call to walk through the export settings. The customer accepts, and Sofia resolves the issue in minutes.

When you respond on any channel, confirm which customer and which environment the issue concerns. Never assume that the person contacting you is the only stakeholder — logistics operators often have several dispatchers who share one account.

## 4. Escalation

Escalation is not a failure. It is a way of making sure the right expertise reaches the customer at the right time. Escalate when you cannot resolve the issue within a reasonable effort, when the issue affects multiple customers, or when you suspect a defect in the Routing Core or Fleet Insights products.

Follow these steps:

- **First, document.** Write down what you have tried, what the customer has confirmed, and what remains unclear. A well-documented escalation saves the next engineer valuable time.
- **Second, escalate to your team lead.** Your team lead can decide whether to bring in another customer success manager or to contact the engineering team directly.
- **Third, if the issue is technical, involve Routing Core.** Ines Duarte, the engineering manager for Routing Core in Berlin, can route the issue to the right engineer. For Fleet Insights issues, involve that product team through the same channel.
- **Fourth, for security-related concerns, contact security@kranich.example.** Priya Nayar's team handles anything involving data protection or unauthorized access. Do not delay — security matters are always urgent.

For billing or contractual questions, refer to FIN-RATES-2026 and involve the Finance team. Do not make commitments about pricing or terms on your own.

When you escalate, tell the customer what is happening. A short message — "We have involved our engineering team and will update you as soon as we know more" — goes a long way. Keep the customer informed at each step, even if there is no new information yet.

Example: Deniz receives a Priority-2 ticket about incorrect mileage calculations for a customer in France. After reproducing the issue with Marek, she escalates to Ines, who assigns an engineer from Routing Core. Deniz lets the customer know the team is investigating, and she checks back the next day with an update.

After any escalation is resolved, review the ticket with your team lead. Ask what could have been caught earlier and whether the handbook needs an update. Support is a learning loop, and every escalation is a chance to improve.
