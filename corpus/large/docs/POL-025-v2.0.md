# Performance Review Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy describes how performance reviews work at Kranich Software GmbH. It applies to all employees, regardless of where they work from, and covers our annual review cycle, the ratings we use, and how we ensure fairness across teams and offices.

This version supersedes version 1.5. It reflects feedback from employees and managers gathered over the past year, and clarifies how calibration works so that everyone experiences reviews as consistent and transparent.

Our goal is simple: we want every person to know how they are doing, what they should keep doing, and where they can grow. We also want managers to have the information they need to support their teams well. Reviews are a shared responsibility — they work best when employees, managers, and People & Culture all take part thoughtfully.

## 2. Review cycle

We run one performance review cycle per calendar year. The cycle opens in January and closes in early spring. People & Culture announces the exact opening date on Grove and via email at least a few days in advance.

The cycle has four phases:

- Self-review: every employee reflects on their own contributions over the past year.
- Manager review: each manager assesses the work of every person on their team.
- Calibration: managers and People & Culture meet to align ratings across teams.
- Feedback conversation: each employee meets with their manager to discuss the outcome.

Self-reviews are due within 7 working days of the cycle opening. Managers receive a reminder from Fern when the self-review window opens, and they are expected to encourage their teams to complete this step promptly.

After self-reviews are submitted, managers have a few weeks to complete their assessments. People & Culture then schedules calibration sessions. The feedback conversations take place after calibration is finished, so that what a manager shares with an employee reflects the final, calibrated rating.

For people who joined Kranich in the second half of the year, managers may adapt the process. In that case, the manager and the employee agree on a review period that covers the time the employee has actually been with us. People & Culture is available to help structure this.

Example: Sofia, a product manager on Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, joined Kranich in September. Her manager agrees with her that the review will cover the months since her start date, and they set a shorter self-review window accordingly.

## 3. Ratings

Every employee receives one of five ratings for the review period. The ratings are:

- **Far exceeds expectations** — for truly exceptional contributions that go well beyond the role.
- **Exceeds expectations** — for strong performance that clearly goes above what the role requires.
- **Meets expectations** — for solid, reliable performance that fully delivers on the role.
- **Partially meets expectations** — for performance that falls short in some areas, with room to improve.
- **Does not meet expectations** — for performance that is significantly below what the role requires.

A rating is not a judgment of a person’s worth. It describes how well the work in the review period matched the expectations for the role. We expect most people to land at “meets expectations,” and that is a good outcome. It means someone is doing exactly what their role requires, and that is what we need from the majority of our people.

Ratings inform but do not automatically determine other outcomes, such as salary adjustments or development opportunities. Those conversations happen separately, and managers should not promise any specific consequence based on a rating alone.

## 4. Calibration

Access: Managers only

Calibration is the process where managers come together, with support from People & Culture, to discuss ratings before they are finalized. The purpose is to make sure that a “meets expectations” in one team means the same thing as a “meets expectations” in another team, and that no team is systematically harsher or more generous than others.

Calibration happens in small groups, usually organized by department or by related teams. Each manager presents the ratings they have proposed for their direct reports, and the group discusses cases where there is doubt. Managers are expected to come prepared with examples of work that support their proposed ratings.

No more than 20% of a team's ratings may be 'exceeds expectations'. This limit helps us keep ratings meaningful and comparable across the company. It also encourages managers to be honest about what “exceeds” really means, rather than using the rating as a default for everyone who does good work.

The limit applies per team, not per department or per office. If a team has fewer than five people, the limit still applies in spirit: a team of four can have at most one person at “exceeds expectations,” and a team of three can have at most one as well. Ratings of “far exceeds expectations” are included in this limit, since they are a higher expression of the same category.

People & Culture facilitates every calibration session and keeps the final record. Managers do not share calibration discussions with their teams beyond the outcome. What is said in calibration stays in calibration.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, leads a team of six. She proposes “exceeds expectations” for two of them. During calibration, her peers point out that one of those two, while very strong, has had a year that looks similar to what other teams would call “meets expectations.” Deniz agrees and adjusts the rating to “meets expectations,” keeping her team within the limit.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, the review cycle, or your own review, there are several places to go.

- **Your manager** — they are the first person to talk to about your review, your rating, or your development.
- **People & Culture** — the team can answer questions about how the process works and how to prepare. You can reach them at people@kranich.example.
- **Grove** — the policy library on Grove contains this policy and related ones, including POL-004, which covers how performance connects to compensation, and POL-011, which sets out the learning budget in its second section.

If you believe a rating was applied unfairly, you can raise a concern with People & Culture. They will review the case together with the relevant manager and, where needed, with the Head of People & Culture. We take fairness seriously, and we want every employee to feel that the process was transparent and respectful.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, is unsure how to write a good self-review. He reaches out to his manager, who suggests he look at the self-review guidance on Grove and offers to review a draft. Marek also sends a question to people@kranich.example about whether he can include client feedback in his self-review, and receives a helpful answer within a few days.

Feedback about the review process itself is always welcome. If something felt unclear, rushed, or unfair, please tell us. We review this policy each year and make changes based on what we hear.
