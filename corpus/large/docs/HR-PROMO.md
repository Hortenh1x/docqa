# Promotion Process

## 1. Purpose

Promotions are one of the ways we recognize growth at Kranich Software. This document explains how a promotion happens, who is involved, and what you can expect along the way.

We want the process to be fair, transparent, and consistent across teams and locations. Whether you work from Berlin, Lisbon, or remotely from another country, the same principles apply.

A promotion is not a reward for tenure. It reflects that your work, skills, and impact have grown into a new level of responsibility. We also understand that growth looks different for everyone. This process is designed to support that variety.

## 2. Who this is for

This policy applies to all employees, regardless of your team, role, or where you are employed. It covers promotions from one role level to the next within your current career track.

If you are unsure which track or level you are on, you can find this in Fern under your profile. Your manager can also help you understand the leveling framework.

This process does not cover lateral moves, which are handled separately by People & Culture. If you change teams but stay at the same level, that is a transfer, not a promotion.

## 3. Process

Promotions are considered twice a year as part of a company-wide review cycle. The process has several steps.

### 3.1 Your manager initiates

Promotions are typically initiated by your manager. They look at your recent work, your contributions to the team, and how you have grown relative to the expectations of your current level.

Your manager will prepare a short written case. This case should describe your achievements, the skills you have developed, and examples of work that show you are already operating at the next level.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, has been leading the design of a new service for the Routing Core team. Her manager notices that she has taken on responsibilities that go beyond her current role, such as mentoring a newer colleague and driving technical decisions. Her manager decides to put her forward in the next cycle.

### 3.2 You can also express interest

If you believe you are ready for a promotion, you do not have to wait for your manager to come to you. You can raise it in your regular one-on-one conversations or during your check-ins.

Your manager will listen and help you assess your readiness. If you both agree it makes sense, your manager will prepare the case. If your manager disagrees, they will explain why and suggest what you can work on for the next cycle.

### 3.3 The calibration meeting

All promotion cases from a team are reviewed together in a calibration meeting. This meeting includes the relevant engineering manager or team lead, a representative from People & Culture, and sometimes a peer manager from another team.

The purpose of calibration is fairness. It ensures that similar contributions are treated similarly across the company. During the meeting, each case is discussed and compared against the leveling criteria.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, is put forward alongside a colleague from the same team. The calibration meeting looks at both cases side by side. The group discusses whether each person's impact matches the next level, and they agree that Deniz's case is stronger because she has taken on a broader client portfolio.

### 3.4 Decision and communication

After the calibration meeting, the final decision is made. Your manager will meet with you to share the outcome, whether positive or not.

If your promotion is approved, the conversation covers your new role, any changes to your responsibilities, and the effective date. Your manager will also explain what happens next in terms of your compensation. Compensation changes follow the guidelines in POL-004.

If your promotion is not approved, your manager will explain the reasons clearly. They will also work with you to create a development plan for the next cycle. A "not yet" is not a rejection of you as a person; it is a signal about where to focus your energy.

### 3.5 Documentation

Once a promotion is approved, People & Culture updates your record in Fern. Your new title and level are reflected there. You will also receive a confirmation by email from People & Culture.

## 4. Timelines

The promotion cycle runs twice a year. The exact dates can vary, so we do not publish a fixed calendar. Your manager will let you know when the next cycle opens.

As a general guide:

- Your manager should prepare the case before the cycle opens.
- Calibration meetings happen after the case submission window closes.
- Decisions are communicated within a few weeks after the calibration meetings.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, hears from his manager that the next cycle opens soon. He prepares a summary of his recent client work. His manager submits the case, and Marek receives the decision a few weeks after the calibration meeting.

If you join the company partway through a cycle, you may still be considered if you have been in your role long enough to show meaningful impact. Your manager will advise you on whether it makes sense to apply.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this process, start with your manager. They know your situation best and can give you specific guidance.

You can also reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, and the rest of the team are happy to help.

If you have concerns about fairness or believe the process was not followed correctly, please contact People & Culture directly. You can also reach out to Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture, if you prefer.

For questions about how a promotion affects your compensation, please refer to POL-004. For questions about your level or role profile, check Fern or ask your manager.

We want everyone at Kranich Software to grow in a way that feels meaningful. This process is here to support that growth with clarity and care.
