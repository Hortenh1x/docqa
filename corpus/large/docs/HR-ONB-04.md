# Onboarding Guide — Customer Success

## 1. Purpose

This guide helps new Customer Success team members find their footing at Kranich Software GmbH. It complements the general company onboarding materials you receive from People & Culture and focuses on what is specific to working with our logistics customers.

You will learn how we support mid-size logistics operators using Kranich Route Cloud, how we collaborate with other teams, and where to turn when you need help. Your manager and your onboarding buddy will walk you through each step. This guide is a reference you can return to at any time.

## 2. Before you start

Before your first day, take care of a few practical matters so you can focus on learning once you arrive.

- **Equipment.** Your manager will confirm what you need. The equipment budget for new joiners is described in POL-006 §3. If anything is missing, write to it-help@kranich.example and the team will help promptly.
- **Accounts.** You will receive invitations to the tools we use daily: Grove for policies and announcements, Fern for personal data and leave, Ledgerly for expenses, and 1Password for shared credentials. Accept these invitations when they arrive.
- **Security training.** Complete your security training within the timeline stated in POL-005 §3. Priya's team sends a reminder, but you can also reach out to security@kranich.example if you have questions.
- **Your buddy.** You will be introduced to a colleague from Customer Success who will be your first point of contact for informal questions. They are there to help you feel at home, not to evaluate you.

## 3. Step by step

**First week.** Your focus is orientation. You will meet your team, learn how we talk about Kranich Route Cloud, and read through the key policies on Grove. Your manager will schedule a short check-in to see how you are settling in. Expect to spend most of this time listening and asking questions.

**Second week.** You will shadow a Customer Success Manager during their daily work. This is a chance to see how we handle customer questions, how we use our internal notes, and how we escalate issues to Routing Core or Platform & Infrastructure. You are encouraged to ask anything — no question is too small.

**Third week.** You will begin handling simple customer inquiries with your buddy reviewing your responses. You will also join your first customer call as an observer. After the call, your buddy will walk you through what went well and what we might do differently next time.

**Fourth week and beyond.** You will take on a small portfolio of customers with close support from your manager. You will learn how to run a quarterly business review, how to spot customers who need extra attention, and how to hand off leads to the Go-to-Market team. Your manager will agree with you on a plan for the coming months.

Throughout onboarding, you will meet colleagues from other teams. Deniz, a Customer Success Manager in Berlin, often joins product feedback sessions with Sofia, who is a Product Manager for Fleet Insights and works remotely from Spain. Marek, a field solutions engineer based in Poland, works closely with customers on site and can share practical insights about how operators use our software. Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, may join customer calls when technical questions come up. These connections will help you build a picture of how we work together.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, recently joined a customer call where the operator asked about a specific routing scenario. Anna listened, explained what was possible, and then worked with the Routing Core team to improve the documentation. As a Customer Success Manager, you will often be the bridge between customers and our technical teams.

## 4. Common questions

**Where do I find customer information?** Customer notes live in our shared workspace. Your manager will show you where to look and how to keep records up to date. Always check the notes before reaching out to a customer so you have context.

**How do I request time off?** Use Fern. Your request goes to your manager for approval. The People & Culture team has published guidance on leave on Grove.

**How do I book travel or submit expenses?** Use Ledgerly for both. If you travel to a customer site or to one of our offices — Berlin's Ritterstraße office or Lisbon's Rua do Alecrim office — book through Ledgerly and keep receipts for anything you pay yourself.

**What if I need a desk or a meeting room?** Use Perch. You can book spaces in either office ahead of time. When you are remote, you can still use Perch to see who is in the office on a given day.

**Who do I contact with technical problems?** Write to it-help@kranich.example for anything related to your laptop, accounts, or access. For security concerns, contact security@kranich.example.

**What if a customer asks for something we do not offer?** Be honest and helpful. Explain what we can do, and if it is a feature request, share it with the product team through the usual channel. Sofia and her colleagues in Fleet Insights and Routing Core appreciate clear, real-world examples from customers.

**How does remote work fit into onboarding?** We are a remote-first company, and your onboarding is designed to work whether you are in Berlin, Lisbon, or at home. Your buddy and manager will schedule regular video calls with you. If you live near an office, you are welcome to work from there sometimes, but it is not expected.
