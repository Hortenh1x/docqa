# Vacation & Leave Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH wants everyone to take time to rest, recharge and attend to personal matters. This policy explains how vacation works at Kranich, how to request time off, and what happens when you have unused days at the end of the year. We believe that planned, regular time away from work makes for healthier colleagues and better products.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of whether you work from our office in Berlin, our office in Lisbon, or remotely from your home in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France or the United Kingdom. It covers vacation entitlement, the request process, carryover rules and special leave. If you are employed through a local entity in one of our countries of operation, the statutory minimums of that country apply as a floor; where this policy is more generous, this policy governs.

## 3. Annual vacation entitlement

Full-time employees receive 24 days of vacation per calendar year. This entitlement is prorated for part-time employees and for those who join or leave Kranich partway through the year. Your entitlement is calculated based on the number of months you are employed during the calendar year, and People & Culture will confirm your individual balance in Fern when you start and at the beginning of each year.

Vacation days are counted as full working days. If you work a reduced schedule, your days are counted proportionally. You accrue vacation throughout the year, but you may request days before the full entitlement has accrued, as long as the request is reasonable and your team can plan around it.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined Kranich in the middle of the year. Her entitlement for her first calendar year is prorated to reflect the months she is employed, and she can see her exact balance in Fern.

## 4. Requesting vacation

You request vacation through Fern. Submit your request at least a few days in advance for short breaks, and plan further ahead for longer time away. Requests for more than five consecutive days must be submitted 4 weeks in advance. This lead time helps your team plan work, arrange cover and avoid last-minute surprises.

When you submit a request, your manager reviews it and either approves or declines it. We aim to approve requests promptly. A request may be declined if too many people from your team are away at the same time, if a critical deadline falls in that period, or if the request was submitted too late. If your request is declined, your manager will explain why and suggest alternatives.

We encourage you to coordinate with your team before submitting a request, especially for popular holiday periods. For longer breaks, a short handover note for your colleagues is good practice. You do not need to be available for work during your vacation, and we ask that you set an out-of-office message in your email so colleagues know when you will return.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, wants to take a two-week trip in summer. She submits her request in Fern in early spring, well before the 4 weeks required for more than five consecutive days, and her manager approves it the same week.

## 5. Carryover

We want you to use your vacation within the calendar year. However, we know that plans change, and sometimes you cannot take all your days before December ends. Unused vacation days must be taken by March 15 of the following year. Days not taken by that date are forfeited.

If you have carryover days, you should schedule them early in the new year, before you plan new vacation. Your manager can help you find a good time, particularly if your team has seasonal peaks. People & Culture will send a reminder in January to anyone with a carryover balance.

Carryover is not paid out in cash at the end of the year. The purpose of the March 15 deadline is to ensure you actually take time off rather than accumulate days indefinitely. If exceptional circumstances prevent you from taking your days by the deadline, speak with your manager and People & Culture as early as possible; we will handle such cases individually and with care.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, had three unused days at the end of December. He books them for early February, well before the March 15 deadline, and lets his clients know his availability in advance.

## 6. Special leave

Beyond annual vacation, Kranich offers special leave for certain life events. You do not need to use your vacation days for these situations. Special leave is paid and does not reduce your vacation balance.

- **Bereavement leave.** If a close family member passes away, you may take a few days of paid leave to attend to arrangements and to grieve. Your manager and People & Culture are available to support you.
- **Care leave.** If a dependent falls ill or needs urgent care, you may take a short period of paid leave to manage the situation. For longer care arrangements, please talk to People & Culture about options.
- **Moving day.** When you move house, you may take a day of paid leave to handle the move. This applies once per move.
- **Medical appointments.** Routine medical appointments that cannot be scheduled outside working hours do not count as vacation. Please inform your manager in advance.
- **Public duties.** If you serve on a jury or are called for civic duties, you may take paid leave for the required time.

Special leave is requested through Fern using the appropriate category. If you are unsure whether your situation qualifies, ask People & Culture. We trust you to use this leave responsibly, and we will never ask for intrusive details about your personal circumstances.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, takes a day of special leave when she moves to a new apartment across town. She submits the request in Fern the week before and spends the day unpacking without checking work email.

## 7. Questions

If you have questions about your vacation balance, the request process or special leave, the first place to look is Fern, where you can see your entitlement and submit requests. For questions about how this policy applies to your situation, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example.

Your manager can also help you plan your time off, especially when you need to coordinate with your team. For technical issues with Fern, such as trouble logging in or a request that will not submit, contact it-help@kranich.example.

We want you to take the time you need to stay healthy and engaged. A well-rested team makes better decisions, writes better code and supports our customers better. If anything in this policy is unclear, please ask — we are happy to explain.
