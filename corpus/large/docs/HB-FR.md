# Employee Handbook — France

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of our team. This handbook explains how employment works for our colleagues in France. It covers the essentials: vacation, probation, notice periods, working hours, benefits and more. Please read it alongside the policies referenced here.

We are a remote-first company. You may work from home in France, and you may also use the offices we maintain in Berlin and Lisbon when you travel for work. Your manager and your People & Culture contact are there to support you. If anything in this handbook is unclear, please reach out.

We value plain language. This handbook is written for you, not for lawyers. Where a policy document contains more detail, we point you to it. In case of conflict between this handbook and a policy, the policy governs.

## 2. Your employment in France

Your employment contract with Kranich Software GmbH is governed by French law. You are employed through our French entity, and your workplace is considered to be in France for legal and tax purposes. Your contract states your role, your working time and your place of work.

We expect you to comply with the internal policies that apply to all employees. You can find them in Grove, our intranet and policy library. The most relevant policies for day-to-day work include POL-004 (information security) and POL-007 (sick leave and medical certificates).

Your personal data is processed in line with our privacy notices. You can update your personal details in Fern, our HRIS. If you change your address, bank details or emergency contacts, please update them promptly.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, changed her bank account recently. She updated the details in Fern so that her salary would be paid without delay. You should do the same whenever your details change.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in France receive 25 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This is your paid time off for the reference year. You accrue vacation according to the legal rules that apply in France.

You request vacation through Fern. Please submit your request as early as possible so that your team can plan. Your manager approves or declines the request based on team capacity and business needs. We encourage you to take your full vacation entitlement each year. Rest is important for your wellbeing and for your performance.

Vacation days are calculated in working days. Weekends and public holidays are not counted as vacation days. If a public holiday falls during your vacation, you do not use a vacation day for that date.

Unused vacation days are handled according to French law. Please talk to your manager or People & Culture if you have questions about carrying days over to the next year.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, planned a family visit in the summer. She submitted her request in Fern six weeks in advance. Her manager approved it, and the team arranged coverage for her accounts during her absence.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in France is 7 months. The probation period allows both you and Kranich to assess whether the role is a good fit. It begins on your first working day.

During probation, either you or Kranich may end the employment with a shorter notice period than the standard one. The rules for termination during probation follow French law and are described in your contract.

Your manager will give you feedback during the probation period. You will have a formal check-in partway through and a final review before the end of probation. If the probation period is extended, that extension follows the legal limits and is confirmed in writing.

If you have questions about probation, please speak with your manager or People & Culture. We want you to feel supported from day one.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, found the probation period useful to learn our product and our way of working. He received structured feedback at the midpoint and felt confident going into his permanent role.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in France is 11 weeks. This notice period applies when either you or Kranich ends your employment after the probation period has been completed.

The notice period starts on the date the termination is notified in writing. During the notice period, you are expected to continue your work as usual. Your manager may ask you to hand over your tasks and responsibilities to colleagues.

In some situations, the notice period may be waived or reduced. This happens only by mutual agreement and is always confirmed in writing. French law may provide for different notice periods in specific circumstances, for example in case of redundancy. Your contract and the applicable law govern.

You are not required to work during the notice period if you are on sick leave. In that case, the notice period may be suspended according to French law. Please contact People & Culture if you find yourself in this situation.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, resigned to take a new opportunity. She gave notice in writing, and her manager agreed on a handover plan for the notice period. She used the time to document her projects and introduce her successor to the team.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in France observe 13 days of public holidays in the reference year. Public holidays are paid days off. If a public holiday falls on a weekend, you do not receive an additional day off in lieu, unless your contract or local practice provides otherwise.

You can find the list of public holidays for the current year on Grove. The list follows the French calendar and includes the holidays that apply across the country. Some holidays may be specific to certain regions in France.

If you work on a public holiday because your role requires it, you will agree on time off in lieu with your manager. This arrangement is made case by case and follows the principles in your contract.

Public holidays that fall during your vacation do not count as vacation days. You do not need to use a vacation day for a public holiday.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, checked the public holiday calendar on Grove before planning a team event in France. She made sure the date did not clash with a French public holiday so that colleagues in France could attend.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in France is 37.5 hours per week. This is the reference for full-time employees. Your contract states your individual working time.

We are a remote-first company, and we trust you to manage your time. However, we also expect you to be available during core collaboration hours. Your team defines these hours together, taking into account time zones and client needs.

You should track your working time in the tool your team uses. This helps us comply with French rules on working time and ensures that you do not work excessive hours. If you regularly work more than your contractual hours, please speak with your manager. We do not expect you to work long hours on a regular basis.

Breaks are part of your working day. You are entitled to the breaks required by French law. Please take them. We also encourage you to step away from your screen regularly during the day.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, works with her team to define core hours. Her team members in France and Portugal agree on a window in the late morning and early afternoon when everyone is available for meetings and collaboration.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are unwell and cannot work, please inform your manager as soon as possible. You should also notify People & Culture through the process described in POL-007. The sick-note rules are in POL-007; please follow that policy for the documents you need to provide.

If your absence lasts longer than a few days, you will need a medical certificate. Your doctor in France can provide this. The certificate must be submitted according to the rules in POL-007.

During sick leave, you continue to receive your salary according to French law and your contract. Kranich may provide additional coverage beyond the statutory scheme. Details are in POL-007 and in the benefits section of this handbook.

If you are on sick leave, you are not expected to work or to respond to work messages. Please focus on your recovery. When you return, your manager will welcome you back and help you catch up on what you missed.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, woke up with a fever on a Monday morning. She messaged her manager and submitted her sick note through the process in POL-007. She rested for a few days and returned to work once she felt better.

## 9. Remote work in France

You are employed in France and you may work remotely from home. Remote work is the norm at Kranich. You are not required to work from an office on a regular basis.

Your manager and your team will agree on the collaboration rhythm. This includes regular team meetings, one-on-ones and any on-site events. You are expected to attend these as agreed.

When you work from home, you are responsible for having a suitable workspace. Kranich provides the equipment you need, such as a laptop and a monitor. If you need additional equipment, please contact IT through the it-help alias.

You may also work from the Berlin office or the Lisbon office when you travel for work. Please book a desk in advance through Perch, our booking tool. Perch also shows meeting-room availability if you need a space for calls or workshops.

We are committed to your wellbeing while working remotely. Please maintain a healthy routine, take breaks and set boundaries between work and personal time. If you feel isolated or need support, please talk to your manager or People & Culture.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, works from home most days. When he visits clients in France, he sometimes works from the Berlin office for a day. He books a desk in Perch before he arrives, so he always has a place to sit.

## 10. Benefits in France

Kranich offers a benefits package for employees in France. The package is designed to support your health, your wellbeing and your financial security.

You are enrolled in the French statutory health insurance scheme. In addition, Kranich provides a complementary health coverage plan. This plan covers costs that are not fully reimbursed by the statutory scheme, such as dental care, optical care and certain medical treatments. Details of the plan are available on Grove.

You also have access to our employee assistance program. This program offers confidential support for personal or professional challenges. You can reach it at any time, and the service is free for you.

We offer a retirement savings plan in line with French practice. You can find the details of the plan and how to enrol on Grove. If you have questions, People & Culture can help.

Other benefits may be offered from time to time. They are described on Grove when they are available. Benefits may change, and the current description is always the one on Grove.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Spain, needed new glasses. She checked the complementary health coverage plan on Grove and submitted her claim. The reimbursement was processed promptly. Colleagues in France have a similar experience with their own plan.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly to your French bank account. The payment date is stated in your contract. You will receive a payslip each month, which you can access through Ledgerly, our expenses and payroll tool.

Kranich handles payroll in France through a local payroll provider. The provider calculates your salary, social contributions and withholding tax according to French rules. The withholding tax is deducted from your salary and paid to the tax authorities on your behalf.

You are responsible for filing your annual tax return in France. Your payslips and the annual tax summary provided by the payroll provider will help you complete your return. If you have questions about your taxes, please consult a tax advisor. Kranich cannot give individual tax advice.

Expenses and travel bookings are handled through Ledgerly. If you incur work-related expenses, you can submit them in Ledgerly. The approval process is described in the expenses policy. Please submit your receipts promptly.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, travels to France for a client meeting. She books her train through Ledgerly and submits her meal receipts after the trip. Her expenses are approved by her manager, and the reimbursement is paid with her next salary.

We are glad you are here. Thank you for being part of Kranich Software GmbH.
