# Compensation Review Process

Access: People & Culture only

## 1. Purpose

This document describes how Kranich Software GmbH conducts its annual compensation review. The process exists to keep pay fair, transparent, and aligned with the company's financial health. It applies to all employees in every country where Kranich operates.

We review compensation once per calendar year. The review considers individual performance, market conditions, and internal equity. The goal is not to reward everyone equally, but to ensure that people who contribute meaningfully are recognised, and that no one falls behind due to factors outside their control.

This process is owned by People & Culture. Finance provides budget parameters. Managers provide performance input. Final decisions are made jointly by People & Culture and the leadership team.

## 2. Who this is for

This document is for People & Culture team members who administer the review. It is also relevant for managers who submit recommendations, and for Finance colleagues who validate the budget envelope.

Employees do not see this document. If an employee asks about the review, refer to the general communication prepared by People & Culture. That communication describes the timeline and the appeals path in employee-friendly language.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, asks her manager when the review results will be announced. Her manager explains the timeline from the general communication and does not share internal guidance from this document.

## 3. Process

The review runs in four phases. Each phase has a clear owner and a clear output.

### 3.1 Performance calibration

Managers submit a written assessment for each direct report. Assessments focus on achievements during the review period, not on personality. People & Culture provides a template and a rubric. The rubric defines what each performance level means in behavioural terms.

Calibration sessions follow. In these sessions, managers from the same team compare their assessments to ensure consistency. A facilitator from People & Culture attends each session. The facilitator may challenge assessments that seem inconsistent with evidence provided.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, receives a strong assessment from her manager Ines Duarte. During calibration, another manager notes that Anna took on a complex migration project. Ines confirms this with specific examples. The assessment stands.

### 3.2 Market and equity review

People & Culture prepares a market analysis using reputable salary surveys. The analysis covers each role family and each country of employment. Market data is refreshed annually; we do not rely on stale figures.

An internal equity review follows. This review checks whether people in similar roles with similar performance and experience are paid consistently. Where gaps exist, People & Culture flags them to the leadership team.

The equity review does not automatically produce an adjustment. It produces a list of observations. Leadership decides which observations to act on, considering budget constraints.

### 3.3 Budget allocation

Finance provides a total budget envelope for the review. The envelope is expressed as a percentage of the current payroll. People & Culture distributes this envelope across teams based on team size and performance distribution.

Managers receive their team's envelope and submit individual recommendations. Recommendations must stay within the envelope. People & Culture reviews all recommendations against the calibration outcomes and the equity observations.

If a manager proposes something that conflicts with the equity review, People & Culture asks the manager to justify it. The manager may revise the proposal or provide additional context.

Example: Deniz manages a small team. One of her reports has been performing well but is paid below peers in the same role. Deniz proposes a larger adjustment for that person and a smaller one for another report. People & Culture approves.

### 3.4 Approval and communication

People & Culture consolidates all recommendations into a single plan. The plan is presented to the leadership team for approval. The CEO and the CFO sign off on the final plan.

After approval, People & Culture prepares individual letters. Managers receive talking points for each conversation. Conversations happen in one-on-one meetings, never in group settings.

Employees who receive no adjustment are told why. The reason may be performance, budget, or position relative to market. Employees who receive an adjustment are told the new salary and the effective date.

## 4. Timelines

The review follows the same rhythm each year. The exact dates vary, so this section describes the sequence rather than specific days.

- Performance calibration starts in the first quarter of the year. Managers receive the template and rubric, and have a few weeks to submit assessments.
- Calibration sessions take place shortly after submissions close. Each session lasts approximately an hour.
- The market and equity review runs in parallel with calibration. People & Culture prepares the analysis before calibration sessions begin.
- Budget allocation happens after calibration is complete. Finance confirms the envelope, and People & Culture distributes it within a few days.
- Manager recommendations are due promptly after the envelope is distributed. People & Culture reviews them within a week.
- Leadership approval takes place in the second quarter. Communication follows within a few days of approval.
- Adjustments take effect from the start of the following month after communication.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, receives his letter in the second quarter. The adjustment applies from the first day of the next month. His manager explains the change in a one-on-one call.

## 5. Questions

Questions about this process should go to the People & Culture business partner assigned to your team. If you do not know who that is, contact people@kranich.example.

For questions about the budget envelope, contact Finance directly. For questions about the performance rubric, speak to the People & Culture facilitator who attended your calibration session.

If you believe a decision was made in error, raise it with the Head of People & Culture within a few days of the decision. They will review the case with the relevant manager. The review considers whether the process was followed correctly, not whether the outcome should be different.
