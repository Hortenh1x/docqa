# Employee Handbook — Germany

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of our team. This handbook describes how employment works for our colleagues employed in Germany. It complements your individual employment contract and the policies you find on Grove, our intranet and policy library.

We are a remote-first company. Whether you work from Berlin, Lisbon or from home elsewhere in your country of employment, you are part of one team. We build route-optimization software for mid-size logistics operators, and we do that best when we support one another with clarity and care.

This handbook is written by People & Culture for you. It is not a legal document. If anything in it conflicts with your contract or with German law, your contract and the law come first. If you have questions, reach out to people@kranich.example. We are here to help.

## 2. Your employment in Germany

If you are employed by Kranich Software GmbH in Germany, your employment is governed by your individual contract, by this handbook, by applicable company policies, and by German labor law. Your contract states your role, your working time model, and your salary. Your personal data is managed in Fern, our HRIS. You can view your contract, your personal details, and your organizational chart there at any time.

Your employment begins on the start date stated in your contract. Before your first day, People & Culture will send you onboarding information and invite you to the relevant systems. On your first day, you will meet your manager and your team, and you will receive access to the tools you need, including Perch for meeting-room and desk booking, 1Password for password management, and Grove for policies and company news.

We expect all employees to act in line with our values: respect, reliability, and responsibility. That includes treating colleagues with kindness, protecting company and customer data, and following the policies that apply to your work. If you are unsure about a policy, ask your manager or write to people@kranich.example.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, joined Kranich in the spring. On her first day, her manager walked her through the team's goals and Lea from People Operations helped her set up Fern and Perch. Deniz knew exactly whom to ask when she had questions about her benefits.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in Germany receive 27 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This is your full annual entitlement, calculated on a calendar-year basis. You accrue your vacation entitlement pro rata for each full month of employment in a given year.

You plan your vacation in Fern. Please submit your request as early as you can, and at least a few weeks before your planned leave, so your team can plan around your absence. Your manager approves or declines your request. We ask that you do not take all your vacation at once; spread it across the year so you and your team stay rested.

Vacation should be taken within the calendar year. If you cannot take all your vacation due to operational reasons or illness, you may carry it over into the first quarter of the following year. Unused vacation from previous years expires in line with German law unless your manager has explicitly agreed to a carryover in writing.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, is employed in Germany. She plans a two-week trip to visit family in the autumn. She submits her request in Fern in early summer, her manager approves it, and her team coordinates coverage. Anna still has several days left for the winter holidays.

If you leave Kranich, your accrued but unused vacation will be paid out in your final salary, or you will take it during your notice period, depending on what your manager and People & Culture agree with you. For questions about your vacation balance, write to people@kranich.example.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in Germany is 6 months. During this time, both you and Kranich can assess whether the role and the collaboration are a good fit. The probation period is stated in your employment contract.

During probation, you are entitled to the same vacation, sick leave, and benefits as any other employee. You participate in team meetings, you receive feedback, and you are expected to give feedback as well. Your manager will hold check-ins with you during the probation period to make sure you have what you need to succeed.

If, during probation, either you or Kranich decides that the employment should not continue, the notice period is two weeks, as described in the next section. We encourage open conversations throughout probation. If you have concerns about your role, your team, or your workload, raise them early with your manager or with People & Culture.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, joined Kranich during the summer. His manager scheduled a check-in after the first month and again after the third month. In those conversations, Marek shared that he wanted more exposure to client onboarding. His manager adjusted his project assignments, and Marek felt supported going into the rest of his probation.

At the end of the probation period, your manager will confirm in writing that your employment continues. If your manager believes that the probation period should be extended, that requires your written consent and is only possible in exceptional circumstances and within legal limits.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in Germany is 2 weeks. This applies during and after the probation period, unless a longer statutory notice period applies because of your length of service. German law provides for longer notice periods for employees who have been with the company for several years; those statutory periods apply automatically if they are more favorable to you.

Notice must be given in writing. A notice sent by email is not sufficient under German law. Please send your resignation letter to your manager and to People & Culture, and make sure it is signed by hand.

If Kranich gives you notice, you will receive a written termination letter that states the effective date. During your notice period, you remain entitled to your salary and benefits. You are expected to continue your work and to hand over your tasks in an orderly manner.

Example: Deniz decides after a few years at Kranich that she wants to pursue a new opportunity. She sends a signed resignation letter to her manager and to People & Culture. Because her statutory notice period is longer than the contractual notice period, that longer period applies. Her manager and People & Culture confirm the end date, and Deniz uses her remaining weeks to document her accounts and introduce her colleague to her key clients.

If you have questions about notice periods, your contract, or your final entitlements, contact People & Culture. We want your departure, when it happens, to be as smooth and respectful as your time with us.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in Germany observe 11 days of public holidays in the reference year. These are the public holidays that apply at your workplace. Because public holidays in Germany are regulated by the federal states, the exact days depend on the state in which you work. Kranich follows the public holidays of the German state where your contractual workplace is located.

If a public holiday falls on a weekend, there is no additional day off in lieu. Public holidays do not reduce your vacation entitlement. If you work on a public holiday because your role requires it, your manager will arrange compensatory time off in line with applicable rules.

You can find the list of public holidays for your state on Grove. If you are unsure which state applies to you, check your contract or ask People & Culture.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, works remotely from Spain. Because she is employed in Germany, she observes the public holidays of the German state where her contractual workplace is located. She checks the list on Grove at the start of the year and plans her family visits around those days.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in Germany is 38.0 hours per week. This is the basis for full-time employment. Your individual contract states whether you are full-time or part-time and, if part-time, your agreed weekly hours.

We are a remote-first company, and we trust you to structure your working day in a way that suits your role and your team. Within the framework of German working time law, you are responsible for taking adequate breaks and for not exceeding maximum daily working hours. Your manager is responsible for planning workloads so that you can work healthy hours.

Core collaboration times may be set by your team. If your team agrees on common hours for meetings or stand-ups, please respect them. Outside those times, you are free to organize your work as long as you meet your commitments and your contractual hours.

Overtime is not a regular expectation. If your role occasionally requires more hours, for example during a critical release or a client go-live, your manager will discuss this with you in advance and agree on compensatory time off. Please record your working time in the tool provided by your manager, as required by German law.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, works full-time on a 38.0 hours per week basis. Her team has a daily stand-up at a time that works for colleagues in Germany and Portugal. Anna starts her day a little later, takes a proper lunch break, and finishes in the early evening. When a release requires extra evening work, her manager agrees on time off in lieu the following week.

If you consistently struggle to complete your work within your contractual hours, please speak with your manager. It is a sign that priorities or capacity need adjusting, not that you should work longer hours.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are ill and unable to work, your health comes first. Please inform your manager as soon as you know that you cannot work, ideally before your working day starts. You do not need to provide details about your illness; a short message that you are unwell is enough.

From the first day of illness, your salary continues to be paid by Kranich for a period in line with German law. After that, your health insurance provider pays sick pay. You do not need to do anything special; payroll handles the coordination.

If your illness lasts longer than a few days, you need a medical certificate. The sick-note rules are in POL-007. Please follow that policy carefully and submit your certificate through the process described there.

If you fall ill during your vacation, the days on which you were ill do not count as vacation days, provided you obtain a medical certificate for those days. Please inform your manager and People & Culture promptly so we can adjust your vacation balance in Fern.

Example: Marek wakes up with a fever on a Tuesday morning. He messages his manager before the stand-up and then rests. On Thursday, he is still unwell, so he visits his doctor and obtains a certificate. He follows POL-007 for submitting the certificate. His manager and team cover his client calls, and Marek returns to work the following Monday fully recovered.

When you return from sick leave, you do not need to catch up on missed work immediately. Your manager will help you prioritize. If you are ill frequently or for a long time, we encourage you to talk to People & Culture. We want to support your recovery and, where needed, explore adjustments to your role or working conditions.

## 9. Remote work in Germany

Kranich is remote-first. If you are employed in Germany, you may work from home or from any location within Germany, as long as your role allows it and your manager agrees. Some roles, such as field solutions engineering, require travel to client sites; for those roles, remote work applies between assignments.

Your contractual workplace is stated in your contract. If you work remotely from home on a regular basis, we ask you to confirm your home address in Fern and to ensure that your home workspace meets basic requirements: a quiet space, a reliable internet connection, and an ergonomic setup. Kranich provides the equipment you need, such as a laptop, monitor, keyboard, and headset.

If you wish to work from a location outside Germany for a short period, please discuss this with your manager and with People & Culture. Working from another country can have tax and social security implications, and we need to review those before you go. Working from abroad without prior approval is not permitted.

If you work in the Berlin office or visit it occasionally, you can book a desk or a meeting room through Perch. The office in Ritterstraße has space for focused work, meetings, and socializing. We also have an office in Lisbon at Rua do Alecrim; if you travel there for work, you can book a desk there as well.

Example: Deniz lives in Berlin and works from home most days. Twice a week, she books a desk at the Ritterstraße office through Perch to collaborate with colleagues in person. When her team holds a planning workshop, she books a meeting room for the afternoon. The rest of the week, she works from her home office.

If you have questions about remote work, equipment, or your workspace, contact your manager or Workplace & Operations. We want you to work in a way that is productive and sustainable for you.

## 10. Benefits in Germany

We offer a range of benefits to support your health, your financial well-being, and your work-life balance. Your benefits are described in this section and in the policies referenced here. If a benefit is not listed, it does not exist.

You receive a monthly salary as stated in your contract. In addition, Kranich contributes to your statutory pension, health, nursing care, and unemployment insurance as required by German law. The employee and employer contributions are shown on your payslip.

We provide a company pension scheme through deferred compensation. You can choose to divert a portion of your gross salary into this scheme, and Kranich adds an employer subsidy in line with the current regulations. Details are available in the policy on Grove.

For your daily work, Kranich provides the equipment you need. You also receive a budget for ergonomic office equipment for your home workspace. You can use this budget to buy items such as an office chair or a footrest; the process is described on Grove.

We offer access to an employee assistance program that provides confidential counseling for personal or professional challenges. You can use this service for yourself or for family members who live in your household. Contact information is on Grove.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, recently set up her home office. She used her ergonomic equipment budget to buy a supportive chair. She also enrolled in the company pension scheme through deferred compensation, and she knows she can call the employee assistance program if she ever needs someone to talk to.

For a full overview of your benefits, including how to enroll or change your choices, please refer to the benefits page on Grove. If you have questions, write to people@kranich.example.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly to your German bank account. Payday is the last working day of the month. If that day is a weekend or public holiday, payment is made on the preceding working day. You will receive your payslip electronically in the payroll system; access details are sent to you on onboarding.

Kranich handles payroll in compliance with German tax and social security law. Your income tax, solidarity surcharge if applicable, and social security contributions are deducted from your gross salary and remitted to the relevant authorities. Your tax class and other payroll-relevant data are based on the information you provide, and you can update your tax class through your tax office; please inform People & Culture of any changes.

If you have a church tax obligation, it will be deducted as well. If you change your religious affiliation or your tax class, please update your details with the tax office and send the new information to People & Culture so payroll can adjust.

Your annual tax certificate will be made available to you in the payroll system by the end of the following year, in line with statutory deadlines. You can use it for your income tax return.

If you have questions about your payslip, deductions, or tax matters, please contact People & Culture or the payroll contact listed in the next section. We cannot give individual tax advice, but we can explain how your payroll works and point you to resources.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, is employed in Germany and receives her salary in euros to her German bank account. When she changes her tax class after her marriage, she updates her details with the tax office and sends the new certificate to People & Culture. Her next payslip reflects the change.

If you are unsure whom to contact, ask your manager or write to people@kranich.example. We will route your request to the right team.

Example: Deniz notices that her laptop is behaving strangely and suspects it may be infected. She immediately writes to security@kranich.example. The Information Security team responds promptly, guides her through the next steps, and IT help provides her with a replacement device while hers is checked.

We hope this handbook gives you a clear picture of working at Kranich in Germany. Your manager and People & Culture are always happy to discuss anything that is unclear. Thank you for being part of Kranich Software GmbH.
