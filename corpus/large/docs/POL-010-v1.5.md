# Internal Data Protection Policy

Superseded by version 2.0.

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH is committed to protecting the personal data of our employees, applicants, customers, and business partners. This policy explains how we handle personal data in the course of our daily work, what we do to keep it safe, and what you should do if you have a question or concern.

This policy applies to everyone who works with Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of whether you are employed in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom, and regardless of whether you work from our offices in Berlin or Lisbon, from home, or on the road. It covers personal data in any form — on paper, on your laptop, in our internal tools, or in conversations.

We all have a role to play in protecting personal data. If you are unsure whether a particular use of personal data is allowed, ask before you act.

## 2. Personal data we handle

In our work, we handle personal data of several groups. This section gives you an overview of the categories and the reasons we process them.

**Employee data.** We process personal data about our people for employment administration, payroll, benefits, performance management, and internal communication. This includes contact details, emergency contacts, bank account information for salary payments, and records related to leave and absence. Our HRIS Fern holds the core employee records, while Ledgerly processes expense and travel information.

**Applicant data.** When you apply for a role at Kranich Software GmbH, we collect the information you provide in your application, such as your CV, cover letter, references, and any correspondence you exchange with our recruiting team. We process this data to evaluate your suitability for the role and to manage the hiring process.

**Customer and partner data.** Because we build route-optimization software for logistics operators, we handle personal data that our customers share with us. This may include contact details of their dispatchers and drivers, user account information for the Kranich Route Cloud, and operational data that contains names or other identifiers. We process this data to provide our services, support our customers, and improve our products.

**Visitor and office data.** At our Ritterstraße and Rua do Alecrim offices, we may collect basic information about visitors for security and building access purposes. Perch, our desk and meeting-room booking tool, also holds data about who uses which spaces.

You should treat all personal data you encounter with care. Only access personal data that you need for your role. Do not browse, copy, or share personal data out of curiosity or convenience.

**Example:** Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, receives a support call from a logistics dispatcher. Deniz only looks up the customer account details needed to resolve the issue and does not open the driver records of unrelated customers.

## 3. Retention

We do not keep personal data longer than necessary. Retention periods depend on the category of data and the legal or business reason for holding it. When a retention period has passed, the data is deleted or anonymised in line with our deletion procedures.

**Employee data.** Employee records are kept for as long as the employment relationship lasts. After an employee leaves Kranich Software GmbH, certain records are retained to meet legal obligations, while others are deleted promptly.

**Applicant data.** Applicant data is deleted 8 years after the hiring decision. This period applies to both successful and unsuccessful applicants, and it allows us to defend against legal claims and to maintain consistent records. After that period, we delete or anonymise the application materials.

**Customer data.** Customer data is retained for the duration of the customer contract and for a reasonable period afterwards to handle any disputes or follow-up requests. Data stored in backups is deleted according to our backup retention schedule, which aligns with the underlying business need.

**Example:** Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, receives a request from a former applicant asking whether we still hold their application. Lea checks the records and confirms that the retention period for that application has not yet ended, so the data is still stored securely.

If you are unsure how long a particular record should be kept, contact Legal or your manager before deleting or archiving anything.

## 4. Requests from data subjects

People whose personal data we hold have rights under applicable data protection law. These rights include the right to access their data, the right to request correction, the right to request deletion, and the right to object to certain processing.

If you receive a request from an employee, applicant, customer, or any other person asking about their personal data, do not ignore it. Forward the request promptly to Legal and to the security team at security@kranich.example. Do not respond to the request yourself unless you have been asked to do so.

Legal will verify the identity of the requester before responding. This is important to prevent personal data from being disclosed to someone who is not entitled to receive it.

**Example:** Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, receives an email from a customer contact asking for a copy of all personal data the customer holds about them. Marek does not reply with any data. Instead, he forwards the request to Legal and security@kranich.example on the same day.

When you handle a data subject request, keep a record of what you did. Do not discuss the request with colleagues who do not need to know about it.

## 5. Working with processors

From time to time, Kranich Software GmbH engages external companies that process personal data on our behalf. These companies are called processors. Examples include our cloud infrastructure providers, our internal tool vendors such as Fern and Ledgerly, and other service providers that support our business.

Before we engage a processor, Legal and the Information Security team review the processor's data protection practices. We only work with processors that offer sufficient guarantees to protect personal data. Each engagement is governed by a data processing agreement that sets out the scope of processing, the security measures required, and the obligations of the processor.

As an employee, you must not sign up for or procure a new tool or service that will process personal data without going through this review. If you need a new tool for your team, raise it with your manager and with Platform & Infrastructure. They will coordinate with Legal and Information Security.

You also have a duty to use approved tools correctly. Store personal data only in the systems we provide, such as our internal tools and approved cloud storage. Do not send personal data to your personal email account, do not store it on personal devices, and do not use unapproved file-sharing services.

**Example:** Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, wants to use a new survey tool to collect feedback from customer dispatchers. Before she signs up, she contacts Platform & Infrastructure, who check with Legal whether the tool can be used under a data processing agreement.

If you become aware that a processor has lost data or has experienced a security incident, report it immediately to security@kranich.example.

## 6. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, about a specific data handling situation, or about your obligations under data protection law, please reach out. The first point of contact is your manager or the People & Culture team at people@kranich.example. For technical or security-related questions, contact it-help@kranich.example. For questions about the legal interpretation of this policy, contact Legal.

You can also raise concerns confidentially. No one will be penalised for asking a question or for reporting a potential issue in good faith.

Thank you for taking the time to read this policy and for doing your part to protect the personal data we handle every day.
