# External Communications & Social Media

*Superseded by version 2.0.*

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software builds trust with customers, partners, and the public through clear and honest communication. This policy helps you understand when you speak for Kranich, how to handle personal posts that mention your work, and what needs approval before it goes out.

We want you to share your expertise and enthusiasm. At the same time, we must protect confidential information, respect our customers, and keep a consistent voice. This policy applies to everyone at Kranich, whether you work from the Ritterstraße office, the Rua do Alecrim office, or remotely from your home country.

The principles are simple: be accurate, be respectful, and be clear about who you are. When in doubt, ask before you post.

## 2. Speaking for Kranich

You speak for Kranich when you are authorized to represent the company, for example in a press interview, at a conference, in a webinar, or on an official Kranich social media account. Only people with explicit approval from the Go-to-Market team may speak in this capacity.

If you are not authorized, do not imply that you are. Do not use the Kranich logo, a company email signature, or a title such as "spokesperson" in public communications without approval. When you introduce yourself at an external event, your job title is fine; a statement of company positions is not.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, is asked by a trade publication for a comment on route-optimization trends. She is happy to help, but she checks with the Go-to-Market team first. They arrange for a product manager to speak instead, and Deniz shares the published article on her personal profile afterwards.

Confidential information stays internal. This includes unreleased product features, customer data, financial results, security incidents, and internal discussions. If you are unsure whether something is public, treat it as confidential and ask your manager or the Go-to-Market team.

When you speak for Kranich, stick to your area of expertise. If a question touches on another team's work, such as pricing or security, offer to connect the questioner with the right person rather than guessing.

## 3. Personal accounts

You are welcome to maintain personal social media accounts and to discuss your work in general terms. Personal accounts are yours, but posts about Kranich reflect on the company. Follow these guidelines.

Be transparent about your role. When you post about your work at Kranich, it is good practice to mention that you work here. You do not need to add a disclaimer to every post, but you should not hide your affiliation when the topic relates to your job.

Do not share confidential information, even in a private group or a direct message. Assume that anything you write can become public. This includes screenshots of internal tools such as Perch, Ledgerly, or Fern, and details about customer implementations.

Be respectful of customers, partners, and colleagues. Do not criticize them publicly, even if you disagree with a decision. If you have a concern, raise it internally through the usual channels. Public complaints about work matters are never appropriate.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to write a blog post about a technical challenge she solved recently. She describes the general approach without naming the customer or showing code from our repository. She mentions that she works at Kranich and keeps the post at a level that does not reveal internal architecture. This is fine without further approval.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, sees a customer post that contains inaccurate information about our product. He does not correct the customer in public. Instead, he notes the issue and shares it with the Customer Success team internally.

If you are active on social media in a professional field, consider adding a line to your profile that your views are your own. This is not required, but it helps avoid confusion.

## 4. Approval of public content

Some public content requires approval before it is published. The Go-to-Market team coordinates all official communications, including press releases, blog posts on the Kranich website, social media posts on official Kranich accounts, and speaking proposals made in the company's name.

For personal content, approval is only needed in specific cases. Get approval before you:

- share details of a customer project that could identify the customer,
- publish screenshots or recordings from internal meetings or tools,
- discuss unreleased product plans or roadmap items,
- respond to media inquiries about Kranich, or
- post on behalf of the company, for example by managing a Kranich account.

When you request approval, send a draft or a summary of what you plan to publish to the Go-to-Market team. They will review for accuracy, confidentiality, and tone. Turnaround is usually prompt. If you are planning something time-sensitive, such as a conference talk, start the review early.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, wants to present at an industry meetup about how we think about fleet data. She prepares a draft outline and sends it to Go-to-Market. They suggest a few adjustments to keep customer specifics generic, and Sofia incorporates the feedback before submitting her talk proposal.

Do not create official-looking Kranich accounts on social platforms without approval. If you see an unofficial account using the Kranich name or logo, report it to the Go-to-Market team or to security@kranich.example.

When you speak at an event in a personal capacity, you do not need approval for the content of your talk. However, if your biography mentions Kranich or if the event materials associate you with the company, let Go-to-Market know in advance. They can help ensure the description is accurate.

## 5. Questions

If you are unsure whether something needs approval, ask. The Go-to-Market team is happy to advise on external communications. For questions about confidentiality or security, contact security@kranich.example. For general questions about this policy, reach out to your manager or to the Go-to-Market team.

When you receive an unexpected media inquiry, you do not need to answer on the spot. A simple "I will connect you with the right person" is enough, then forward the inquiry to Go-to-Market. Do not speculate or offer off-the-record comments.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, is contacted by a journalist who found her on LinkedIn and asks about working culture at Kranich. Lea does not respond directly. She forwards the inquiry to Go-to-Market, who decides whether to engage and who should respond.

Remember that this policy complements other internal documents. For example, POL-004 covers information security expectations, and the Grove policy library contains related guidance on data protection and code of conduct. When in doubt, the stricter rule applies.

Thank you for helping Kranich communicate with clarity and care.
