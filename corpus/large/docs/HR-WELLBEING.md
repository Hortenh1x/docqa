# Wellbeing Resources

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we believe that sustainable performance starts with sustainable people. This document brings together the wellbeing resources available to you, so you know what is there, who to turn to, and how to access support when you need it.

Wellbeing at Kranich covers your mental, physical, and social health. It includes support for everyday stressors, as well as for more difficult moments in your personal or professional life. We do not expect you to navigate these moments alone.

This policy complements, and does not replace, other provisions in our policy library. Where a topic touches on leave, health, or workplace adjustments, the relevant policy takes precedence — see §3 for references.

## 2. Who this is for

This document applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of your location, role, or working arrangement. Whether you work from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from your home in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom, the resources described here are available to you.

Some resources are delivered locally and may differ slightly by country. Where that is the case, we will point you to the local contact or provider. The core commitment — that you have access to confidential, quality support — is the same everywhere.

Managers and team leads will find this document useful as a reference when supporting team members. If you are unsure how to help a colleague, start by pointing them to these resources and to the relevant People & Culture contact.

## 3. Process

### 3.1 Confidential support line

All employees have access to an external, confidential support service. This service is independent of Kranich and is available for personal or work-related concerns. You can speak with a professional counselor about stress, anxiety, relationship difficulties, or any other issue that is affecting you.

Your conversations with this service are private. The provider does not share any details with Kranich. The only exception is where there is an immediate risk of serious harm, and even then, the provider will follow professional standards of confidentiality.

To access the service, visit the Wellbeing page on Grove. There you will find the contact details for your country, as well as information on how to book a session.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, was feeling overwhelmed by a difficult client period. He called the support line one evening and spoke with a counselor. The conversation helped him clarify what was bothering him and gave him practical steps for the following week. Nobody at Kranich knows about the call.

### 3.2 Manager conversations

Your manager is there to support you, but they are not a therapist. For day-to-day wellbeing concerns related to your work — workload, priorities, collaboration, or team dynamics — please talk to your manager openly. They can help adjust your work, involve others, or escalate to People & Culture when needed.

If you do not feel comfortable speaking with your manager, you can reach out to any member of the People & Culture team, or to your HR business partner. You can also use the people@kranich.example alias and we will route your message confidentially.

### 3.3 People & Culture support

The People & Culture team can help with more structural wellbeing matters. This includes:

- Workplace adjustments, such as ergonomic equipment or flexible scheduling, where a health condition requires it.
- Navigating sick leave and return-to-work processes.
- Connecting you with local resources, such as health services or support groups in your country.
- Supporting managers who want to improve team wellbeing.

Reach out to Lea or another member of the People & Culture team directly, or write to people@kranich.example.

### 3.4 Peer support

We encourage a culture where colleagues look out for one another. This is not a formal process, but a simple practice: check in on your teammates, notice when someone seems off, and ask how they are doing.

Some teams have adopted a short wellbeing check-in at the start of their regular meetings. This is entirely voluntary. If you prefer not to share, that is respected.

### 3.5 Emergency situations

If you are in immediate danger or experiencing a crisis, please contact local emergency services first. The confidential support line also has crisis protocols and can guide you to appropriate help.

For urgent work-related concerns outside of business hours, contact your manager or, if the matter involves safety or security, the relevant internal team. For matters of information security, contact security@kranich.example.

## 4. Timelines

We aim to make access to wellbeing resources prompt and uncomplicated.

- For the confidential support line: you can typically book a session within a few days of your first contact. In urgent cases, the provider will prioritize you.
- For People & Culture support: we will acknowledge your message within a few business days and agree on next steps together. If your matter is urgent, say so in your message and we will respond sooner.
- For manager conversations: please do not wait for a scheduled one-on-one if you are struggling. Send a message and ask for time; managers are expected to make room for these conversations promptly.

There is no waiting period for any of the resources in this document. You can use them from your first day of employment.

If you have requested a workplace adjustment, the timeline will depend on the nature of the adjustment and on local procurement or service arrangements. We will keep you informed throughout.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this document or about wellbeing at Kranich in general, please reach out:

- By email: people@kranich.example
- In person or virtually: any member of the People & Culture team
- Via Grove: the Wellbeing page contains up-to-date contacts, provider details, and local information for each country.

If you have feedback on how we can improve our wellbeing offering, we would love to hear it. You can share it anonymously through the feedback form on Grove, or directly with your manager or People & Culture.

For questions about how your personal data is handled in relation to wellbeing services, please refer to our data protection guidance on Grove, or contact the relevant team.

We hope you find these resources useful. Looking after yourself is not a sign of weakness — it is a prerequisite for doing your best work and living a full life. We are here to support you.
