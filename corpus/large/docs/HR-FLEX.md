# Flexible Working Requests

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we know that people do their best work when they can shape their day around their lives. Flexible working is part of how we work, and this policy explains how you can request a change to your working pattern, when you can expect an answer, and what happens after a request is approved.

Flexible working can mean many things: adjusting your start and end times, working from home on certain days, or changing the distribution of your hours across the week. It is not limited to parents or carers — anyone can ask, whatever their reason. We trust you to make a case that works for you and for your team.

## 2. Who this is for

This policy applies to all employees in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom, regardless of where you usually sit — at the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from home.

Some teams, such as Routing Core or Platform & Infrastructure, have on-call or release responsibilities. Others, like Customer Success, need to cover client hours. Your role and your team's commitments will shape what is possible, but they should not stop you from asking.

If you are a manager, you will be involved in the conversation as a supporter, not a gatekeeper. If you are an employee, this policy is written for you.

## 3. Process

**Before you ask.** Think about what you need and why. Consider how your proposed pattern would affect your team, your clients, and your own work. You do not need to prepare a formal business case, but a few clear sentences about the change and its impact help everyone move faster.

**Making the request.** Send your request in writing to your manager, copying the People & Culture team. Use the request template on Grove, our intranet, which asks for:

- the change you are asking for
- the reason, in your own words
- a proposed start date
- how long you expect the change to last, if it is not permanent

You do not need to share medical or personal details. A simple explanation is enough.

**The conversation.** Your manager will invite you to a meeting to discuss the request. This should feel like a dialogue, not an interview. You may bring a colleague from People & Culture if you prefer. In the meeting, you will talk through the practical side: coverage, handovers, communication rhythms, and how your team will stay connected.

Your manager may consult your team lead or the Head of Workplace & Operations, Rui Almeida, when the request touches office space or equipment. For requests that affect security or data handling, the Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar, may be asked for input.

**The decision.** After the conversation, your manager will respond in writing. There are three possible outcomes:

- **Approved.** The change takes effect from the date you agreed.
- **Approved with adjustments.** Your manager agrees in principle but proposes small changes — for example, shifting your start time by an hour rather than two, to cover a team meeting.
- **Declined.** This will only happen when there is a clear operational reason, and the response will explain why.

If your request is declined, you may ask for a review by the VP People & Culture, Aylin Demir. Send your request for review within a few days of the decision, and the VP will look at the case personally.

**Trial period.** For larger changes, we may agree on a trial period first. This gives you and your team time to see how the new pattern works in practice. At the end of the trial, you and your manager will check in and decide whether to make the change permanent, adjust it, or end it.

**Changing your mind.** You can ask to revert to your previous working pattern at any time. The same process applies, though the conversation is usually quicker.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wanted to shift her working day earlier so she could pick up her daughter from school. She sent a request through the template, met with her manager, and they agreed on a trial period of a few weeks. After the trial, her manager confirmed the change was permanent.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, asked to work from home every Friday. Her manager explained that Fridays were a peak day for client onboarding calls. They agreed instead that Deniz would work from home on Mondays and Thursdays, when her team had fewer client-facing commitments.

## 4. Timelines

We aim to handle requests promptly. From the day you send your request, you can expect:

- a meeting with your manager within a few days
- a written decision within a few days after that conversation

If we need to consult another team — for example, Platform & Infrastructure for equipment or Information Security for data access — the timeline may stretch slightly. We will keep you informed if that happens.

For trial periods, the length will be agreed between you and your manager and set out in the decision letter. There is no fixed duration, and either side can propose ending the trial early if it is clearly not working.

If your circumstances change while a request is under review — for example, a new personal commitment — let your manager know. It will not disadvantage you, but it may affect the conversation.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager or your People Partner. You can also reach the People & Culture team directly at people@kranich.example.

For questions about how flexible working interacts with your employment contract or local law in your country, People & Culture will point you to the right resource. Related policies are available on Grove, including POL-004 on remote work arrangements.

If you experience any difficulty in making a request, or you feel the process was not followed fairly, please contact Aylin Demir directly. She is the owner of this policy and will take your concern seriously.
