# Board Memo 1 — 2025

Access: Leadership only

## 1. Summary

We closed the previous year in a strong position. Kranich Route Cloud continues to win mid-size logistics operators across our core European markets, and our focus on product reliability and customer outcomes is paying off. This memo summarises where we stand on growth, cash, and the risks we are watching most closely, and it lays out the specific asks we bring to the board.

## 2. Growth

ARR grew 15% year over year. This performance was driven by steady expansion within our existing customer base rather than by a small number of large new logos, which is exactly the mix we have been aiming for. Our Fleet Insights module, in particular, saw healthy adoption among customers who started with Routing Core and then expanded into adjacent workflows.

From a geographic perspective, growth was broad-based. Our German home market remains the largest contributor, while Poland and the United Kingdom both grew at a healthy clip. The Iberian market, served largely from our Lisbon office, is still smaller but is building momentum. We see no single-market concentration risk emerging at this stage.

On the product side, we shipped several releases that improved route quality and reduced manual intervention for dispatchers. Customer feedback on these releases has been positive, and early indicators suggest that the improvements are supporting both retention and expansion conversations. We will continue to invest in the areas where customers tell us they feel the most pain: real-time visibility, exception handling, and integration with their existing telematics and ERP systems.

## 3. Cash

Cash runway stands at 32 months. We ended the previous fiscal year with a comfortable cash position, and our burn rate remains moderate relative to our revenue base. We have not needed to draw on any external financing, and we do not anticipate doing so within the planning horizon covered by this runway.

Our spending discipline has held. The largest cost lines remain personnel and cloud infrastructure, both of which scale with the business. We have kept marketing spend efficient by leaning on customer referrals and on the field work done by our solutions engineers, who are often the difference in competitive deals.

We expect operating expenses to rise modestly in the coming year as we hire into the Customer Success and Platform & Infrastructure teams. These hires are deliberate: they support the growth we are seeing and protect the quality of the customer experience. Even with this increase, our runway position gives us ample room to execute without short-term pressure.

## 4. Risks

We see three risks worth the board's attention.

First, competitive intensity. The route-optimisation space is attracting new entrants, and some larger platforms are bundling optimisation features into broader offerings. We are not seeing price erosion yet, but we are monitoring deal cycles closely. Our defence is depth: we solve the hard operational problems that generic tools do not, and our customers tell us that switching costs are real.

Second, talent. We are remote-first across several countries, which widens our hiring pool but also means we compete for senior engineering and customer-facing talent against larger, well-funded companies. Retention has been good so far, and our internal engagement survey reflects that. Still, we need to keep investing in career development and in the quality of our management, particularly as our engineering organisation grows.

Third, operational resilience. We depend on a small number of critical cloud providers, and our customers depend on us for daily operations. A prolonged outage at a provider, or a significant regional incident, would directly affect our customers' ability to run their fleets. We have invested in redundancy and in our incident-response processes, and we will run additional resilience exercises in the coming quarters. Related considerations are documented in our security policies, including POL-004.

## 5. Asks

We bring three asks to the board.

First, we ask for endorsement of our hiring plan for the current year, focused on Customer Success and Platform & Infrastructure. This is a modest increase and is fully covered by our cash position.

Second, we ask the board to review and endorse the updated compensation framework that People & Culture is preparing. The framework is designed to keep us competitive in the markets where we hire, and it will be shared with the board before any communication to employees. Details on the underlying salary bands are in FIN-RATES-2026.

Third, we ask for guidance on our capital-allocation priorities for the second half of the year. We do not need external capital, but we want the board's view on whether we should prioritise product investment, geographic expansion, or a more aggressive go-to-market push once the current hiring round is complete.

We are happy to take questions on any of the above and will provide deeper operational data in the regular board pack.
