# Working Time & Overtime Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH values the wellbeing of its employees and the quality of the work we deliver together. This policy explains how we think about working time, overtime, and time off so that everyone can plan their days with clarity and trust.

We are a remote-first company with teams in several countries. Local laws and employment contracts remain the foundation. Where this policy is more generous than local rules, the policy applies. Where local rules require more, local rules apply. If you are ever unsure, reach out to People & Culture.

This policy applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH and its offices in Berlin and Lisbon. It covers regular working time, overtime, and how we record time. It does not replace your employment contract or any collective agreement that may apply to you.

## 2. Working time

Working time means the hours you spend performing your duties for Kranich Software GmbH. It includes time spent on internal meetings, client calls, project work, and other activities you do as part of your role. It does not include breaks, personal time, or voluntary activities outside your responsibilities.

Your regular working time is agreed in your employment contract and reflects your role, your location, and your team. Most of us work a standard full-time schedule, but part-time arrangements exist and are respected. We trust you to structure your day in a way that suits your work and your life, as long as you meet your commitments and remain available for collaboration during core hours relevant to your team.

Working time may not exceed 44 hours in any week. This is an upper limit, not a target. We expect most weeks to be shorter. If you find yourself regularly approaching this limit, please speak with your manager. It may be a sign that your workload needs adjustment.

We also encourage you to take breaks during the day. A short pause helps you stay focused and productive. Please make sure you step away from your screen regularly, especially when working remotely.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, usually works about eight hours a day. During a release week, she works a bit longer on two days. She still stays well below the weekly limit and takes a longer lunch break the following Friday.

## 3. Overtime and compensation

Overtime is work performed beyond your regular contracted hours, at the request of your manager or with their approval. Occasional overtime can happen in our business, for example around a product launch or when a client has an urgent issue. It should remain the exception, not the rule.

You are never expected to work overtime regularly. If overtime becomes frequent, your manager should review the situation and adjust priorities or resources. Working long hours over a sustained period is not good for you, your team, or the quality of our work.

Overtime is compensated with time off within 10 weeks. This means that when you work extra hours, you should take an equivalent amount of time off soon after, so that the extra effort is balanced in a timely way. Time off is taken in agreement with your manager and should not disrupt your team or your clients.

You do not need to record overtime in any special system. Simply inform your manager when you work extra hours, and agree together when you will take the corresponding time off. If you prefer, you can note it in your calendar or in a message to your manager.

Time off in lieu is meant to be used. Please do not let it accumulate. If you have outstanding time off and find it difficult to plan, your manager will help you find a good moment. In any case, time off should be taken within the period stated above.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, works a few extra hours one evening to help a client through a configuration issue. She mentions it to her manager the next day, and they agree that she will leave early on Friday of the following week.

Overtime is not compensated in money. We believe that time off is the most valuable form of compensation, because it supports your recovery and your life outside work. If your role has special arrangements regarding overtime, these are described in your contract.

## 4. Time tracking

We track working time to comply with legal obligations and to protect your wellbeing. Time tracking is not about surveillance. It is a tool for transparency, for you and for the company.

You are responsible for recording your working time in the tool provided by Platform & Infrastructure. Please record your hours at least once a week. The record should reflect the actual time you worked, including overtime. It does not need to be precise to the minute, but it should be honest and complete.

Your manager can see your time records. People & Culture and Finance may also access them for legal and payroll purposes. Nobody else sees your records unless there is a specific reason, such as an audit or an investigation.

If you notice an error in your time records, please correct it promptly. If you have questions about how to record time, contact it-help@kranich.example. If you have questions about this policy, contact people@kranich.example.

We understand that remote work can make time tracking feel unusual. Please treat it as a simple administrative task, like submitting an expense in Ledgerly or updating your leave in Fern. It takes a few minutes each week and helps everyone stay aligned.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, travels to a client site for two days. He records his travel time and his on-site hours in the tracking tool when he returns, and his manager reviews the record at the end of the week.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, our people operations specialist, is happy to help you understand how the policy applies to your situation.

If you believe your workload is consistently too high, or that you are working overtime too often, please talk to your manager or to People & Culture. We take these concerns seriously and will work with you to find a solution.

If you have concerns about time tracking or about how your time records are used, you can contact security@kranich.example. Priya Nayar, our Head of Information Security, oversees data protection matters.

For questions about your employment contract or local legal requirements, please refer to your contract or contact People & Culture. Related policies can be found in Grove, our intranet and policy library. You may also find POL-004 helpful for matters related to leave and absence.

This policy is reviewed regularly and updated when needed. The version in Grove is always the current one. Thank you for taking the time to read it, and please reach out if anything is unclear.
