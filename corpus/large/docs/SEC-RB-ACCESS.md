# Access Requests Runbook

## 1. Purpose

This runbook explains how employees request access to Kranich Route Cloud and the internal tools we rely on every day. It covers new access, changes to existing access, and what happens when an account is locked.

We keep access management simple and transparent. Whether you are joining Kranich, changing teams, or taking on a new responsibility, this runbook tells you what to do. If something is unclear, reach out to it-help@kranich.example and we will point you in the right direction.

Our goal is to make sure everyone has the access they need to do their work — and no more. This keeps our customers' data safe and our systems easy to manage.

## 2. Requesting access

Access requests are handled through a short form on the Grove intranet. You will find the link under "IT & Security" in the policy library. The form asks for your name, your team, the system you need access to, and a brief reason.

Most access is granted by your manager or a team lead. For example, when Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joins a new project in Routing Core, her engineering manager approves the request for the relevant repositories and environments. For customer-facing systems, the Customer Success team follows the same process.

If you need access to a system that is not listed on the form, select "Other" and describe what you need. The Information Security team will review your request and follow up with you directly.

Access changes follow the same path. When you move teams — say, from Fleet Insights to Platform & Infrastructure — submit a new request for the systems you need in your new role. Your old access is removed as part of the change, so you do not need to ask for that separately.

When you leave Kranich or go on a long absence, your manager notifies People & Culture, and access is revoked promptly.

## 3. Locked accounts

Accounts can become locked after several failed sign-in attempts. This happens to everyone at some point, and it is not a reflection on you.

If your account is locked, do not keep trying to sign in. Instead, contact it-help@kranich.example and let us know which system you are trying to reach. IT resets a locked account within 30 minutes during business hours. Outside business hours, the reset happens the next working day.

If you are locked out of 1Password and cannot reach the IT help alias, ask a teammate to submit a request on your behalf. They should mention your name and the system in the request.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, was locked out of Ledgerly while submitting a travel expense on a Friday evening. She waited until Monday morning, contacted it-help@kranich.example, and her account was reset within 30 minutes.

## 4. Reviews

Access reviews happen on a regular schedule. Information Security coordinates these reviews with each team lead. During a review, the team lead goes through the list of people who have access to their team's systems and confirms that each person still needs it.

You do not need to do anything for a routine review. Your manager will reach out if there is a question about your access.

Reviews also happen when someone changes roles. If you move from one team to another, your old team lead confirms that your previous access is no longer needed, and your new team lead confirms what you should receive. This happens in the background; you only need to submit the access request described in §2.

For systems with sensitive customer data, reviews are more frequent. The Fleet Insights team, for example, reviews access to customer telemetry data every quarter. Your team lead will let you know if your team follows a similar pattern.

If you notice that a former colleague still appears in a system, or that you have access to something you no longer use, let Information Security know. We would rather remove access too early than too late.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this runbook, start with the FAQ on Grove. It covers common scenarios, such as requesting access for a contractor or handling access for someone who is on parental leave.

If you do not find an answer, write to it-help@kranich.example for technical questions or security@kranich.example for questions about access policy. For questions about how access relates to your role or team, speak with your manager first.

People & Culture can help with questions about access in the context of employment changes. Contact people@kranich.example if you are unsure whether your situation counts as a role change or a new role.

We are happy to help. Access management is a shared responsibility, and we appreciate you taking the time to read this runbook. If you spot something that could be clearer, let us know — the runbook is reviewed and updated regularly, and your feedback makes it better for everyone.
