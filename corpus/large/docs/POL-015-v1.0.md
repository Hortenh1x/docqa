# Internal Mobility Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we believe that growing within the company is one of the best ways to build a long and fulfilling career. People change, interests evolve, and the needs of our teams shift over time. Internal mobility gives you the chance to explore new challenges, apply your skills in a different context, and bring fresh perspectives to the work we do together.

This policy describes how you can move to a new role at Kranich — whether that means joining a different team, taking on new responsibilities in the same team, or changing your focus area entirely. We want these moves to be transparent, fair, and supportive for everyone involved.

Internal mobility is not just about filling an open position. It is also about recognising that your career is yours to shape. Your manager, your People Partner, and the People & Culture team are here to help you think through what might be next for you, even if you are not ready to make a move today.

## 2. Eligibility

Most employees are eligible to apply for an internal transfer. We want to make it easy for you to find the right fit, and we also want to make sure that moves happen at a time when they make sense for you and for the company.

Internal transfers require at least 15 months in the current role. This applies to your time in your specific position, not your overall time at Kranich. If you joined the company recently but moved into a new role, the clock starts when you began that role.

There are a few exceptions. If you are moving into a role that is a clear promotion within the same team and your manager supports the move, the 15 months requirement can be waived with approval from the VP People & Culture. The same applies if your current role is being eliminated or significantly changed as part of a reorganisation. In those situations, we will work with you to find a good next step as soon as possible.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined the Routing Core team two years ago. She recently became interested in the work done by the Fleet Insights team. Because she has been in her current role for more than 15 months, she is eligible to apply for an open position there.

If you are on a probationary period, you are not eligible to apply for an internal transfer until that period has ended. If you have an active performance improvement plan, please speak with your manager or a member of the People & Culture team before applying; we want to make sure a move is the right step for you at this time.

## 3. How to apply

Internal opportunities are posted on Grove, our intranet and policy library. You will find a dedicated section for internal roles, alongside any open positions shared by the Go-to-Market team for client-facing roles.

When you see a role that interests you, the first step is to have a conversation with your current manager. Tell them what you are considering and why. This is not a request for permission — it is a chance to have an open discussion about your development and to plan for a smooth transition if you are successful. We understand that this conversation can feel awkward, especially if you are curious about a role but not sure yet. In that case, you can reach out to your People Partner or to people@kranich.example for a confidential conversation first.

Once you have spoken with your manager, submit your application through the internal posting on Grove. Your application should include a short note about why you are interested in the role and what you would bring to it. You do not need to prepare a formal CV, although you may share one if you prefer.

The hiring manager for the internal role will review your application. If they would like to move forward, they will invite you to an interview. The interview process for internal candidates is typically lighter than for external candidates, because we already know your work and your values. It usually involves a conversation with the hiring manager and, depending on the role, one or two other team members.

Your current manager will be kept informed throughout the process. We ask hiring managers to update your manager after each stage, so that no one is surprised by the outcome. If you prefer that your manager not be involved at a particular stage, please let the hiring manager or your People Partner know; we will do our best to accommodate your request while being transparent with the teams involved.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, saw an internal role on the Platform & Infrastructure team. She spoke with her manager first, then applied through Grove. The hiring manager invited her for a conversation with the team. Deniz's manager was updated after each step, so when Deniz received an offer, her manager was already prepared to support the transition.

If you are not selected for a role, you will receive feedback from the hiring manager. This is meant to help you understand what you can develop and to encourage you to try again in the future. Not being selected for one role does not affect your standing in your current position.

## 4. Transition

Once you accept an internal offer, we want the transition to be smooth for you, your current team, and your new team. A thoughtful handover protects the work of your current team and gives you a clean start in your new role.

Your current manager and your new manager will agree on a start date. The typical notice period for an internal move is a few weeks, but we aim to be flexible. The exact timing will depend on the nature of your current work, any ongoing projects, and the needs of your new team. We ask that you and both managers work together to find a date that works for everyone.

During the handover period, you should focus on documenting your work and supporting your colleagues. This might include updating documentation, walking a teammate through your ongoing tasks, or wrapping up smaller pieces of work. Your manager will help you decide what is most important.

Your new manager will prepare for your arrival. This includes setting up your access to the tools you will need, introducing you to the team, and helping you understand your new responsibilities. You will also be assigned a buddy in your new team if you do not already know your colleagues well.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, moved from the Customer Success team to the Go-to-Market team. He spent two weeks handing over his client accounts to a colleague, then started in his new role with a clear set of goals and a buddy who helped him learn the ropes.

If your move involves a change in location — for example, from the Berlin office to the Lisbon office — please speak with your new manager and the People & Culture team early in the process. We will help you understand what this means in practice, including any practical considerations. Note that Kranich is remote-first, so in many cases a move between teams does not require a physical move at all.

Your compensation in the new role will be set according to the same framework used for external hires at that level. In most cases, an internal move is lateral, meaning your salary stays the same. If the new role is at a different level, your manager and the People & Culture team will discuss this with you before you accept the offer.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, your eligibility, or the internal mobility process in general, please reach out to the People & Culture team at people@kranich.example. You can also speak with your People Partner or with Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture.

For questions about how a move might affect your benefits, your working pattern, or other employment details, please contact people@kranich.example and we will point you to the right person.

We also encourage you to talk to colleagues who have made an internal move. They can share their experience and give you a sense of what the process feels like from the inside. Your network at Kranich is one of your greatest resources, and internal mobility is a great way to build it further.

Example: Sofia, a product manager on the Fleet Insights team, was curious about a role in the Routing Core team but was not sure whether she was ready. She reached out to people@kranich.example and had a confidential conversation with a People Partner. That conversation helped her decide to apply, and while she was not selected this time, she received useful feedback and a clearer sense of what she wants to develop next.

We are excited to support you in finding the work that brings out the best in you. Internal mobility is one of the ways we live our value of growing together, and we look forward to seeing where your next step takes you.
