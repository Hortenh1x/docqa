# Procurement Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how to request, approve, and complete purchases at Kranich Software GmbH. It applies to every employee, regardless of their role or location, whether they work from the Ritterstraße office, the Rua do Alecrim office, or remotely from home.

This version supersedes version 1.0. The previous version did not clearly describe approval thresholds or preferred suppliers; this version does. If you have questions about how this policy applies to a specific situation, contact Finance or your manager.

We want purchasing to be simple and fast, while making sure we spend company money responsibly. We also want to avoid surprises: no one should commit Kranich to a cost without following the steps below. If you are unsure whether something counts as a purchase under this policy, treat it as one and ask.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, wants to buy a new headset for client calls. She checks this policy, follows the request steps, and receives her headset within a few days. The process is straightforward because she planned ahead.

## 2. Requesting a purchase

Before you buy anything for Kranich, you must submit a purchase request. Do not place an order, sign a contract, or use your own money before the request is approved, unless you have explicit prior approval from Finance.

To request a purchase, follow these steps:

- Describe what you need and why. Include the business purpose, for example "a second monitor for the Fleet Insights team" or "a customer workshop in Poland."
- State the estimated cost. If you do not know the exact price, provide your best estimate and explain how you arrived at it.
- Name the supplier you plan to use, if you have one. If you are not sure which supplier to choose, see §4 for preferred suppliers.
- Submit the request through the tool your manager uses for approvals. If you cannot find the right place, ask it-help@kranich.example for guidance.

Once you submit a request, it goes to the right approver based on the approval thresholds in §3. You will receive a notification when a decision is made. If your request is approved, you may proceed with the purchase. If it is declined, the approver will explain why and suggest next steps.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, needs a portable projector for an upcoming client demonstration. He submits a request with the estimated cost and the supplier name. His manager approves it the same day, and Marek places the order.

Some purchases do not follow the regular request process. These include expenses you incur while traveling, which you handle through Ledgerly, and purchases made with a corporate card, whose limits are defined in POL-017 §5. If you use a corporate card for a purchase, you still need to follow this policy for approval before you buy.

## 3. Approval thresholds

Access: Finance only

Approval thresholds determine who can approve a purchase request. The thresholds are based on the estimated cost of the purchase, not the final invoice. If your estimate is wrong, the actual cost may still be approved as long as it does not exceed the threshold by a significant margin; if it does, you must submit a new request.

Purchases up to €600 are approved by your manager. This covers most day-to-day needs, such as office supplies, books, software subscriptions, and small equipment. Your manager may ask you to explain the need or to consider a cheaper alternative before approving.

Purchases above €20,000 require CFO approval. Between these two thresholds, approval is needed from the head of your team or the relevant department lead. For example, a purchase for the Routing Core team above €600 but not exceeding €20,000 is approved by the engineering manager for that team.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, wants to buy a license for a specialized analytics tool. The cost is above €600 but below €20,000. She submits her request, and the head of Fleet Insights approves it. If the tool had cost more than €20,000, the request would go to Tomasz Zieliński, the CFO, for approval.

When a request needs CFO approval, allow a few extra days for review. The CFO may ask for a short business case or a comparison of alternative options. Please respond promptly to these requests so the purchase is not delayed.

If you split a purchase into several smaller requests to avoid a higher approval threshold, that is not allowed. Approve each purchase based on its true total cost. If you are unsure whether two requests are really one purchase, ask Finance before submitting.

## 4. Preferred suppliers

Kranich has agreements with a set of preferred suppliers for common goods and services. Using these suppliers saves time and money because pricing and terms are already negotiated. You are encouraged to check the list of preferred suppliers on Grove before you submit a purchase request.

Preferred suppliers are available for categories such as:

- Office furniture and equipment
- Software subscriptions and cloud services
- Travel and accommodation, booked through Ledgerly
- Meeting-room and desk equipment, including items for the Perch system

If a preferred supplier offers what you need, choose that supplier. If you believe another supplier is better suited, explain why in your purchase request. For example, a preferred supplier may not offer a product with the specific feature your team needs, or a local supplier may provide faster service in your region.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, needs a new keyboard. The preferred supplier for office equipment delivers to Portugal within a few days and offers the model she wants. She selects that supplier, and her manager approves the request the same day.

For purchases above €20,000, Finance may ask you to obtain quotes from more than one supplier, even if a preferred supplier exists. This helps us confirm that we are getting fair value. If you need help comparing quotes, contact Finance.

The list of preferred suppliers is reviewed regularly and updated when agreements change. If you have a suggestion for a new preferred supplier, share it with Finance. We welcome input from all teams, especially from Customer Success and Go-to-Market, who often know what suppliers work well in the field.

## 5. Questions

If you have any question about this policy, start by talking to your manager. They can help you decide whether a purchase is necessary and which approval threshold applies.

For questions about approval thresholds, preferred suppliers, or whether a purchase needs CFO approval, contact Finance. For questions about paying an invoice or reimbursing an expense, use Ledgerly and follow the guidance there.

If you believe this policy is missing something or could be clearer, tell us. We review policies regularly and value feedback from employees. You can send suggestions to people@kranich.example, and they will be forwarded to the policy owner.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, is unsure whether a team-building activity counts as a purchase under this policy. She asks Finance, and they confirm that it does, because it involves spending company money on an external service. Lea then submits a request with the estimated cost and waits for approval before booking.

Remember: when in doubt, ask. A quick question before you buy can save you from an unapproved expense and a lengthy correction later. We are here to help you get what you need to do your best work.
