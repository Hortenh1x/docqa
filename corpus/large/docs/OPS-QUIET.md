# Quiet Zones

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software is a remote-first company, but our offices in Berlin (Ritterstraße) and Lisbon (Rua do Alecrim) are lively places where colleagues meet, collaborate, and connect. Not everyone wants the same level of background activity, though. Some tasks — deep coding, report writing, customer calls, or focused analysis — need sustained concentration.

This policy explains where you can find a calm place to work, and how to behave there so that everyone can focus.

## 2. Where

Each office has dedicated quiet zones. They are clearly marked on the floor plans in Grove and with signage at the entrance of each zone. Quiet zones are available on a first-come, first-served basis. You do not need to book them in advance.

If you work from home or another location, this policy does not apply to your home setup. It applies only to the physical office spaces we operate.

The quiet zones are separate from regular open-plan areas and from meeting rooms. If you need to take a call, hold a conversation, or work in a pair, please use a meeting room or the open-plan area instead. You can book a meeting room or desk through Perch.

If you are unsure whether a space is a quiet zone, check the signage or ask a colleague from Workplace & Operations — Rui's team will be happy to help.

## 3. Rules

The following rules apply in every quiet zone:

- Keep conversations to a minimum. Brief, whispered exchanges are acceptable, but please take longer discussions elsewhere.
- Take phone and video calls outside the quiet zone. If you must answer an urgent call, leave the zone promptly and return when you are done.
- Use headphones for any audio — music, videos, or virtual meetings you are only listening to.
- Set your devices to silent. Vibrations can be as distracting as ringtones.
- Avoid eating noisy snacks. Crunchy food and rustling wrappers disturb others.
- Keep your workspace tidy. When you leave, take your belongings and any rubbish with you.
- Be mindful of how you move. Avoid loud footsteps, heavy bags, or dragging chairs.

These rules apply equally to everyone, regardless of team or seniority. They are in place to protect the focus of colleagues who choose to work in a quiet zone.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, often spends her afternoons in the quiet zone when she needs to concentrate on complex routing algorithms. Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, uses the quiet zone in the morning for writing detailed handover notes. Both appreciate that the rules are simple and consistently respected. If Marek visits the Berlin office for a day of client preparation, he can rely on the quiet zone being a place where he can review technical documentation without interruption.

Occasionally, you may find that a quiet zone is fuller than you expected, or that a colleague has forgotten a rule. In that case, a polite reminder is usually enough. If the issue persists, speak to your manager or contact Workplace & Operations. We want everyone to feel comfortable using these spaces.
