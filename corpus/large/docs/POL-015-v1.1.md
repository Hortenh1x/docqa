# Internal Mobility Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we believe that growing within the company is as valuable as growing into a new company. The Internal Mobility Policy supports employees who want to move to a different team, take on a new responsibility, or explore a new function without leaving Kranich. This policy sets out a clear, fair, and transparent path for internal transfers.

This version supersedes version 1.0 of this policy. It reflects feedback we have gathered from employees and managers over the past year and simplifies the process for everyone involved. We encourage you to read the full policy before starting a conversation about moving teams.

Internal mobility benefits you, your current team, and your future team. It helps us retain knowledge, build cross-functional understanding, and keep our work engaging. Whether you are a backend engineer curious about product management, a customer success manager interested in field work, or a product manager who wants to move closer to engineering, this policy is here to support you.

## 2. Eligibility

Most employees are eligible to apply for an internal transfer. We ask for a minimum of 15 months in your current role before you begin the process. This gives you time to settle in, deliver meaningful work, and contribute to your team's goals before considering a move.

Your 15 months are counted from your first day in your current role, not from your start date at Kranich. If you have recently changed roles within the company, the clock restarts. If you joined Kranich through an acquisition, your time in the equivalent role at your previous company counts toward this period, as long as there has been no gap in employment.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, joined Kranich two years ago. She has been in her current role since day one, so she is eligible to apply for an internal transfer now. Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, moved into his current role a few months ago. He will need to wait until he reaches 15 months in that role before he can apply.

We make exceptions only in rare circumstances, such as when a role is being eliminated or when there is a documented hardship. In those cases, please speak with your manager or with People & Culture. The decision to grant an exception rests with People & Culture in consultation with the relevant engineering or business leaders.

Being on a performance improvement plan does not automatically make you ineligible, but we encourage you to focus on your current role first. If you are unsure about your eligibility, reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example, and we will help you understand where you stand.

## 3. How to apply

The application process is designed to be transparent and low-pressure. You can explore opportunities informally before committing to an application. Here is how it works.

First, have a conversation with your current manager. This is a courtesy and a practical step. Your manager can help you understand how your move might affect your current team and can support you in preparing for interviews. If you feel uncomfortable speaking with your manager, you can contact People & Culture directly, and we will facilitate the conversation.

Next, browse the internal roles posted on Grove, our intranet and policy library. Hiring managers across Kranich post their open positions there. If you see a role that interests you, reach out to the hiring manager or the relevant team lead to ask questions. You can also speak with your own network — colleagues in other teams, your mentor, or your previous manager.

When you are ready to apply, submit your application through the same process as any external candidate, but indicate that you are an internal applicant. Your application should include a short note about why you want to move and what you would bring to the new team. You do not need to prepare a formal resignation letter or go through an external recruitment agency.

The hiring manager will review your application. If they would like to proceed, they will invite you for an interview. The interview process is typically lighter than for external candidates, but it still gives both sides a chance to assess fit. You may be asked to complete a small task or give a short presentation, depending on the role.

You are welcome to apply for more than one role at a time, but we ask that you keep your current manager informed of each application. If you receive an offer, you can take a few days to decide. If you decide not to move, there is no penalty; you can stay in your current role and apply again later.

Throughout the process, People & Culture is available to answer questions, coach you on interviews, and help you navigate any tricky conversations. We want you to feel supported, not stressed, as you explore your next step.

## 4. Transition

Once you accept an internal offer, the transition begins. The transition period between teams is 5 weeks. This period is designed to give you and both teams enough time to hand over responsibilities, onboard you into the new role, and ensure continuity of work.

The 5 weeks are structured as follows, though the exact split may vary depending on your role and the needs of both teams. In the first part of the transition, you focus on handing over your current work. You will document your tasks, share knowledge with colleagues, and close out or reassign open items. Your current manager will work with you to create a handover plan.

In the second part of the transition, you start ramping up in your new team. You will meet your new colleagues, learn about their ways of working, and begin taking on small pieces of work. Your new manager will assign you a buddy or mentor where possible, and you will have regular check-ins to track your progress.

Your current manager and your new manager will coordinate the exact start and end dates of the transition. They will agree on a handover plan and share it with you before the transition begins. People & Culture will facilitate the conversation and make sure that both managers are aligned.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, accepted a role in the Fleet Insights team. Her current manager and her new manager agreed that she would spend the first 5 weeks of the transition wrapping up her current project and the remaining time learning the Fleet Insights codebase. Anna worked with her current team to document her work and introduced her colleague to ongoing tasks. She then joined her new team's stand-ups and started contributing to small tasks under supervision.

During the transition, your compensation and benefits remain unchanged. You will continue to report to your current manager until the transition is complete, and then you will report to your new manager. If you are moving between countries, for example from Berlin to Lisbon, please coordinate with Workplace & Operations and People & Culture early in the process. They will help you understand practical considerations such as work location and local employment details.

If the transfer does not work out — for example, if you or the new team feel that the fit is not right — we will work with you to find the best path forward. This may mean returning to your previous team, if there is an open role, or exploring other options within Kranich. We will handle these situations with care and respect.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. You can also speak with your manager, your HR business partner, or Lea, our people operations specialist in Berlin. We are happy to walk you through the process, discuss your eligibility, or help you think through whether an internal move is right for you at this time.

For questions about how a transfer affects your employment details, such as your contract or your work location, please contact Workplace & Operations at it-help@kranich.example, and they will connect you with the right person.

We also encourage you to review related policies on Grove, including POL-004, which covers role changes and job architecture, and FIN-RATES-2026, which provides an overview of compensation bands. These documents will give you additional context as you consider your options.

Internal mobility is one of the many ways we invest in your growth at Kranich. We hope this policy makes the path clear and inviting. If you have ideas for how we can improve the experience, we would love to hear them. Please share your feedback with People & Culture at any time.
