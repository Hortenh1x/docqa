# Exit Interview Guide
Access: People & Culture only

## 1. Purpose

When someone leaves Kranich Software, we want to understand why. Exit interviews give us a chance to hear, in the departing person's own words, what worked well and what did not. The goal is not to persuade anyone to stay — by the time we reach this conversation, the decision has been made and we respect it. The goal is to learn.

We use what we hear to improve how we work: how teams are led, how projects are planned, how feedback flows, and how we support each other across a remote-first company. A single exit interview rarely changes anything by itself. Over time, patterns emerge, and patterns are what we act on.

This guide explains how exit interviews are conducted at Kranich Software. It also explains what we do with the information afterwards. Confidentiality is the foundation of the entire process. If people do not trust that their words are handled carefully, they will not speak honestly — and we will learn nothing.

## 2. Who this is for

This guide is for People & Culture team members who conduct or support exit interviews. It is also for managers and team leads who want to understand what happens after someone on their team resigns.

The departing employee is not expected to read this guide. They will receive a short, plain-language invitation from People & Culture explaining the purpose of the conversation, how long it will take, and who will be present.

People & Culture owns the process. Managers do not conduct exit interviews for their own direct reports. This keeps the conversation neutral and gives the departing person space to speak freely.

## 3. Process

When a resignation is confirmed, the People Operations specialist — usually Lea — schedules the exit interview. The conversation happens with one person from People & Culture. No manager from the departing person's team is present.

The interview is held as a video call by default, since most of us work remotely. If the person prefers an in-person conversation and is near the Berlin or Lisbon office, that can be arranged. The departing person chooses the format that feels most comfortable.

Before the conversation, People & Culture reviews the person's employment history in Fern: their start date, team, role changes, and any formal feedback or performance notes. This context helps us ask better questions. We do not share this context with anyone outside People & Culture.

During the conversation, we take notes. We tell the person that we are taking notes and that the notes will be stored confidentially. We do not record the conversation unless the person explicitly asks us to, and we only do so with their written consent.

After the conversation, People & Culture writes a summary. The summary captures themes and direct quotes where useful, but removes identifying details unless the person has given permission to share them. The summary is stored in the exit interview folder in Grove, accessible only to People & Culture.

If the departing person mentions a serious concern — for example, something that could amount to harassment or discrimination — People & Culture follows the relevant policy and escalates to the Head of Information Security or the VP People & Culture as appropriate. Confidentiality has limits, and we are transparent about those limits at the start of the conversation.

## 4. Timelines

The exit interview happens during the notice period, ideally before the person's last working day. People & Culture aims to schedule it within a few days of the resignation being confirmed.

If the person prefers to speak after their last day, we accommodate that. Some people feel more comfortable once they have fully left. A conversation held a few weeks after departure can be just as valuable as one held during the notice period.

The summary is written promptly after the conversation, while the details are still fresh. Aggregated, anonymised learnings are shared with leadership on a regular cadence — at least a few times per year. Individual summaries are not shared with managers automatically. A manager may receive anonymised feedback about their team if the departing person agrees and if the feedback is specific enough to be actionable.

## 5. Questions

We keep the question set short and open-ended. The point is to listen, not to work through a checklist. The following questions are a guide, not a script. Follow the person's lead.

- What prompted you to start looking for a new role?
- What did you enjoy most about working at Kranich Software?
- What did you find most challenging?
- How would you describe the culture of your team, and of the company as a whole?
- Was there anything you needed from your manager, your team, or People & Culture that you did not receive?
- How did your role change over time, and did that match what you expected when you joined?
- What could Kranich Software have done differently to make you stay?
- Is there anything about how you were managed that you would want your manager to know?
- What advice would you give to someone joining your team tomorrow?
- Is there anything else you would like to share before we close?

Some questions may not apply. If someone is leaving for a personal reason — a partner's job move, a desire to travel, a family situation — we do not push for a deeper professional reason. We thank them for their time and wish them well.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, resigns to join a client company. In his exit interview, he explains that he loved the client-facing work but felt that internal documentation for new deployments was hard to find. He gives permission for People & Culture to share this feedback with the Platform & Infrastructure team, without naming him. The team uses it to reorganise the deployment guides in Grove.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, leaves after a short tenure. In her interview, she says she received excellent onboarding but rarely heard from her manager outside of scheduled check-ins. People & Culture aggregates this pattern across several interviews and shares it with the Customer Success leadership as a theme, without identifying any individual.

What we never do: we never use an exit interview to argue with the person's reasons, to defend a manager, or to convince them to withdraw their resignation. We listen, we thank them, and we learn.
