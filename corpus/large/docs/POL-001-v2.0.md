# Vacation & Leave Policy

Superseded by version 2.3.

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how vacation works at Kranich Software GmbH. We believe that time away from work helps everyone return refreshed and do their best work. This policy sets out your annual vacation entitlement, how to request time off, and what happens to unused days at the end of the year.

We keep the process simple. You request vacation through Fern, our human resources system, and your manager approves it. We trust each other to plan ahead so that teams can cover important work while you are away.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of whether you work from our Berlin office in Ritterstraße, our Lisbon office in Rua do Alecrim, or remotely from your home in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom. We are a remote-first company, and this policy treats everyone the same no matter where they are employed.

The policy covers annual vacation entitlement, the request process, carryover of unused days, and special leave. It does not cover sick leave, parental leave, or other statutory leave types; those are handled separately and you can find more information in the relevant policies on Grove, our intranet.

## 3. Annual vacation entitlement

Full-time employees receive 25 days of vacation per calendar year. This entitlement is for the calendar year, which runs from January through December, matching our fiscal year.

If you join Kranich Software partway through the year, your entitlement is calculated proportionally based on the months remaining in the calendar year. If you leave the company partway through the year, you are expected to use vacation days in proportion to the time you have worked; any days taken beyond that proportion will be deducted from your final pay.

Part-time employees receive vacation on a pro-rata basis. For example, if you work half of a full-time schedule, you would receive half of the annual entitlement. Your exact entitlement is visible in Fern, and the People & Culture team can help you understand how it is calculated for your specific working pattern.

Vacation days are counted in full days. If you normally work a partial day, you should discuss with your manager how to account for vacation time in a way that is fair and consistent.

## 4. Requesting vacation

You request vacation through Fern. Submit your request as early as you can, especially for popular times of year such as summer months or the winter holidays. Your manager reviews the request and either approves or declines it. If your manager declines a request, they will explain why and suggest an alternative, for example because several teammates have already requested the same period.

Requests for more than five consecutive days must be submitted 4 weeks in advance. This lead time gives your team enough opportunity to plan coverage and ensures that client commitments are met. For shorter requests, we ask that you give your manager as much notice as possible, but we do not set a fixed minimum.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to take a week off in late summer to visit her family in Porto. Because her trip covers more than five consecutive working days, she submits her request in Fern about a month ahead of time. Her manager approves it, and she coordinates with her teammate to hand over the tasks she is working on.

When you request vacation, consider the needs of your team and our clients. We do our best to accommodate everyone, but sometimes we must limit how many people are away at the same time, particularly in teams that support client-facing work. If two people request the same period and only one can be away, the manager decides based on business needs and fairness.

If your plans change and you need to cancel or shorten a vacation, update your request in Fern as soon as you know. This frees up the days for someone else if they want them.

## 5. Carryover

We encourage you to use your full annual vacation entitlement within the calendar year. Taking regular breaks supports your wellbeing and prevents burnout.

Unused vacation days must be taken by March 31 of the following year. In other words, if you have days left over at the end of December, you may carry them into the new year, but you must use them by the end of March. Days not taken by that date are forfeited.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, had a busy autumn and did not use all of her vacation days by the end of the year. She carries the remaining days into the new year and books them for a long weekend in February. Because she uses them before March 31, she loses nothing.

If you know early in the year that you will not be able to use all of your days, talk to your manager. Sometimes a different schedule or a shorter break can help. The goal is that everyone takes the time they are entitled to.

## 6. Special leave

In addition to annual vacation, you may be eligible for special leave in certain circumstances. Special leave is paid time off for events that require you to be away from work for a short period. It is separate from your annual vacation entitlement and does not reduce the days you have for vacation.

Examples of special leave include moving to a new home, attending a close family member's wedding, or dealing with a sudden personal matter. You may also take special leave for important appointments that cannot be scheduled outside working hours, such as a visa appointment or a legal hearing.

Special leave is granted at the discretion of your manager and the People & Culture team. The amount of time depends on the situation; we ask you to be reasonable and to keep your manager informed. For most situations, a day or two is sufficient, and longer arrangements should be discussed with People & Culture.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, needs to attend his sister's wedding on a Thursday. He speaks with his manager, who approves one day of special leave. Marek uses the day for the wedding and schedules his client visits around it.

To request special leave, submit a request in Fern and select the appropriate leave type. Your manager will approve it if the circumstances qualify. If you are unsure whether your situation counts as special leave, ask People & Culture before you make plans.

## 7. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, the easiest first step is to talk to your manager. They can help with practical questions about scheduling and team coverage.

For questions about your entitlement, carryover, or special leave, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. The People Operations team, including Lea in Berlin, is happy to help. You can also find answers to common questions on Grove.

If you notice a technical issue with Fern, such as a request that will not submit or an incorrect balance, contact it-help@kranich.example. The Platform & Infrastructure team will look into it.

We review our policies regularly and value your feedback. If you have suggestions for how this policy could be clearer or fairer, please share them with People & Culture. Your input helps us make Kranich Software a good place to work for everyone.
