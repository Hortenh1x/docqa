# Probation Reviews

Access: Managers only

## 1. Purpose

Probation reviews help us make good decisions about new team members and help new team members understand how they are settling in. The purpose of this document is to give you a clear, consistent framework for running probation reviews across Kranich Software GmbH.

We believe probation is a two-way process. It is a time for the new colleague to learn about us, and for us to learn about them. A thoughtful review protects everyone: the individual, the team and the company.

This policy explains when reviews happen, who is involved and what you should prepare. It also gives you guidance on having honest, kind conversations about performance and fit.

## 2. Who this is for

This document is for managers who oversee a colleague during their probation period. That includes engineering managers, team leads and anyone else with direct people responsibility, regardless of whether you are based in Berlin, Lisbon or working remotely from another country.

If you are a new colleague on probation, your manager will share the relevant parts of this process with you directly. You do not need access to this document; your manager will guide you.

People Operations is your partner throughout. If you are unsure about any step, reach out to the People & Culture team via people@kranich.example. For questions about the technical setup of review forms or scheduling tools, contact it-help@kranich.example.

## 3. Process

Probation reviews follow a simple rhythm: prepare, discuss, decide, document.

**Prepare.** Before each review conversation, gather input from people who have worked closely with the new colleague. This might include peers, direct reports or colleagues from other teams such as Customer Success or Platform & Infrastructure. Ask for specific observations about work quality, collaboration and alignment with our values. Do not rely on your own impression alone.

Review the colleague's original job description and the goals agreed at onboarding. Note where they are meeting expectations and where they need support.

**Discuss.** Hold a dedicated conversation with the colleague. This is not a casual chat; block out proper time and give the colleague advance notice so they can prepare too. The conversation should cover:

- What is going well
- Challenges or areas for growth
- How the colleague feels about their role and the team
- Support they need from you or from Kranich

Listen more than you speak. Probation reviews are a chance for the colleague to raise concerns about clarity, tools or working relationships. Take those concerns seriously.

**Decide.** After the conversation, consider the outcome. There are three possible decisions:

- Probation continues as planned
- Probation ends early because the colleague is clearly performing well
- Probation is extended or the employment ends

Most reviews will result in the first outcome. Extending probation is rare and requires approval from People & Culture. Ending employment during probation is a significant decision; you must involve People & Culture before any such conversation.

**Document.** Use the probation review form available on Grove, our intranet and policy library. Complete the form promptly after the discussion and share it with the colleague. They may add their own comments. Store the completed form in the colleague's personnel file via Fern, our HRIS.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, reviews a new team member who joined from a competitor. Deniz gathers feedback from two colleagues in Go-to-Market and one in Routing Core. The feedback is consistently positive, and the new team member has already improved one of our client onboarding templates. Deniz decides to end probation early and documents this clearly.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, recently became a manager. She runs her first probation review for a new engineer. The engineer is technically strong but has missed several team ceremonies. In the review conversation, Anna learns the engineer was confused about the calendar invite time zone. Anna adjusts the team's communication norms and probation continues as planned.

## 4. Timelines

Your colleague's probation period is stated in their employment contract. You should have received a reminder from Fern before the first review point. If you have not seen a reminder, check the colleague's start date in Fern and plan accordingly.

The standard rhythm is a review roughly halfway through the probation period and a final review before probation ends. For shorter probation periods, a single review may be appropriate. When in doubt, have more conversations rather than fewer.

Schedule the final review early enough that, if the decision is to end employment, the correct notice period is respected. Your People Partner will help you calculate this; do not attempt to do it alone.

If a colleague is absent for a significant portion of their probation — for example, due to illness — discuss with People & Culture whether the probation period should be adjusted. Never make this decision unilaterally.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about a specific case, start with your People Partner. They know the details of employment contracts and local requirements in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom.

For general questions about this policy, contact people@kranich.example. For feedback on the review form itself, mention that in your message so we can route it to the right person.

If you believe a review decision was made unfairly, or if you have concerns about how a colleague was treated during probation, escalate to Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture. You may also contact Priya Nayar, Head of Information Security, if the concern involves a breach of confidentiality or data protection.

Remember that probation reviews are confidential. Share the content of reviews only with those who need to know: the colleague, their People Partner and, where necessary, your own manager. Respect the privacy of the individual throughout.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, is reviewing a new colleague on his team. He is unsure whether a particular client compliment should carry weight in the review. He asks his People Partner for guidance and learns that client feedback is useful context but should not override internal observations. Marek includes the compliment as supporting evidence and keeps the focus on the colleague's own work.

Thank you for taking probation reviews seriously. Done well, they set the tone for a productive and trusting working relationship.
