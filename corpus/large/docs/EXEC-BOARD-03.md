# Board Memo 3 — 2025
Access: Leadership only

## 1. Summary

We enter the second half of the year in a strong position. Kranich Route Cloud continues to gain traction with mid-size logistics operators across Europe, and our focus on reliability and customer outcomes is paying off. This memo summarises growth, cash position, key risks, and the asks we bring to the board.

## 2. Growth

ARR grew 38% year over year. This growth is broad-based rather than dependent on a single large deal. Our Routing Core and Fleet Insights products both contributed meaningfully, and we saw particular strength in the Polish and French markets. Customer retention remains healthy, and the expansion revenue from existing accounts continues to be a reliable driver.

The go-to-market motion is maturing. We have shortened the sales cycle for standard deployments, and the field solutions engineering team, including colleagues like Marek in Poland, is helping prospects realise value earlier in the conversation. Product-led growth experiments from the Fleet Insights team, led by Sofia, are showing early promise in converting trial users into paying customers.

We are investing in Customer Success to protect this momentum. Deniz and her colleagues in Berlin are building playbooks that help customers adopt more of the platform. We believe the current pipeline supports continued strong growth into next year, though we expect some seasonal softness in the summer months.

## 3. Cash

Cash runway stands at 24 months. We have not needed to draw on external financing, and our disciplined approach to hiring and spend has kept us comfortably within budget. The finance team, under Tomasz, has improved our forecasting cadence, which gives us better visibility into monthly cash consumption.

We continue to fund the Lisbon office expansion and the Platform & Infrastructure team's work on scalability. These investments are deliberate and tied to our product roadmap. We are not planning any major capital expenditure in the near term. If growth accelerates beyond plan, we have headroom to increase hiring, but we will only do so when the return is clear.

## 4. Risks

The main risks we track are competitive pressure, key-person dependency, and operational resilience.

On competition, larger enterprise vendors are increasingly bundling route optimisation into broader logistics suites. We counter this with our focus on mid-size operators, where our time-to-value and customer support are differentiators. We will continue to monitor this closely.

On people, a few senior engineers and product leaders hold deep institutional knowledge. We are mitigating this through documentation, cross-training, and the org structure maintained in Fern. Aylin's team is running stay conversations with critical roles to understand what keeps them engaged.

On operations, we depend on the availability of our cloud infrastructure. The Platform & Infrastructure team has improved our incident response, and Priya's security programme has matured. We conduct regular reviews of our business continuity plans. The recent audit referenced in POL-004 identified no critical findings.

We also note the usual market risks: macroeconomic softness could lengthen sales cycles, and currency fluctuations between the euro and the pound affect our UK business. None of these currently threaten our plan, but we are watching them.

## 5. Asks

We ask the board for the following:

- Endorsement of the current strategy and investment priorities for the remainder of the year.
- Guidance on the competitive landscape, particularly any signals the board sees from the broader logistics software market.
- Approval to continue hiring for a limited number of engineering and customer-facing roles as outlined in the people plan.

We also ask the board to note the updated expense policy referenced in FIN-RATES-2026, which will be circulated separately. We welcome any questions in advance of the next meeting.
