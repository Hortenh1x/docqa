# Contractor Engagement Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains when and how we engage contractors at Kranich Software GmbH. Contractors bring specialised skills and extra capacity to our teams for defined pieces of work. We want the process to be clear for hiring managers, for People & Culture, and for the contractors themselves.

The policy sets out the basic rules for proposing a contractor, the maximum length of an engagement, and how we manage access and equipment. It applies to all contractor engagements across the company, in every office and for every remote worker, regardless of the country where the contractor is based.

We distinguish contractors from employees. Employees are hired through our standard process and appear in Fern. Contractors are engaged through a separate agreement and are not part of the Fern org chart. If you are unsure whether a role should be filled by an employee or a contractor, talk to your manager and to People & Culture before you start the process.

## 2. When to use a contractor

Use a contractor when you need a specific skill or an extra pair of hands for a project with a clear end date. Contractors are a good fit for work that is temporary, project-based, or highly specialised and not available within the company.

Contractors are not a way to bypass the hiring process or to fill a permanent role. If you see a need for ongoing work that will continue past the end of a project, that is a signal that you should open a permanent position instead. Speak with People & Culture if you are not sure which route fits.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, needs help building a set of onboarding materials for a new client segment. The work will take a few weeks and will not continue afterwards. A contractor is the right choice. In contrast, if Deniz expected to keep producing onboarding materials for new segments every quarter, she should discuss a permanent role with her manager.

Contractor engagements must be approved before the contractor starts. The hiring manager submits a request through People & Culture, who checks the business case and confirms that the work is genuinely temporary. Legal reviews the contract terms before the contractor signs.

We do not use contractors for work that is core to our product roadmap or that involves managing other people. Contractors work on defined tasks under the direction of a Kranich employee. They do not supervise Kranich staff and they do not make hiring or firing decisions.

## 3. Duration and renewal

A contractor engagement has a defined start date and a defined end date. The end date is agreed before the work begins and is written into the contract.

A contractor engagement may not exceed 9 months without a Legal review. This means that the total length of the engagement, including any extensions, must stay under that limit unless Legal has reviewed and approved a longer arrangement.

If you think the work will take longer than the allowed period, you must plan for that before the contractor starts. Talk to Legal early, explain the situation, and get a decision. Do not let the engagement run past the limit while you wait for approval.

Renewals are possible but not automatic. If you want to extend a contractor’s engagement, you must submit a renewal request to People & Culture before the current end date. People & Culture will check the reason for the extension and confirm that the work is still temporary. Legal reviews the renewal if the total engagement would cross the limit set out above.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, is helping a customer implement a new integration. He expects the implementation to take a few months. When the customer asks for additional work that would push the engagement past the maximum period, Marek contacts his manager and Legal before agreeing to anything. They review the situation and decide whether a longer engagement is justified.

No contractor may continue working after their end date without written approval. If you become aware that a contractor is still working after the agreed end date, tell People & Culture immediately.

## 4. Access and equipment

Contractors receive access to the Kranich systems they need to do their work. Access is granted on a least-privilege basis, meaning the contractor gets only the tools and data required for the specific tasks in their contract.

The hiring manager requests access through Platform & Infrastructure. Access is set up before the contractor’s start date and is removed on the contractor’s end date. It is your responsibility to notify Platform & Infrastructure if a contractor’s work changes so that access can be adjusted.

Contractors use a Kranich-managed device for their work. The device is provided by Workplace & Operations and is configured according to our security standards. Contractors do not use their personal devices for Kranich work unless an exception has been approved by the Head of Information Security.

All contractor devices are set up with our standard security tools, including 1Password for password management and access to the internal systems they need. Contractors do not get accounts in Fern or Ledgerly, as those tools are for employees only. They may use Grove to read relevant policies, but they do not contribute to internal content.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, is supervising a contractor who helps with a data migration. Anna requests access for the contractor through Platform & Infrastructure, specifying the repositories and the test environment the contractor will use. When the migration is complete, Anna confirms that the work is done, and Platform & Infrastructure removes the contractor’s access on the agreed date.

Contractors are expected to follow the same security and data-protection rules as employees. They must handle customer data carefully, use only approved tools, and report any security incident promptly to security@kranich.example. The contractor’s contract includes confidentiality obligations, and the contractor is bound by those obligations even after the engagement ends.

If a contractor needs to book a meeting room or a desk at one of our offices, they can do so through Perch. Access to the offices themselves is arranged by Workplace & Operations and is limited to the duration of the engagement.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, talk to your manager or to People & Culture. For questions about contract terms, renewal, or the maximum engagement period, contact Legal. For questions about access, devices, or security, contact Platform & Infrastructure or the Head of Information Security.

People & Culture maintains a list of approved contractor agencies and individual contractors who have worked with us before. This list is available on Grove under the People & Culture section. Using an approved contractor does not remove the need for approval under this policy.

We review this policy from time to time to make sure it stays useful and reflects how we work. You will always be able to find the current version on Grove.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, receives a question from a hiring manager about whether a contractor can be extended for another few weeks. Lea explains the renewal process, checks the total engagement length against the limit in §3, and advises the manager to contact Legal if the total would exceed that limit.

Thank you for helping us keep contractor engagements clear, compliant, and fair for everyone.
