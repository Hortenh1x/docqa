# Sick Leave & Medical Absence

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how sick leave and medical absence work at Kranich Software GmbH. We want you to focus on getting better when you are unwell, without worrying about administrative steps or pay. This version supersedes version 1.0 of this policy and clarifies how we handle notifications, certificates, pay, and returning to work.

We recognize that illness can take many forms: a short flu, a longer recovery after surgery, or a chronic condition that flares up from time to time. This policy applies to all employees, regardless of where you are based or whether you work from a Kranich office or from home. The same principles of trust, transparency, and care apply across all our locations and countries of employment.

If you have questions at any point, please reach out to People & Culture or your manager. We are here to support you, not to make a difficult time harder.

## 2. Telling us

When you are unable to work because of illness, please let your manager know as soon as you can. A quick message in the morning is usually enough — you do not need to provide details about your symptoms. We simply need to know that you are unwell and will not be working that day.

Please also keep your team informed if your absence affects shared work or deadlines. You can ask a teammate to cover urgent tasks, or let your manager decide what to reprioritize. If you are well enough, a short note in your team's channel helps everyone plan their day.

If your illness continues beyond the first day, please contact your manager again each day you remain absent, unless you have already agreed on a longer reporting rhythm. For longer absences, we may agree that you check in less frequently — for example, once every few days. This keeps the communication light while ensuring we know your status.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wakes up with a fever. She sends a message to her manager before the start of her workday, saying she is unwell and will rest. She does not need to explain what she has or how long she expects to be away. Her manager acknowledges the message and tells her to focus on recovery.

If you are in a country where local law requires a specific notification procedure, please follow that procedure as well. When in doubt, contact People & Culture for guidance.

## 3. Doctor's certificate

For short absences, we trust your word. You do not need a doctor's certificate for the first days of illness.

A doctor's certificate is required from the 3rd calendar day of absence. This means if your absence starts on a Monday, you need to provide a certificate covering Monday and any following days by Wednesday. The certificate should state that you are unfit to work and, where possible, the expected duration of your incapacity.

You can submit the certificate to People & Culture through Fern, our HRIS. If you cannot access Fern, you can email it to people@kranich.example. Please submit the certificate promptly so we can update your records and arrange your pay correctly.

If local law in your country of employment requires a certificate earlier than the 3rd calendar day, please follow the stricter local requirement. Your manager or People & Culture can tell you what applies in your location.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, feels unwell on a Tuesday and stays home. She does not need a certificate on Tuesday or Wednesday. When she is still unwell on Thursday — the 3rd calendar day of her absence — she visits her doctor, obtains a certificate, and uploads it to Fern the same day.

If you are absent for a recurring medical reason, such as a chronic condition, please speak with People & Culture. We can agree on a practical arrangement for certificates that reduces the administrative burden for you.

## 4. Pay during illness

Kranich continues full pay for the first 6 weeks of illness. This applies to each continuous period of absence caused by the same or a related illness. You do not need to use vacation days or personal days for illness — your pay continues as usual.

After the 6 weeks of full pay, your pay may continue under local statutory provisions in your country of employment. In Germany, for example, health insurance provides a sickness benefit after the employer's continued-pay period ends. In other countries, similar schemes may apply. People & Culture can explain what applies to you based on your contract and location.

If your illness is work-related, additional rules may apply under local accident insurance or workers' compensation schemes. Please inform your manager and People & Culture as soon as possible so we can support you with the right process.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, has surgery and is unable to work for several weeks. During the first 6 weeks, Kranich pays his full salary. After that, his pay continues through the Polish social insurance system. People & Culture helps him understand the paperwork and timelines.

We ask that you keep us informed about the expected duration of your absence when you know it. This helps us plan workloads and, if needed, arrange temporary support for your team.

## 5. Returning to work

When you are ready to return, please let your manager know in advance — ideally a few days before your planned return date. This gives your team time to prepare and ensures you have a smooth re-entry.

You do not need to provide a doctor's clearance to return unless your absence was long or your doctor recommended a phased return. If a phased return is appropriate — for example, starting with reduced hours or lighter duties — please discuss this with your manager and People & Culture. We will do our best to accommodate a gradual transition that supports your recovery.

If your absence was related to a mental health condition, we encourage you to return at a pace that feels sustainable. You may also want to speak with your manager about any adjustments that would help you settle back in, such as a quieter workload for the first week or more frequent check-ins.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, returns after a two-month absence. She agrees with her manager to start with half days for the first week. People & Culture coordinates with her to ensure her pay and benefits are correctly adjusted during the phased return.

After you return, your manager may schedule a short conversation to see how you are doing. This is not a performance review — it is simply a way to check that you feel supported and that any ongoing needs are addressed.

If you find that you need to take further time off shortly after returning, that is okay. Illness does not follow a schedule. Please follow the same notification process described in §2.

## 6. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, please contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. We are happy to clarify how this policy applies to your specific situation, your country of employment, or your contract.

For questions about pay during illness, Finance can also help. You can reach them through Ledgerly or by asking People & Culture to connect you.

If you believe you have been treated unfairly in relation to sick leave, or if you need a reasonable accommodation related to a medical condition, please speak with your manager, People & Culture, or our Head of Information Security if the matter involves a confidentiality concern. You can also reach out to our leadership team directly — all of us are committed to a fair and supportive workplace.

We review this policy periodically. If you have suggestions for improvement, please share them with People & Culture. Your experience helps us make Kranich a better place to work for everyone.
