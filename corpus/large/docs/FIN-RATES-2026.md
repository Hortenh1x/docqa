# Expense Rates & Per-Diems 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document sets the per-diem rates that Kranich Software GmbH reimburses for business travel in 2026. It applies to all employees, regardless of their home office location, when they travel for work within the countries listed in §2. For any travel not covered here, or for questions about unusual situations, please reach out to Finance before your trip.

These rates are meant to cover meals and incidental expenses while you are away from your usual work location. They are not intended to cover accommodation, transport, or other costs that you book through Ledgerly; those are handled separately under the travel booking process.

This is the current sheet for 2026. When you submit expenses in Ledgerly, select the rate that matches the country where you spent the travel day. If a future version of this sheet is published, the version number in the document id (FIN-RATES-2026) will change accordingly.

## 2. Per-diem rates by country

The following daily rates apply for full travel days in 2026. A full travel day means that you were away from your usual work location for at least eight hours, as described in §3.

| Country | Per-diem rate |
|---|---|
| Germany | €28 per day |
| Portugal | €24 per day |
| Spain | €26 per day |
| Poland | €22 per day |
| France | €32 per day |
| United Kingdom | €36 per day |

## 3. Partial travel days

Not every work trip fills a full day. When you travel for a shorter period, the per-diem is adjusted accordingly. Trips under eight hours pay 50% of the country rate. This applies regardless of whether the trip crosses a meal time or not. For example, a short client visit in the morning counts as a partial day, and you claim 50% of the rate for the country you visited.

A trip that lasts eight hours or more counts as a full travel day, and you claim the full rate for that country. When your travel spans two countries on the same day, you should claim the rate for the country where you spent most of the day. If the time is evenly split, choose the rate of the country where you had your main business activity.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, travels to a site for a morning meeting and returns home by early afternoon. His trip is under eight hours, so he claims 50% of the Polish rate for that day. On a separate occasion, he spends a full day visiting a client in Germany, and he claims the full German rate.

## 4. Meals provided by others

When a meal is provided to you as part of the business activity — for example, a client hosts lunch, or a conference includes breakfast — you must deduct that meal from your per-diem claim. The deduction applies per meal, and the remaining per-diem covers the other meals and incidentals of that day.

You do not need to deduct a meal that is incidental, such as coffee or a snack during a meeting. If you are unsure whether a provided meal counts, use your judgment: a full breakfast, lunch, or dinner counts; light refreshments do not.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, attends a client workshop in France for a full day. The client provides lunch. She claims the French per-diem for that day, minus the deduction for the provided lunch, and submits the claim in Ledgerly with a short note explaining the situation.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about these rates, how to apply a deduction, or how to handle a travel day that does not fit the rules above, contact Finance through Ledgerly or write to the usual support alias. For questions about how to submit a claim correctly, the Expense Policy (POL-004) provides further guidance.

If you believe a rate does not reflect the actual cost of a destination, please let Finance know before your trip. We review this sheet each year and appreciate feedback from employees who travel regularly.
