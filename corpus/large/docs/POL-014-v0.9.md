# Public Holidays & Bridge Days

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how public holidays are handled at Kranich Software GmbH and introduces bridge days, a benefit that helps you make the most of public holidays that fall on a Tuesday or a Thursday. We want you to plan your time off with confidence, knowing exactly what applies in your country of employment.

Kranich is a remote-first company with employees in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom. Public holidays differ from country to country, and sometimes from region to region. This policy respects those differences while ensuring a consistent, fair approach across the company.

The policy is written for employees, not lawyers. If anything is unclear, please reach out to People & Culture — we are happy to help.

## 2. Public holidays

Public holidays are days on which you are not expected to work and for which you receive your regular pay. You are entitled to the public holidays that apply in the country where you are employed. For employees in Germany, that includes the national holidays and the regional holidays of the German state in which you work. For employees in Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom, the same principle applies: you follow the national calendar of your country of employment.

Because Kranich is remote-first, we use the location of your employment contract to determine which public holidays apply to you. If you work remotely from a different country on a short-term basis, your entitlement does not change — you still follow the holiday calendar of your country of employment. If your remote work arrangement changes permanently, People & Culture will update your records accordingly.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, follows the Portuguese public holiday calendar. If she spends a few weeks working from Berlin, she still observes Portuguese public holidays during that time.

When a public holiday falls on a weekend, you do not receive an additional day off during the working week. Some countries have specific rules for this, and we follow the local practice of your country of employment. Where a public holiday is substituted to another date by law or local custom, that substituted date is the one you observe.

You are not required to use your vacation days for public holidays. Public holidays are separate from your annual vacation entitlement. If you are on vacation when a public holiday occurs, that day does not count against your vacation balance — it is a public holiday.

If your role requires you to work on a public holiday, for example during a critical system deployment or a client-facing event, your manager will discuss this with you in advance. In such cases, you will receive a day off in lieu at a mutually agreed time. This arrangement respects the spirit of the policy while acknowledging that our operations sometimes need coverage.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, supports a client during a scheduled maintenance window that falls on a public holiday. She agrees with her manager to take a day off in lieu the following week.

## 3. Bridge days

A bridge day is a day off that connects a public holiday to a weekend. When a public holiday falls on a Tuesday or a Thursday, the bridge day gives you the Monday or the Friday off, so you can enjoy a longer break without using your vacation days.

Kranich grants 3 days of bridge-day leave per year in addition to vacation. These days are available to all employees, regardless of your country of employment or your regional public holiday calendar.

The purpose of the bridge day is to bridge a public holiday that falls on a Tuesday or a Thursday. If a public holiday falls on a Monday or a Friday, you already have the long weekend, so no bridge day applies. If a public holiday falls on a Wednesday, there is nothing to bridge, and you use a bridge day only if a local holiday pattern makes it meaningful in your region.

Bridge days are flexible. You do not have to use them on the exact Monday or Friday adjacent to a public holiday. You may use them at any time during the calendar year, as long as your manager approves the time off in advance. This flexibility is useful when your team has a busy period around a public holiday, or when you prefer to take your bridge day at another moment.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, has a public holiday on a Thursday. His team has a delivery milestone that Friday, so he agrees with his manager to take his bridge day the following Monday instead.

Bridge days are granted on a calendar-year basis. They do not carry over to the next year. You are encouraged to plan and use your bridge days throughout the year rather than saving them for the end. Your manager and your team will appreciate knowing your plans in advance, so please request bridge days through the usual time-off process in Fern.

Bridge days are separate from your vacation entitlement. They are also separate from sick leave, personal leave and any other leave types described in other policies. Bridge days are intended for rest and recuperation, and they are paid days off.

If you join Kranich partway through the year, your bridge-day entitlement is prorated based on the months remaining in the calendar year. People & Culture will confirm your individual entitlement when you join. If you leave Kranich partway through the year, you are expected to use your bridge days before your last working day. Unused bridge days are not paid out upon departure.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, joined Kranich in the middle of the year. She checks her Fern profile and sees her prorated bridge-day balance for the remainder of the year. She plans to use them around the autumn public holidays in her region.

Bridge days do not affect your vacation balance, your overtime compensation or any other element of your employment. They are an additional benefit, granted on top of your statutory and contractual entitlements. If you are unsure whether a specific day qualifies as a bridge day, or how to request one, please speak with your manager or People & Culture.

## 4. Questions

If you have questions about public holidays or bridge days, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. For questions about how to record your time off in Fern, please contact it-help@kranich.example.

Your manager can also help you understand how this policy applies to your team and your schedule. For questions about how public holidays interact with your employment contract or your country-specific regulations, People & Culture will provide guidance.

We encourage you to read this policy together with related documents, such as POL-004 for vacation and other leave types. If you notice an error in this policy or have a suggestion for improvement, please let us know. We review our policies regularly and value your feedback.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights, works remotely from Spain. She has a question about whether a regional holiday in her area qualifies for a bridge day. She emails people@kranich.example and receives a clear answer within a few days, along with a note on how to record the day in Fern.

This policy is effective as of 2023-01-01 and applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH. People & Culture owns this policy and will communicate any updates through Grove, our intranet and policy library.
