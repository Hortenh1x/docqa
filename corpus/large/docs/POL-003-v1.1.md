# Workation Guideline

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we know that a change of scenery can spark fresh ideas and renewed energy. A workation — working temporarily from a location within the EU/EEA other than your usual workplace or home office — can be a wonderful way to combine work with a change of environment.

This guideline explains how you can make use of a workation, what to consider before you go, and how to get approval. It replaces version 1.0 of this policy. If you work remotely within your own country of employment, that arrangement is governed by POL-002; this guideline focuses on working from another EU/EEA country for a limited period.

We want you to enjoy your time away while staying compliant with tax and insurance rules, and while keeping collaboration with your team smooth. Please read this document carefully and reach out to People & Culture if anything is unclear.

## 2. How much workation is allowed

Workation is limited to 30 working days per calendar year within the EU/EEA. This means you can spend up to 30 working days per year working from another EU/EEA country, in addition to any remote work you already do within your home country under POL-002.

A single workation stay may not exceed 21 calendar days. If you wish to work from abroad for longer, please consider whether a different arrangement might suit your needs, and speak with your manager and People & Culture early.

Some practical points to keep in mind:

- Working days are counted as the days you would normally work, excluding weekends and public holidays.
- You may split your allowance into several shorter stays throughout the year.
- You cannot carry unused days into the next calendar year.
- A workation is not a holiday. Your regular working hours, availability, and responsibilities remain unchanged.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, plans to spend a few weeks with family in Portugal. She checks her calendar, confirms her customer meetings can be rescheduled or handled remotely, and submits a request. Her stay fits comfortably within the limits described above.

## 3. Approval

A workation requires approval from your manager before you travel. Please submit your request through Fern at least a few weeks in advance, so that your manager, your team, and People & Culture have enough time to review it.

When you request approval, please include:

- The destination country and the planned dates of your stay.
- Confirmation that your role can be performed from that location.
- A brief note on how you will stay connected with your team.

Your manager will consider team coverage, project deadlines, and any client-facing commitments. For roles that involve working with clients in specific regions, such as field solutions engineering, your manager may also check with Customer Success or Go-to-Market.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, requests a workation in Spain. His manager checks whether any on-site client visits are planned during that period. Since none are, the request is approved.

If your request is denied, your manager will explain the reasons and discuss alternatives. You can also reach out to People & Culture if you have concerns about the decision.

## 4. Tax and insurance

Working from another EU/EEA country can have implications for tax and social security, even for a short period. Kranich Software is committed to keeping you compliant, and we ask for your cooperation in return.

Before you travel, please notify People & Culture through your approval request. We will assess the specific situation based on your home country, the destination country, and the length of your stay. In some cases, we may need to involve external advisors or obtain certificates to confirm which country's social security system applies.

Insurance is another important consideration. Your usual health and accident coverage may not apply in the same way when you work from another country. Please check with People & Culture about what coverage is in place and whether any additional arrangements are needed for your destination.

Please also be aware that tax rules can vary by country and by individual circumstances. We cannot provide personal tax advice, but we will share relevant information and point you to resources when available.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to spend two weeks working from France. Before she books anything, she submits her request and notes her destination. People & Culture reviews the situation and confirms that her existing coverage is sufficient for the stay, and that no additional paperwork is needed.

If you are considering a workation in a country that is not your home country and not Germany or Portugal, please start the conversation early. Some destinations require more preparation than others, and we want to avoid any last-minute complications.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this guideline, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. For questions related to tax, insurance, or compliance, you may also contact security@kranich.example or it-help@kranich.example, depending on the nature of your query.

Your manager can also help you think through how a workation might fit with your team's rhythm and your current projects. We encourage you to have that conversation early, even before you submit a formal request.

We hope this guideline gives you clarity and confidence to plan a workation that works for you and for Kranich Software. A change of scenery can be refreshing, and we are happy to support you in doing it well.
