# Desk Booking How-To

## 1. Purpose

This document explains how to book desks and meeting rooms using Perch, our internal booking tool. It applies to everyone who works from our Berlin or Lisbon offices, whether you are based there or visiting from another country.

Booking a desk in advance helps your colleagues plan their days and keeps each office comfortable and well-organized. If you are unsure whether you need a desk, check Perch first — if a desk is free, you are welcome to take it.

## 2. Perch basics

Perch is available on the web and as a mobile app. You log in with your Kranich account. Once logged in, you see the floor plans for both offices and can browse availability by day.

To book a desk:

- Choose the office and the date you plan to be there.
- Pick a desk from the floor plan. Desks are marked as free, booked, or occupied.
- Confirm your booking. You will receive a confirmation in Perch.

Meeting rooms work the same way. When you book a room, add a short title so others know what the room is for. You can cancel or edit a booking at any time, and we ask that you do so promptly if your plans change.

If you are visiting an office for the first time, the Workplace & Operations team can show you around and help you set up Perch on your device. For access issues, contact it-help@kranich.example.

## 3. Etiquette

Booking a desk is a courtesy, not a requirement. Still, a few habits keep things pleasant for everyone:

- Book only the days you genuinely plan to be in the office. If your plans shift, free the desk so a colleague can use it.
- When you arrive, check in on Perch. This helps colleagues see who is actually present.
- Keep your area tidy. At the end of the day, clear your desk of personal items and waste.
- For meetings, book a room that fits the group. If your meeting grows or shrinks, update the booking.
- Respect quiet zones. Some areas are marked for focused work — please take calls and conversations elsewhere.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, books a desk for a day when she has client calls. Mid-morning, one call is cancelled, and she frees the desk for the afternoon because she plans to work from home. A colleague later books the same desk for the afternoon — everyone benefits.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer visiting from Poland, uses Perch to find a desk near the window in the Ritterstraße office. He checks in on arrival and leaves the desk clean at the end of the day.

If you notice a repeated issue with desk availability or cleanliness, please tell Workplace & Operations rather than confronting a colleague directly. We will follow up in a constructive way.
