# Performance Review FAQ

## 1. Questions

**When do performance reviews happen?**
Reviews follow the calendar year. Your manager will schedule a conversation with you in the weeks after the review period closes. See POL-004 for the full timeline.

**Who conducts my review?**
Your direct manager leads the review. If you have changed teams during the period, your previous manager contributes input. Ines Duarte, as your engineering manager, might also join if you work in Routing Core and your direct manager is unavailable.

**What does the process look like?**
You prepare a short self-reflection, your manager gathers feedback from colleagues you worked with closely, and the two of you meet to discuss achievements, challenges, and growth areas. The conversation ends with agreed focus points for the coming period.

**How is my performance rated?**
Ratings are based on the criteria in POL-004. Your manager considers what you delivered, how you collaborated, and how you developed. Ratings are calibrated across teams to ensure consistency.

**Will I see what my manager writes about me?**
Yes. The written summary is shared with you before the review conversation, so you have time to read it and prepare your questions.

**Can I add my own feedback to the record?**
Absolutely. Your self-reflection becomes part of the review record, and you can add comments to the final summary after the conversation if anything was missed or misunderstood.

**What happens after the review?**
Your manager confirms the outcome in writing. The review informs development conversations and, where relevant, salary adjustments. Any figures or rates are handled separately through the process described in FIN-RATES-2026 and POL-004.

**What if I disagree with my rating?**
First, discuss it with your manager. If the matter is not resolved, you can reach out to People & Culture via people@kranich.example. They will review the case confidentially and fairly.

**How does a review affect a promotion?**
A review is one input. Promotion decisions also consider role requirements, business need, and readiness, as outlined in POL-004. Your manager can explain the criteria during the review conversation.

**What if I joined recently?**
If you joined within the current review period, your review focuses on the time you have been with us. Your manager will adjust expectations accordingly.

**Example:** Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined Kranich midway through the year. Her review covered only the months since her start, and her manager noted that she ramped up quickly and contributed meaningfully to the routing service.

**Who do I contact with questions about my review?**
Start with your manager. For process questions, confidentiality concerns, or calibration questions, contact People & Culture. Lea, our people operations specialist, is happy to help you navigate the process.

**Are reviews confidential?**
Your review is visible to you, your manager, and People & Culture. Summary data may be used anonymously for company-wide learning, but individual details are shared only on a need-to-know basis.
