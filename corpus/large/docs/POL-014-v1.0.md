# Public Holidays & Bridge Days

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how public holidays are handled at Kranich Software GmbH and how bridge days work. It applies to all employees across our offices in Berlin and Lisbon and to everyone working remotely from Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom.

This version supersedes version 0.9 of this policy. We have updated it to clarify how bridge days are granted and to align the rules across the countries where we employ people.

We know that public holidays differ from country to country and even within a country. The intention of this policy is to be fair and transparent: wherever you work, you should be able to plan your time off with confidence and enjoy the same overall treatment.

## 2. Public holidays

Public holidays are the official holidays of the country where you are employed. Kranich follows the statutory public holiday calendar of your country of employment. If you work in Germany, the public holidays of the federal state where your workplace is located apply. If you work remotely from Spain, the public holidays recognised in your region apply.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, takes the public holidays of Berlin. Marek, who works with clients in Poland, takes the public holidays observed in Poland. Sofia, a product manager based remotely in Spain, follows the regional calendar where she lives.

Public holidays are paid days off. You do not need to submit a leave request for a public holiday in the system. Your manager and the People team will ensure that the calendar is kept up to date for each location.

If a public holiday falls on a day when you would not normally work, you do not receive an additional day in lieu. This is because the policy is based on the principle that public holidays are tied to the calendar, not to your individual working pattern.

If you work in a client-facing role and a public holiday falls on a day when you are scheduled to work, please discuss with your manager in advance how to handle the day. In most cases, we will arrange for you to take the day off on the public holiday itself or, where this is not possible due to client commitments, you may take a day off at another time. Please arrange this with your manager and record it appropriately.

## 3. Bridge days

A bridge day is a day that falls between a public holiday and a weekend. When a public holiday lands on a Tuesday or a Thursday, the Monday or the Friday in between can be taken as a bridge day so that you can enjoy a longer break without using your regular vacation days.

Kranich grants 3 days of bridge-day leave per year in addition to vacation. These days are available to all employees regardless of their country of employment. The bridge-day allowance is calculated on a calendar-year basis, from January to December.

You do not need to wait for a public holiday to be adjacent to a bridge day to use your allowance. You may take your bridge days at any point during the year, in agreement with your manager. This gives you flexibility, especially in countries where public holidays rarely fall on a Tuesday or Thursday.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, has a public holiday on a Thursday in June. She would like to take the Friday off to have a long weekend with her family. She requests the Friday as a bridge day using the same process as for vacation days. Her manager approves the request, and the day is deducted from her annual bridge-day allowance.

Bridge days are paid days off. They are separate from your vacation entitlement and from any statutory public holidays. You cannot carry unused bridge days over into the next year. Unused bridge days simply expire at the end of the calendar year. We encourage you to plan ahead and use them, because rest is part of doing good work.

When you request a bridge day, please do so with reasonable notice so that your team can plan accordingly. For people in client-facing roles, such as Customer Success or field solutions, please check with your manager that no critical client commitments are scheduled for that day. In most cases, a few days of notice is enough, but for longer breaks you should coordinate earlier with your team.

If you join Kranich in the middle of the year, your bridge-day allowance is not pro-rated. You receive the full annual allowance regardless of when you start. If you leave Kranich during the year, no deduction is made for bridge days you have already taken.

Bridge days are not considered when we calculate overtime or when we assess attendance for any purpose. They are a simple entitlement, and we trust you to use them sensibly.

## 4. Questions

If you have questions about public holidays in your location, please reach out to your manager or to the People team at people@kranich.example. We are happy to confirm which days are public holidays for you and how they affect your team's schedule.

If you have questions about bridge days, how to request them, or whether a specific day can be taken as a bridge day, please contact the People team first. They will confirm the rules and, if needed, coordinate with your manager.

For questions about how public holidays or bridge days interact with your leave record in Fern, please contact the People team as well. They can help you understand what you see in the system and how to record your time correctly.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, often receives questions from new joiners about whether they need to submit a request for public holidays. She explains that public holidays are pre-populated in the calendar and that only bridge days and vacation days require a request.

If you believe that a public holiday has been recorded incorrectly for your location, please raise this with the People team promptly. We update the calendars each year before the start of the calendar year, but occasionally a regional holiday changes or is added at short notice.

This policy is maintained by People & Culture. We review it periodically to make sure it remains clear and fair. If you have suggestions for improvement, please share them with us. We would rather hear from you than have you wonder.

For related information about leave and time off, please refer to the vacation policy and to the relevant sections of the employee handbook. Specific questions about how time off is handled in your team can be directed to your manager or to the People team at people@kranich.example.
