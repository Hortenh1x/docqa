# Parental Leave Policy

## 1. Purpose

Becoming a parent is a significant life event. Kranich Software GmbH wants every employee to take the time they need to care for their new child and to settle into their new family situation, without worrying about their job or their income. This policy explains what you can expect from us and what we ask of you in return.

This version of the policy supersedes version 1.0, which was in effect until June 30. We have updated the policy based on feedback from employees across our offices in Berlin and Lisbon, and from our remote colleagues in Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom. The changes reflect our commitment to making parental leave a straightforward, supportive experience for everyone.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of their location, their team, or whether they work from an office or remotely. It covers leave taken for the birth of a child or the placement of a child for adoption. Both parents and co-parents are entitled to the leave described here, and we treat all family structures equally.

The policy covers your time away from work, your pay during that time, and the practical arrangements for your return. It works alongside the statutory requirements of the country where you are employed. Where local law provides more generous rights than this policy, local law applies. Please speak with People & Culture if you are unsure how the rules in your country interact with this policy.

## 3. Telling us

We ask you to tell People & Culture about your planned leave as early as you can. Please tell People & Culture at least 7 weeks before the planned start of the leave. This gives us time to arrange cover for your work, to prepare your documentation, and to make sure everything is in order for your time away.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, told People & Culture as soon as he and his partner had a clear due date. That gave his team enough time to plan client visits and to hand over his ongoing projects without any rush.

When you tell us, please let us know the expected start date of your leave and how long you plan to be away. We understand that plans can change, and you can update us at any point. If the birth or placement happens earlier than expected, just let us know promptly and we will adjust the arrangements.

## 4. Leave and pay

You are entitled to parental leave in line with the statutory provisions of your country of employment. We encourage you to take the full leave available to you. Taking time to bond with your child and to support your family is important, and we do not want anyone to feel they have to rush back.

During your leave, you will receive pay according to the statutory scheme that applies in your country of employment. We will continue to pay your salary through the usual payroll process where the statutory scheme requires us to do so, and we will handle the necessary paperwork with the relevant authorities on your behalf.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, was unsure how her pay would work during her leave. Lea, a people operations specialist, explained the process step by step, confirmed what Deniz would receive and when, and took care of the application to the authorities. Deniz did not have to chase anything while she was on leave.

If you are eligible for additional company benefits during your leave, these will continue as normal. Your health insurance and any other benefits that are part of your standard employment package remain in place. If you have questions about your specific situation, please raise them with People & Culture before your leave starts.

## 5. Before the leave

Once you have told People & Culture about your leave, we will work with you and your manager to plan a smooth handover. Your manager will help you identify the key tasks that need to be covered while you are away and will arrange for colleagues to take over your responsibilities.

Please make sure your work is documented and that your colleagues know where to find the information they need. This includes updating your project notes, setting an out-of-office message, and making sure any ongoing conversations are handed over clearly. Your manager will support you in this and will make sure you are not overloaded with handover work in the weeks before your leave.

We also encourage you to talk to your manager about your plans for returning to work. You do not need to decide everything now, but it helps if your manager has a sense of whether you plan to return full time and roughly when. This allows the team to plan ahead while still leaving you flexibility.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, spent a few days before her leave writing up the state of her current project and recording short handover notes for her teammates. Her engineering manager, Ines Duarte, reviewed the notes with her and assigned a colleague to take over the project. Anna left for her leave knowing that everything was in good hands.

## 6. During the leave

While you are on parental leave, you are not expected to work or to be available for work. We will not contact you about operational matters, and we will not expect you to respond to messages. You should switch off and focus on your family.

You will still have access to your work email and to Grove, our intranet, if you wish to keep loosely informed about company news. Please feel free to ignore these entirely if you prefer. There is no requirement to check anything while you are away.

If there are significant changes at the company during your leave, such as a reorganisation or a change in leadership, we will let you know. We will send you a brief update so that you are not surprised when you return. You are welcome to stay in touch with your colleagues on a personal basis, but there is no expectation that you do so.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, chose to mute all work-related notifications during her leave. She kept in touch with a couple of close colleagues privately, but she did not open her work laptop once. When she returned, her team had everything ready for her and she did not feel she had missed anything important.

## 7. Returning to work

We look forward to welcoming you back. Before your return date, we will arrange a meeting between you, your manager, and a member of People & Culture. This meeting is a chance to catch up on what has changed while you were away, to discuss any adjustments you might need, and to plan your first weeks back.

You will return to the same role you had before your leave, or to a role that is broadly similar in level and responsibility. If your team has changed while you were away, we will make sure you are properly introduced and briefed.

If you wish to change your working pattern when you return, please discuss this with your manager and People & Culture as early as possible. We will consider any request seriously and will respond promptly. We want to find an arrangement that works for you and for the team.

Employees returning from parental leave may take carried-over vacation until June 30. This applies to vacation days that you accrued before your leave and could not take because you were away. Please plan your return with this in mind, and coordinate with your manager to book any time off before that date.

Example: Deniz returned from her leave in the spring. She had accrued vacation days during her time away, and she arranged with her manager to take them in June so that she could ease back into work gradually. Her manager supported the plan, and the team covered her duties for those days.

## 8. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, please contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, our people operations specialist, and her colleagues are happy to help you understand your entitlements and to guide you through the process.

For questions about how your leave interacts with your employment contract or local law in your country, People & Culture can also point you to the right resources. If you have concerns about your privacy or about how your personal data is handled during your leave, please see the relevant sections of POL-004 or contact our Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar, at security@kranich.example.

We want your parental leave to be a positive experience. Please reach out at any point, before, during, or after your leave, and we will do our best to support you.
