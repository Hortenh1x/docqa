# Grievance Procedure

## 1. Purpose

We want Kranich Software to be a place where everyone feels heard, respected, and able to raise concerns without fear. This procedure explains how you can bring forward a grievance — a formal complaint about your work, your working conditions, or how you have been treated by a colleague or manager — and how we will handle it.

A grievance is different from a general question or a casual comment. It is a formal step you take when you believe something is wrong and you want it to be looked into properly. This procedure is here to make that step clear and safe for you.

We take every grievance seriously. Raising one will not affect your employment or your standing in the team. Retaliation against anyone who raises a grievance in good faith is itself a serious violation of our values and will be dealt with accordingly.

## 2. Who this is for

This procedure applies to everyone employed by Kranich Software GmbH — in Berlin, in Lisbon, and across our remote workforce in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom. It covers grievances about:

- your work tasks or workload;
- your working environment, including remote working conditions;
- the behaviour of a colleague, a manager, or a team;
- decisions that affect you personally, such as performance reviews or role changes;
- anything else that makes you feel you are being treated unfairly at work.

If you are unsure whether your concern counts as a grievance, reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. We would rather you raise something with us than stay silent.

## 3. Process

**Step one — try an informal conversation first, if you feel able.** Many concerns are resolved quickly when the people involved talk openly. If the issue is with a colleague, consider speaking with them directly or with your manager. If the issue is with your manager, you can speak with their manager or with People & Culture. This step is optional — you are never required to approach someone you feel uncomfortable with.

**Step two — raise the grievance formally.** If the informal conversation does not resolve the matter, or if you prefer to go straight to a formal process, put your grievance in writing. Send it to people@kranich.example or to your People Partner directly. Your written grievance should describe:

- what happened;
- when it happened, as best you can recall;
- who was involved;
- why you feel it is a problem;
- what outcome you would like to see.

You do not need to use any special format or legal language. A plain description in your own words is enough.

**Step three — acknowledgement.** A member of People & Culture will acknowledge your grievance promptly, normally within a few days of receiving it. We will confirm who will handle your case and explain what happens next.

**Step four — investigation.** We will look into your grievance fairly and thoroughly. This usually involves speaking with you again, speaking with anyone else involved, and reviewing relevant documents or records. We will keep the scope of the investigation proportionate to the concern you raised. Where appropriate, we will keep you informed of progress.

**Step five — outcome.** Once the investigation is complete, we will share the outcome with you in writing. The outcome will set out what we found and any actions we will take. Actions may include changes to working arrangements, further training, mediation, or, in serious cases, disciplinary measures under the relevant policy. We will not share confidential details about other employees beyond what is necessary for you to understand the outcome.

**Step six — appeal.** If you are not satisfied with the outcome, you may appeal. Send your appeal in writing to people@kranich.example within a reasonable time of receiving the outcome. Your appeal will be reviewed by someone who was not involved in the original investigation, usually a senior member of People & Culture or another leader. They will consider whether the process was fair and whether the outcome was reasonable.

Throughout the process, you may be accompanied by a colleague or a people representative of your choice. You are also welcome to contact the Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar, at security@kranich.example if your grievance involves a matter of data protection or information security, and the relevant parts of POL-004 will apply alongside this procedure.

## 4. Timelines

We aim to move through the steps efficiently, but we will not rush an investigation at the expense of fairness. As a guide:

- acknowledgement normally comes within a few days;
- the investigation is usually completed within a few weeks, though complex matters may take longer;
- the outcome is shared as soon as the investigation is complete;
- appeals are reviewed promptly after we receive them.

If any step will take longer than we would like, we will tell you and explain why. We will also tell you if there is a delay caused by someone's absence or by the need to gather information from outside the company. You can ask for an update at any time by writing to people@kranich.example.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, felt that her manager had dismissed her concerns about an unrealistic project deadline during a team meeting. She first spoke with her manager informally, but the conversation did not change anything. Anna then wrote a short email to People & Culture describing what had happened. A People Partner acknowledged her email within a few days, spoke with Anna and her manager separately, and reviewed the project planning notes. The outcome acknowledged that the deadline had been set without proper consultation and adjusted the team's planning approach. Anna felt the process was fair and appreciated being kept informed throughout.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, raised a grievance about a colleague who had made dismissive comments about her accent in a client call. Deniz did not feel comfortable approaching the colleague directly, so she went straight to the formal process. The investigation confirmed the comments had been made. The colleague received feedback and coaching, and Deniz received an apology. Deniz was satisfied with the outcome and did not appeal.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this procedure, or about whether a concern you have is appropriate for a formal grievance, please contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. You can also speak with your manager, your People Partner, or any member of the leadership team.

If you are uncomfortable raising a matter through People & Culture for any reason, you can speak with the VP People & Culture, Aylin Demir, directly. For matters involving suspected misconduct by a member of People & Culture itself, please raise your concern with the CEO, Marta Lindqvist, or the CFO, Tomasz Zieliński.

We also remind you that this grievance procedure is separate from other channels you may use. If you have a concern about your safety or about illegal activity, you should follow the appropriate reporting path rather than waiting for this process. For questions about which channel fits your situation, reach out to us and we will help you find the right route.

Finally, please remember that this procedure is not a substitute for everyday communication. If you have a suggestion, a question, or a small concern, please raise it early — with your manager, your team, or People & Culture. Most issues are easier to resolve while they are small, and we are always glad to listen.
