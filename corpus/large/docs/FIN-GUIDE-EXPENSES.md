# Expense Reports FAQ

## 1. Purpose

This guide answers the most common questions we hear about expense reports at Kranich Software. It is meant to complement, not replace, the formal policy in POL-004. If you notice a conflict between this FAQ and that policy, the policy wins.

We want the expense process to be simple: you spend money on approved business needs, you submit a report in Ledgerly, and Finance reimburses you promptly. Most questions fall into one of three areas: what to submit, what to attach, and when to do it. We cover all three below.

## 2. Submitting

**When should I submit an expense report?**
Submit as you go. It is much easier to file a few small reports than to reconstruct a month of spending from memory. A good habit is to log each expense in Ledgerly on the day it happens and submit the report as soon as your trip or activity is complete.

**What belongs in an expense report?**
Business travel, client meetings, approved software, and other costs you incur for your work. If you are unsure whether something qualifies, ask your manager or Finance before you spend. The general rule: if you would not have incurred the cost without your role at Kranich, it is probably reimbursable.

**What does not belong?**
Personal items, upgrades that are not business-related, and anything already covered by a company arrangement. When in doubt, leave it out and ask.

**Do I need pre-approval?**
For routine expenses, no. For larger or unusual purchases, check with your manager first. When in doubt, a quick message is cheaper than a rejected report.

**Example:** Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, takes a client to lunch after a workshop. She logs the meal in Ledgerly the same day and submits her report at the end of the week. The report includes the purpose of the meeting and the client's name. That is enough.

## 3. Receipts

**Do I need a receipt for everything?**
Yes, for every expense, no matter how small. A receipt is your proof that the cost happened and that it was what you say it was. Without a receipt, Finance cannot verify the report, and the report will be returned to you.

**What counts as a receipt?**
A clear photo or scan of the original receipt, or a digital receipt from the merchant. The receipt should show the date, the merchant, the items, and the total. If any of that is missing, ask the merchant for a corrected receipt.

**What if I lose a receipt?**
Contact the merchant and ask for a duplicate. If you genuinely cannot get one, explain the situation in the report notes. Finance will decide case by case. A missing receipt does not automatically mean rejection, but it does slow things down.

**How should I store receipts?**
Attach them to the expense in Ledgerly at the moment you log it. Do not keep paper receipts in your wallet or a drawer — they get lost, faded, or forgotten. A photo taken on the spot takes seconds and saves you a headache later.

**Example:** Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, buys train tickets for a client visit. The ticket machine gives him a paper receipt. He photographs it immediately and attaches the photo to the expense in Ledgerly before he boards the train. When he submits his report at the end of the week, everything is already in order.

## 4. Timelines

**How quickly should I submit?**
Promptly. The longer you wait, the harder it is to remember the business purpose and the more likely you are to lose a receipt. A good target is to submit within a few days of the expense.

**What happens after I submit?**
Your report goes to your manager for approval, then to Finance for review. If something is missing or unclear, someone will reach out to you. You can track the status in Ledgerly at any time.

**When will I be reimbursed?**
The deadline for reimbursement is set out in POL-004 §6. Reimbursement is paid through Ledgerly to your bank account. If you have not received payment by the time described in the policy, check the status in Ledgerly first, then contact Finance.

**What if I submit late?**
Late submissions are handled case by case. The most important thing is to communicate: if you know you are behind, tell Finance. Silence is the only real problem.

**Example:** Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, travels to Berlin for a planning week. She returns on a Friday and submits her report the following Monday. She attached all receipts as she went, so the report is complete. Finance processes it without any back-and-forth.

## 5. Questions

**Who do I ask?**
Start with Ledgerly's help section or your manager. For policy questions, refer to POL-004. For anything about a specific report, contact Finance through Ledgerly, and someone will get back to you.

**What about rates and limits?**
Per-diem rates and category limits are published separately in FIN-RATES-2026. Do not rely on memory or on what a colleague told you last year — check the current rates before you travel or spend.

**Can I submit an expense for a colleague?**
No. Each person submits their own report. If you paid for something together, split it clearly and each person submits their own share with their own receipts.

**What if I disagree with a decision?**
Talk to Finance first. Most issues are simple misunderstandings — a missing receipt, an unclear purpose, or a category that was applied incorrectly. If you still disagree after that conversation, your manager can help escalate it.

**Example:** Sofia, a product manager in Spain, buys a book for research related to a new Fleet Insights feature. She is unsure whether it counts as a business expense, so she asks Finance before buying. They confirm it does, and she submits the report with the receipt and a note about the feature. No surprises for anyone.
