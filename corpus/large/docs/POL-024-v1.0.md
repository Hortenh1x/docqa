# Vendor & SaaS Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software relies on a growing number of external vendors and software-as-a-service tools to run the business. From our internal collaboration platforms to the cloud infrastructure behind Kranich Route Cloud, every tool we adopt carries cost, risk, and administrative overhead. This policy explains how we evaluate, manage, and retire those relationships responsibly.

This version supersedes version 0.9 of this policy. It reflects how we have matured as a company since we first introduced formal vendor management, and it aligns with the way Finance, Platform & Infrastructure, and Information Security now work together. If you are familiar with the earlier version, the main changes are a clearer process for adding tools, a firmer rule on renewals, and a defined path for offboarding.

We wrote this policy for everyone at Kranich Software. Whether you are a backend engineer in Lisbon who needs a new development library, a customer success manager in Berlin who wants to try a new analytics dashboard, or a product manager in Spain who has used the same project management tool for years — this policy applies to you. It is not meant to slow you down. It is meant to make sure that every tool we pay for is necessary, secure, and properly managed.

## 2. Adding a tool

Before you add any new vendor or SaaS tool, stop and ask whether an existing tool already covers the need. Check Grove for our current tool inventory and ask your team lead or your department head. Duplicating functionality is the most common way we end up with unused licenses and fragmented data.

If no existing tool fits, the next step is to request approval. Send a brief note to your manager and to Finance describing the tool, what problem it solves, and roughly how many people would use it. Do not sign up for a free trial or a paid plan on your own. Even free tools can create data-security obligations, and a trial that starts informally often becomes a permanent expense.

For tools that will handle company data, Information Security must review the vendor first. This review looks at how the vendor stores and protects data, where the data is hosted, and whether the vendor's security practices meet our standards. The review is not a formality. If a vendor cannot meet our requirements, we will not use them, regardless of how convenient the tool might seem.

Once the security review passes and Finance approves the budget, Platform & Infrastructure will set up the tool through our central administration. This means the tool is added to our inventory, users are provisioned through the proper channels, and we have a single record of what we pay and when the contract ends. You will receive login credentials through the approved process. Never create a shadow account or pay for a tool with a personal credit card.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, found a useful code-quality monitoring service at a conference. Before she shared it with her team, she checked Grove, saw that we already use a similar tool, and raised the question with her engineering manager. They decided the existing tool was sufficient, and Anna saved the team the trouble of onboarding a redundant vendor.

## 3. Renewals and notice

Every vendor contract and SaaS subscription has a renewal date. Your responsibility does not end when a tool is set up. If you are the primary user or the person who requested the tool, you are responsible for knowing when the contract renews and for reviewing whether you still need it.

Renewals must be reviewed at least 60 days before the notice deadline. This is not a suggestion. Many vendors require notice well in advance if you do not wish to renew, and missing that window can lock us into another term automatically. Finance maintains a calendar of all renewal dates and notice deadlines. Finance will send you a reminder when a renewal is approaching, but you should not rely on that reminder alone. Mark the date in your own calendar and plan your review accordingly.

When you review a renewal, ask yourself a few questions. Are people actually using the tool? Is there a cheaper plan that covers our needs? Has the vendor changed its pricing or its terms since we signed up? Has our usage grown or shrunk in a way that suggests we should change tiers? If you no longer need the tool, say so clearly. If you are unsure, ask your team. It is better to cancel and re-add later than to pay for another year of something nobody uses.

If you want to renew, you do not need to do anything special beyond confirming to Finance that the tool is still needed. Finance will handle the administrative side. If you want to change the plan, negotiate the price, or cancel, contact Finance as early as possible. Do not communicate directly with the vendor about contract terms unless Finance has asked you to. We have more leverage when we negotiate as a company, and we want to avoid accidental commitments.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, received a reminder from Finance that a client-communication tool was up for renewal. She checked usage reports, saw that her team had barely logged in over the past several months, and told Finance she wanted to cancel. Because she responded well before the notice deadline, Finance was able to terminate the contract without penalty.

## 4. Offboarding a vendor

When a tool is no longer needed, or when we decide to replace a vendor, we offboard properly. Offboarding is more than cancelling the subscription. It means removing access, retrieving our data, and making sure we have no lingering obligations.

The first step is to notify Finance and Platform & Infrastructure. They will coordinate the offboarding and tell you what information they need from you. As the person who knows the tool best, you should be prepared to explain how the tool is used, who has access, and where data lives.

Before the subscription is cancelled, Platform & Infrastructure will export any data we need to keep. This might include historical records, configuration files, or documents that we are required to retain for legal or business reasons. If you are not sure whether data needs to be kept, ask. Once the export is confirmed, Platform & Infrastructure will deprovision all user accounts and revoke any integrations with other tools. This step matters for security: an inactive account on a vendor platform is a risk we do not want to carry.

After access is removed, Finance will cancel the subscription and confirm that the vendor will not charge us again. Finance will also update the vendor inventory and note the end of the relationship. If the vendor held any of our data, we will request its deletion in line with our data-protection obligations.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, used a route-visualization add-on that was being replaced by a feature in Kranich Route Cloud. He told his manager, who looped in Finance and Platform & Infrastructure. Marek helped identify which client projects had used the tool, Platform & Infrastructure exported the relevant maps and notes, and Finance cancelled the subscription. Within a few days, the vendor was fully offboarded and removed from our inventory.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager or your department head. For questions about a specific contract or renewal, contact Finance. For questions about security reviews or data handling, contact security@kranich.example. For questions about the tool inventory or how to request a new tool, contact it-help@kranich.example.

If you are unsure whether a tool needs to go through this process, ask. It is always better to check before you commit to something than to untangle an unapproved subscription later. We want you to have the tools you need to do your best work, and we want to manage our vendor relationships in a way that is transparent, secure, and financially sound.
