# Performance Review Policy

*Superseded by version 2.0.*

## 1. Purpose

Performance reviews help us grow as individuals and as a company. They are a time to reflect on what we have accomplished, what we have learned, and where we want to go next. This policy describes how performance reviews work at Kranich Software GmbH.

We believe that feedback is a gift. Reviews are not a one-way conversation from manager to employee. They are an opportunity for everyone — engineers, customer success managers, product managers, and leaders alike — to understand how their work lands, what supports them, and what they need to thrive.

This policy applies to all employees, regardless of their role, team, or location. Whether you work from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from home in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom, the same principles apply.

Our review process is designed to be fair, transparent, and useful. It is not a ranking exercise. It is not about catching anyone out. It is about alignment: between your goals and your team’s goals, between your development and the company’s needs, and between your experience at Kranich and the impact you want to make.

## 2. Review cycle

Performance reviews happen once per calendar year. The review period covers the preceding calendar year, from January through December. People managers receive training before each cycle begins so they can run thoughtful, consistent conversations.

The cycle follows a clear rhythm:

- **Self-reflection.** Each employee prepares a short written reflection on their year. This is their chance to highlight achievements, challenges, and lessons learned.
- **Peer feedback.** Each employee invites a few colleagues to share feedback. These colleagues should represent the people they work with most closely — teammates, cross-functional partners, or people they support. Feedback is collected anonymously and shared in a summarized form.
- **Manager review.** Each people manager reviews the self-reflection and peer feedback, then prepares their own assessment of the employee’s year.
- **Review conversation.** Manager and employee meet to discuss the review. This conversation is the heart of the process. It should feel like a dialogue, not a verdict.
- **Written summary.** The manager writes a brief summary of the conversation, including agreed next steps. The employee receives a copy and is invited to add their own comments.

Managers schedule the review conversation at a time that works for both parties. We ask that conversations happen within a few weeks of the feedback collection closing, so the material is fresh and momentum is kept.

New joiners participate in the cycle if they have been with Kranich for at least part of the review period. Employees who join near the end of the year still have a conversation with their manager, but the focus is on onboarding, early wins, and setting goals for the year ahead rather than on a full performance assessment.

Employees who leave Kranich during the year do not receive a formal performance review. Instead, their manager conducts an exit conversation to capture learnings and to ensure a smooth handover of responsibilities.

## 3. Ratings

Each employee receives an overall rating for the review period. Ratings are meant to summarize, in plain terms, where someone stands relative to the expectations of their role. They are not a measure of personal worth, and they are not a competition between colleagues.

The rating scale has five levels:

- **Outstanding.** The employee consistently exceeded expectations in their role. Their work had a visible, positive impact on their team and on the company. They are a role model for others.
- **Exceeds expectations.** The employee regularly went beyond what was asked. They delivered strong results and showed initiative in areas that mattered.
- **Meets expectations.** The employee fully delivered on the responsibilities of their role. This is a good outcome — it means the employee is doing the job well and reliably.
- **Partially meets expectations.** The employee met some expectations but fell short in specific areas. This rating comes with a clear development plan and regular check-ins to support improvement.
- **Does not meet expectations.** The employee did not meet the core expectations of their role. This rating triggers a structured improvement process, managed jointly by People & Culture and the employee’s manager.

Most employees will receive a rating of “Meets expectations” or “Exceeds expectations.” That is by design. We hire capable people, and we expect them to do capable work.

Ratings are not linked to any fixed distribution. We do not force a certain number of people into any category. Instead, ratings reflect the honest assessment of the manager, informed by peer feedback and the employee’s own reflection.

The rating is shared during the review conversation. The manager explains the reasoning clearly, with examples from the review period. The employee is encouraged to ask questions and to push back if they disagree. The final rating is confirmed only after the conversation has taken place.

## 4. Calibration

Calibration is the process that ensures ratings are fair and consistent across teams. Without calibration, one manager might be a generous rater while another is strict, and the outcome would depend more on who you sit next to than on how you work.

After managers complete their initial assessments, they come together in calibration sessions. These sessions are facilitated by People & Culture and include managers from related teams. The goal is to discuss each employee’s rating, share context, and adjust where needed.

Calibration sessions are confidential. Managers discuss the substance of an employee’s performance — achievements, challenges, feedback — but the conversation stays in the room. No written notes from calibration sessions are stored in an employee’s file.

During calibration, managers are asked to consider:

- **Consistency.** Would a similar level of achievement receive a similar rating in another team?
- **Context.** Did the employee face unusual circumstances, such as a major re-organization or a difficult project?
- **Growth.** Has the employee shown meaningful development over the year?
- **Impact.** How did the employee’s work contribute to team and company goals?

Calibration is not a popularity contest. It is not a place to argue for a higher rating because someone is well-liked. It is a place to test assumptions and to ensure that we are applying the same standards across the company.

When an adjustment is proposed, the manager who owns the review explains the rationale. The group discusses, and a decision is made. The final rating is then communicated to the employee by their own manager.

Employees who are rated “Partially meets expectations” or “Does not meet expectations” receive additional attention during calibration. People & Culture reviews these cases individually to ensure that the assessment is fair and that the employee will receive the right support going forward.

If an employee disagrees with their final rating, they may raise the concern with their manager first. If the concern is not resolved, they may escalate to People & Culture. Escalations are reviewed promptly and with care.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, please reach out. Your first point of contact is always your people manager. They can explain how the process works in practice and what to expect in your specific situation.

For questions about ratings, calibration, or the overall process, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. The People Operations team, including Lea in Berlin, is happy to help.

For questions about how your review data is stored or protected, contact the Information Security team at security@kranich.example. Priya and her team can explain what data we keep, why we keep it, and who has access.

For practical questions about scheduling or tools, contact IT support at it-help@kranich.example. They can help with any technical issues related to the review platform or your calendar.

We also welcome feedback on this policy itself. If something felt unclear, unfair, or missing from your review experience, please tell us. We review this policy regularly and we want it to serve everyone at Kranich well.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, felt that the peer feedback step did not capture the work she did with colleagues outside her immediate team. She raised this with her manager during the review conversation. Her manager agreed and noted it as a point to raise in the next policy review. Deniz also sent a note to people@kranich.example with her suggestion. The People Operations team acknowledged her input within a few days and confirmed that the feedback collection step would be revisited.

Remember: performance reviews are a shared responsibility. Managers prepare, employees participate, and the company listens. When we all engage honestly and generously, reviews become one of the most valuable things we do together each year.
