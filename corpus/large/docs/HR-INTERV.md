# Interview Guide
Access: Managers only

## 1. Purpose

This guide helps managers conduct thoughtful, consistent interviews for roles across Kranich Software. It describes how to prepare, what to cover, and how to evaluate candidates fairly. It complements the policies in POL-004 and the framework referenced there.

We want every candidate to leave with a clear sense of our culture and the work ahead of them. We also want every hiring decision to rest on evidence, not impression.

## 2. Who this is for

This guide is for managers who interview candidates for positions reporting to them, and for any team lead who joins an interview panel. If you are new to interviewing at Kranich, review this guide before your first session and reach out to People & Culture with questions.

People & Culture supports you throughout. They coordinate scheduling, share candidate materials, and can join a session if you prefer company.

## 3. Process

### Preparing for the interview

Read the job description and the candidate's application materials before the session. Note what you want to verify and what you are curious about. Prepare your questions in advance; avoid improvising the core part of the session.

Decide who leads the interview. For panel sessions, agree on roles beforehand: one person guides the conversation, others observe and take notes. Keep the tone warm and professional throughout.

### During the interview

Introduce yourself and explain the structure of the session. Tell the candidate how much time you have and what you hope to cover. Leave room for their questions.

Ask open questions that invite concrete examples. Follow up with gentle probing: what did you do, what did you learn, what would you change. Listen more than you speak.

Take notes during or immediately after the session. Record observations, not judgments. Write what the candidate said and did, not your impression of their personality.

### After the interview

Submit your notes and recommendation through the internal hiring tool within a few days. Be specific about strengths and concerns. Reference evidence from the session rather than general feelings.

If you have concerns, raise them early. It is easier to explore a question while the conversation is fresh than to revisit it later. If a candidate did not meet expectations, say so clearly and explain why.

### Evaluation principles

Evaluate each candidate against the same criteria for the role. Compare candidates on those criteria, not on how similar they are to you. Avoid letting one strong moment or one weak moment define your view of the whole session.

Be mindful of unconscious bias. Focus on what the candidate can do and has done, not on where they are from, how they speak, or what they studied. If you notice a pattern in your own reactions, name it and adjust.

## 4. Timelines

Start the process with a clear sense of how quickly you need to fill the role. This shapes how many candidates you see and how you pace decisions.

Aim to keep the candidate informed at every step. After each stage, tell them what happens next and when they can expect to hear from us. Promptness matters: a delayed response reflects on the whole company.

Coordinate with People & Culture on scheduling. They manage the candidate's experience across stages and can help if conflicts arise.

If you need to move quickly, say so. If you need more time, say that too. Clarity helps everyone plan.

## 5. Questions

Use the following areas as a starting point. Adapt them to the role and level. You do not need to ask every question; choose what matters most for the position.

### Role-specific questions

Ask about the candidate's relevant experience. Invite them to describe a recent project or task that resembles the work they would do here.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, interviewing a candidate for her team, asks about a system the candidate designed. She follows up on trade-offs and how the candidate handled constraints.

### Behavioral questions

Ask for specific situations from the candidate's past. Use prompts like: tell me about a time when, describe a situation where, give me an example of.

Listen for what the candidate did, not just what happened around them. Clarify their personal contribution and the outcome.

### Collaboration questions

Ask how the candidate works with others. Explore how they handle disagreement, how they give and receive feedback, and how they support teammates.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, asks a candidate how they would respond to a client who is frustrated. She listens for empathy and problem-solving, not scripted answers.

### Motivation questions

Ask why the candidate is interested in this role and in Kranich. Explore what they value in a workplace and what they hope to learn.

Listen for alignment with our culture. We are remote-first and collaborative. We value clarity, kindness, and ownership.

### Questions the candidate asks you

Leave time for the candidate's questions. Answer honestly, even when the answer is not flattering. If you do not know, say so and offer to find out.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, is asked about travel expectations. He describes the rhythm of client work without overstating it.

### Closing the session

Summarize the next steps and thank the candidate for their time. Let them know who they will hear from and when. If you are unsure of the timeline, say you will follow up shortly and do so.
