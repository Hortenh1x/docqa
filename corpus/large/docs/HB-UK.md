# Employee Handbook — United Kingdom

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of our team. This handbook explains the essentials of working with us from the United Kingdom. It covers your employment terms, vacation, working hours, benefits, and the practical details of daily life at Kranich.

We are a remote-first company. Most of us work from home within our country of employment. We build route-optimization software for mid-size logistics operators, and we take pride in solving hard problems together. You will find that our culture is professional, warm, and plain-spoken.

Please read this handbook alongside the policies referenced here. If something is unclear, reach out to People & Culture or your manager. We want you to feel supported from your first day.

## 2. Your employment in United Kingdom

Your employment with Kranich Software GmbH is governed by the laws of the United Kingdom and by your individual employment contract. This handbook summarises the main terms and points you to the detailed policies that apply.

You are employed through our entity in the United Kingdom. Your contract sets out your role, your reporting line, and your place of work. Most colleagues in the United Kingdom work remotely, but some work from a co-working space or from home on a permanent basis. Your contract will reflect your arrangement.

As an employee in the United Kingdom, you have statutory rights. Kranich builds on those rights with additional benefits, described in this handbook. Where there is a difference between a statutory right and a Kranich policy, the more favourable option for you applies, unless the law requires otherwise.

Your personal data is handled in line with our privacy policy and the data protection rules that apply in the United Kingdom. If you have questions about your data, contact privacy@kranich.example.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in United Kingdom receive 28 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This includes the statutory annual leave you are entitled to under UK law, plus additional Kranich leave. The full amount is available for you to plan and take across the leave year.

You accrue vacation from your first day of employment. You should request vacation through Fern, our HRIS. Your manager will approve requests, and we ask you to plan your leave with your team so that we continue to serve our customers well.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, plans a long weekend in the spring. She submits her request in Fern three weeks ahead. Her manager approves it, and Deniz updates her calendar so her colleagues know when she is away.

You may carry over a limited amount of unused vacation into the next leave year if your manager agrees. Unused vacation beyond what the law allows you to carry is lost at the end of the leave year, unless you were unable to take it due to sickness or another approved reason. Please speak to People & Culture if you have questions about carry-over.

When you leave Kranich, you will be paid for any accrued but untaken vacation that you are entitled to under UK law. The calculation follows the statutory rules and your contract.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in United Kingdom is 2 months. During this time, you and Kranich get to know each other and confirm that the role is a good fit.

Your probation starts on your first working day. During probation, you have the same rights and benefits as any other employee, including vacation, sick leave, and access to our internal tools. There is no reduced pay or reduced benefit during probation.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, starts a new role at Kranich. During his first month, he meets his manager weekly to review progress and discuss any concerns. At the end of the second month, his manager confirms that Marek has successfully completed probation.

Your manager will give you feedback during probation. If there are concerns, your manager will raise them early and give you a chance to improve. If Kranich decides that the employment should not continue, you will be given notice in line with your contract and the law.

You may also decide during probation that the role is not right for you. In that case, you can resign by giving the notice period set out in your contract.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in United Kingdom is 12 weeks. This applies both when you resign and when Kranich gives you notice, unless your contract states a different period for a specific situation.

Notice must be given in writing. You should send your resignation to your manager and to People & Culture. Your last working day will be calculated from the date the notice is received, in line with your contract.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, decides to move to another company. She sends her resignation to her manager and to People & Culture on a Monday. Her notice period starts that day, and her last working day falls twelve weeks later, as set out in her contract.

During your notice period, you remain an employee with the same rights and responsibilities. You should continue to perform your duties and hand over your work in an orderly way. Your manager may ask you to use some of your notice period for handover activities or to be released from work earlier, in which case you will be paid for the remainder of the notice period.

If Kranich terminates your employment for serious misconduct, we may dismiss you without notice, in line with UK law.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in United Kingdom observe 12 days of public holidays in the reference year. These are the days when the country marks public holidays, and Kranich gives you these days as paid leave.

The public holidays in the United Kingdom vary slightly depending on where you live, because Scotland and Northern Ireland observe some different days. Kranich follows the public holidays for England and Wales, which are the same across the country for most employees. If your contract says otherwise, the contract prevails.

You do not need to request public holidays in Fern. They are already marked as non-working days in your calendar. If a public holiday falls on a weekend, you are not automatically entitled to a substitute day off, unless your contract says so. Please check with People & Culture if you have questions.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Routing Core who lives in Lisbon, is on a project with colleagues in the United Kingdom. She notices that a public holiday in the United Kingdom falls on a Thursday. Her UK colleagues are off that day, and Anna plans her own work accordingly.

If you are asked to work on a public holiday, your manager will agree this with you in advance. You will receive time off in lieu or another arrangement, in line with your contract and UK law.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in United Kingdom is 40.0 hours per week. This is the baseline for full-time employees. Your contract states your working hours, and you should organise your time to meet the needs of your role.

Kranich is remote-first, and we trust you to manage your time. We do not track your hours minute by minute. Instead, we focus on outcomes and on being available for your team when needed. You should agree your core availability with your manager, especially if you work across time zones.

The Working Time Regulations in the United Kingdom set limits on working time and rest breaks. Kranich respects these rules. You should take regular breaks during the day and rest between working days. If you regularly work more than your contractual hours, speak to your manager — this is usually a sign that your workload needs adjusting.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Routing Core in Berlin, has a team member in the United Kingdom. They agree that the team member is available between mid-morning and late afternoon UK time, with flexibility around that for deep work. Ines makes sure meetings are scheduled within that window.

If your role requires you to be on call or to work outside your normal hours occasionally, your manager will discuss this with you in advance. Any such arrangement will respect your rest periods and the limits in UK law.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are unwell and cannot work, you should let your manager know as soon as possible. You should also log your sick leave in Fern so that People & Culture and payroll have a record.

The first time you are sick, you do not need to provide a doctor's note. For longer absences, the sick-note rules are in POL-007. Please read that policy to understand when you need to provide evidence and how to submit it. POL-007 also explains what happens if you are sick repeatedly over a period.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, wakes up with a fever on a Tuesday. He messages his manager before his first meeting and logs the sick day in Fern. He rests for two days and returns to work on Thursday. No doctor's note is needed for such a short absence.

During sick leave, you continue to receive your normal pay, in line with UK law and your contract. If your absence is longer, you may be entitled to statutory sick pay, and Kranich will support you through the process. People & Culture can help you understand what applies.

If you are sick while on vacation, you can ask to have those days converted to sick leave. You will need to follow the same evidence rules as for any other sick leave. Please contact People & Culture to arrange this.

## 9. Remote work in United Kingdom

Kranich is remote-first. You can work from home or from another location in the United Kingdom, as long as you have a suitable internet connection and a quiet place to work. Your contract states your primary country of employment, and you should work from within the United Kingdom unless you have agreed otherwise.

We provide equipment for your home office, including a laptop, a monitor, and other items you need. If you need something additional, contact it-help@kranich.example. We also offer a budget for home office setup, which you can use in your first months of employment.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Routing Core, lives in Lisbon and works remotely. She has a dedicated room in her flat that she uses as an office. When she joined, she ordered a desk chair and a second monitor through the home office budget, and received them within a few weeks.

You should keep your home workspace safe and comfortable. Kranich follows the UK guidance on home working, and you can find more details on the Grove intranet. If you have concerns about your workspace, speak to your manager or to Workplace & Operations.

When you travel within the United Kingdom for work, you can expense reasonable costs through Ledgerly. This includes transport and accommodation when you need to be away from home. Please follow the expense policy in Ledgerly and keep your receipts.

## 10. Benefits in United Kingdom

Kranich offers a benefits package for employees in the United Kingdom that goes beyond the statutory minimum. The details are in your contract and in the benefits portal. This section gives you an overview.

You are enrolled in a pension scheme. Kranich makes contributions on top of your salary, and you can choose to contribute more if you wish. The default contribution rates are set out in the scheme documents, and you can change your contribution level at any time through the provider.

You have access to private medical insurance. This covers you for a range of treatments and is arranged through our provider. You can also add family members at your own cost. The policy documents explain what is covered and how to make a claim.

You receive life assurance and income protection. These provide financial support to you or your family in the event of serious illness or death. The scheme documents describe the coverage in detail.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, recently used her private medical insurance to see a specialist for a persistent shoulder problem. She booked an appointment through the provider's app, and the consultation was covered without any additional cost to her.

You also have access to an employee assistance programme. This is a confidential service that offers counselling and practical support for personal or work-related issues. You can contact the service directly, and the details are on the Grove intranet.

Some benefits are optional and you can opt out. If you do, you may receive a cash alternative where the law allows. Please speak to People & Culture if you want to understand your options.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly into your UK bank account. Payday is the last working day of each month. You will receive a payslip through Ledgerly, our expenses and payroll tool, on or before payday.

Kranich operates payroll in the United Kingdom through HM Revenue & Customs. This means that income tax and National Insurance contributions are deducted from your salary before you are paid. Your payslip shows the deductions clearly.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, receives her payslip at the end of each month. She checks that her tax code is correct and that her pension contribution has been deducted as she requested. If she has questions, she contacts People & Culture.

You should check your tax code on your payslip and inform People & Culture if it changes. Your tax code is set by HMRC based on your personal circumstances. If you have more than one job or other income, your tax code may need adjustment.

Your pension contributions are deducted from your gross salary and paid to the pension provider. You can see the contributions on your payslip and in the provider's portal.

If you have questions about your pay, tax, or National Insurance, contact People & Culture in the first instance. They can also put you in touch with our payroll provider if needed.
