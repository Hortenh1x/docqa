# Travel & Expense Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how to plan, book, and pay for business travel, and how to get reimbursed for expenses you incur while working for Kranich. We want travel to be safe, comfortable, and straightforward — without unnecessary cost to the company.

We trust you to use good judgment. When in doubt about whether a cost is appropriate, ask your manager or the Finance team before you spend. This policy applies to all employees, regardless of where you are based or how long you have been with us.

## 2. When to travel

Travel is part of many roles at Kranich, but it is not always required. Before you book anything, consider whether the trip is necessary. Ask yourself whether the goal can be achieved with a video call, a shared document, or another remote collaboration tool. If it can, skip the trip.

When travel is genuinely needed, we encourage you to plan it well in advance. Early planning usually means lower costs and more choice. We also ask you to respect your own energy: if you have back-to-back trips, speak with your manager about pacing. We would rather you arrive rested and effective than exhausted.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, needs to visit a client in Hamburg for a workshop. She checks the agenda and confirms with the client that an on-site session is necessary. The workshop cannot be held remotely because the client wants a hands-on demonstration with their fleet data. Deniz proceeds with booking.

## 3. Booking

Use the booking tools in Ledgerly for flights, trains, and accommodation. Ledgerly shows you the options that meet our travel guidelines and keeps your bookings linked to the correct project or client. If you book outside Ledgerly, you must still enter the details there promptly so that Finance can see your trip.

For ground transport, we prefer trains over flights for routes under 5 hours door to door. Train travel is often more productive and less stressful than flying, and it is usually better for the environment. If a train journey would take longer than that, you may choose a flight, but please pick the most direct option.

Choose standard economy class for flights and standard class for trains. We do not book premium cabins for routine travel. If you have a medical condition or another reason that requires more space, contact People & Culture to discuss an accommodation.

For accommodation, book a standard room in a mid-range hotel near your work location. We want you to be safe and comfortable, but we do not pay for luxury suites. When you travel with a colleague, we expect you to book separate rooms unless you both agree otherwise.

If you need a rental car, choose the smallest vehicle that fits your needs. We do not cover fuel for personal use of a rental vehicle. When you use your own car for business travel, you will be reimbursed at the standard mileage rate published in FIN-RATES-2026.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, needs to visit a client in Wrocław. The door-to-door train journey takes under 5 hours, so he books a train through Ledgerly. He selects a standard seat and a hotel near the client's office.

## 4. Per-diems

For meals and incidental expenses while traveling, we pay a fixed daily per-diem. You do not need to keep receipts for meals when you claim the per-diem. The per-diem covers your meals, local transport, and small incidental costs such as tips.

The per-diem rate depends on your destination and is published in FIN-RATES-2026. You receive the full per-diem for each day you are away from your home location, including travel days. If your employer provides a meal — for example, a client lunch or a conference dinner — you should not claim the per-diem for that meal. In that case, you may claim a reduced per-diem for the remaining meals of the day, as described in FIN-RATES-2026.

The per-diem is meant to simplify your life. You do not need to document every coffee or snack. If your actual meal costs are unusually high because of a special occasion, you may claim the actual cost instead of the per-diem, but you must attach itemized receipts and explain why.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, travels to Berlin for a team gathering. She receives the per-diem for each day of her trip. On the day the team has a group dinner paid by the company, she claims the reduced per-diem for that day.

## 5. What is reimbursable

We reimburse reasonable and necessary expenses that you incur while on approved business travel. This includes:

- Transportation to and from the airport or train station, including taxis or ride-sharing when public transport is not practical.
- Baggage fees for one checked bag, if you need it for the trip.
- Work-related phone calls and internet access, such as hotel Wi-Fi.
- Laundry services for trips longer than a few days.
- Visas and other entry documents required for the trip.

We do not reimburse personal expenses, such as movies, minibar items, personal phone calls, or entertainment. We also do not reimburse costs for family members or friends who accompany you on a business trip, unless they are also employees traveling for work.

If you combine business travel with personal time, you may extend your stay, but you must cover the additional costs yourself. You must also ensure that the extended stay does not increase the cost of the business portion of the trip. For example, if you stay an extra weekend, we will cover the hotel only for the business days, and you pay for the weekend nights.

When you are unsure whether an expense is reimbursable, ask before you spend. A quick question to Finance or your manager is easier than a rejected claim later.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Spain, travels to a conference in France. She extends her stay by two days to visit friends. She books her flights so that the business days are not more expensive than they would have been without the extension. She pays for the extra hotel nights and her personal meals herself.

## 6. Submitting expenses

Submit your expense report in Ledgerly after your trip. Expense reports are due within 45 calendar days after the trip ends. This timeline helps Finance close the books accurately and ensures you get reimbursed promptly.

For each expense, attach a receipt unless the expense is covered by the per-diem. Receipts must show the date, the vendor, and the amount. If a receipt is missing, explain why in the report; we understand that sometimes a receipt is lost.

Group your expenses by trip and by category. Ledgerly will guide you through the steps. When you submit, your manager will receive a notification to approve the report. After approval, Finance processes the reimbursement through payroll or bank transfer, depending on your location.

If you have an advance for the trip, you must settle it in the same report. Any unused advance must be returned with the report, and any shortfall will be added to your reimbursement.

If you submit late, we may still process your report, but we ask you to explain the delay. Repeated late submissions may affect how quickly we can reimburse you.

Example: Deniz returns from her client visit in Hamburg on a Thursday. The following week, she opens Ledgerly, attaches her train ticket and hotel receipt, claims the per-diem for her two travel days, and submits the report. Her manager approves it within a few days, and Finance reimburses her in the next payroll cycle.

## 7. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager. For questions about a specific expense, contact Finance through Ledgerly. For questions about how this policy interacts with your employment contract or local law, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example.

We also encourage you to flag anything that feels unclear or unfair. This policy is meant to support you, not to create barriers. Your feedback helps us improve it.
