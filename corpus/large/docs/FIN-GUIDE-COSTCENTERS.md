# Cost Center Guide

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains how cost centers work at Kranich Software GmbH. It is written for everyone who needs to record expenses, book travel, or understand how their team's spending is tracked.

A cost center is a label that tells Finance which part of the company is responsible for a particular cost. When you submit an expense through Ledgerly or book a trip, you assign that cost to a cost center. This helps us understand where our money goes and keeps our planning accurate.

Everyone at Kranich Software belongs to a cost center. Your cost center is usually tied to your team, but sometimes it is tied to a project or a specific activity. If you are unsure which cost center to use, ask your manager or check Fern for your team assignment.

## 2. Structure

Cost centers follow the structure of our teams. Each team has its own cost center, and some teams have additional cost centers for specific purposes.

The main cost centers are:

- Routing Core
- Fleet Insights
- Platform & Infrastructure
- Customer Success
- Go-to-Market

In addition to the team cost centers, there are a few shared cost centers used by several teams. These cover things that benefit the whole company, such as office costs for the Ritterstraße office in Berlin and the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, company-wide tools, and internal events.

Each cost center has a code that appears in Ledgerly and in the Grove policy library. When you create an expense report or a travel request, you will see a list of cost centers to choose from. Pick the one that matches the purpose of the spending, not necessarily the team you sit with.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, visits a client together with Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland. Deniz books her travel under Customer Success. Marek books his travel under the same cost center because the visit serves a customer success purpose, even though his team affiliation is also Customer Success.

If a cost serves two teams equally, choose the team that initiated the activity. If you genuinely cannot tell, ask Finance before you submit.

## 3. Owners

Each cost center has an owner. The owner is the person who manages the budget for that cost center and approves larger spending decisions. Owners are typically the heads of the teams listed in §2.

The cost center owners are:

- Routing Core: Ines Duarte, engineering manager
- Fleet Insights: the product manager for Fleet Insights
- Platform & Infrastructure: Jonas Weber, CTO
- Customer Success: the head of Customer Success
- Go-to-Market: the head of Go-to-Market

Shared cost centers are owned by the Head of Workplace & Operations, Rui Almeida, for office-related costs, and by the CFO, Tomasz Zieliński, for company-wide tools and events.

Owners review spending for their cost center regularly. They look at what has been spent and compare it with what was planned. If a cost center is likely to exceed its plan, the owner discusses this with Finance early, so we can adjust before it becomes a problem.

As an employee, you do not need to interact with cost center owners for everyday expenses. You only need to know who the owner is if you have a question about whether a particular cost belongs to that cost center, or if you need approval for something unusual.

## 4. Questions

If you have questions about cost centers, here is where to start.

For day-to-day questions about which cost center to use, ask your manager or your team's finance contact. They can usually answer right away.

For questions about how to enter a cost center in Ledgerly, or if the cost center you need is missing from the list, contact it-help@kranich.example.

For questions about cost center ownership, budget planning, or whether a cost is allowed at all, contact Finance. You can reach Finance through Ledgerly or by asking your manager to connect you.

For questions that involve company policy, check the Grove policy library first. Relevant policies include POL-004, which covers expense reporting, and FIN-RATES-2026, which covers travel and per-diem rates. These documents explain the rules in detail and include examples.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wants to book a train for a workshop in Berlin. She is not sure whether the travel cost belongs to Routing Core or to Platform & Infrastructure. She checks POL-004, sees that the workshop is organized by Routing Core, and assigns the cost accordingly. She does not need to contact Finance.

If you are still unsure after checking the policies and asking your manager, write to Finance with a short description of what you plan to spend and why. Finance will confirm the correct cost center promptly, usually within a few days. Please do not book anything that depends on a cost center assignment before you have that confirmation.

We keep this guide simple on purpose. Cost centers should help you spend with confidence, not slow you down. When in doubt, ask — it is always better to check than to guess.
