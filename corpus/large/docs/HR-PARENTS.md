# Guide for New Parents

## 1. Purpose

Becoming a parent is a big life change, and we want you to have clarity and support during this time. This guide explains how parental leave works at Kranich Software GmbH, what you can expect from us, and how to plan your time away and your return.

We believe that caring for a new child should not feel complicated. This document is written to answer the most common questions in one place. If something is unclear, please reach out to People & Culture or your manager — we are here to help.

## 2. Who this is for

This guide applies to all employees who become parents, regardless of gender, family structure, or how the child joins your family. It covers birth parents, non-birth parents, adoptive parents, and parents welcoming a child through surrogacy or fostering.

You are covered whether you work from our Berlin office, our Lisbon office, or remotely from your home country. The legal framework may differ depending on where you are employed, but our intention is the same everywhere: to give you time to bond with your child and to make the transition as smooth as possible.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, and Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, both used this guide when planning their leave. Their situations differed, but the process was the same.

## 3. Process

The process has three simple steps. Start early, communicate clearly, and keep your team informed.

**Step one: tell your manager.** As soon as you feel comfortable, share your news with your manager. You do not need to share medical details or personal circumstances. A simple conversation about timing is enough to begin planning.

**Step two: submit your request.** Once you know your intended leave dates, submit a request through Fern, our HRIS. People & Culture will review it and confirm the details with you. If you are unsure about any part of the process, Lea in People Operations is happy to walk you through it.

**Step three: plan your handover.** Together with your manager, plan how your responsibilities will be covered while you are away. This includes updating documentation, informing key clients or partners, and identifying a point of contact for your team. The goal is for you to leave with peace of mind and return to a situation that is under control.

The notice period for parental leave is described in POL-008 §3. Please review that document and make sure you meet the requirements.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, shared his plans with his manager and his main client contacts well in advance. He prepared a short handover note in Grove so his colleagues could find answers quickly while he was away.

## 4. Timelines

Planning your leave is a personal decision, and the right timing looks different for everyone. Some parents prefer to start leave before the expected arrival date; others prefer to start later. Some parents take their leave in one continuous block; others split it into parts. All of these are valid options, subject to the rules in POL-008 and the legal requirements of your country of employment.

We encourage you to begin the conversation early — as soon as you have a general sense of your plans. This gives your manager and team enough time to prepare and gives you time to adjust if anything changes.

During your leave, you are not expected to check email or attend meetings. We trust you to be fully present with your family. If you wish to stay loosely informed about major team developments, that is your choice, but there is no expectation.

Towards the end of your leave, your manager will reach out to schedule a return conversation. This is an opportunity to discuss any changes that happened while you were away, agree on your first priorities, and talk about any adjustments that would help you settle back in.

Example: Sofia, a product manager on Fleet Insights, planned her leave around the school summer holidays so that she could be home with her older child as well as the new baby. She spoke with her manager a few months in advance and they agreed on a handover plan for her ongoing projects.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions that are not answered here, there are several people you can turn to.

- **Your manager** — for questions about team planning, handover, or your return.
- **People & Culture** — for questions about the process, your rights, or how the policy applies to your situation. You can reach the team at people@kranich.example.
- **IT support** — for practical questions about setting up your equipment or access when you return. Write to it-help@kranich.example.

If you are unsure which document applies to your situation, POL-004 gives an overview of the people policies at Kranich Software GmbH and points to the relevant details.

We also encourage you to talk to colleagues who have already been through this. Many parents at Kranich are happy to share their experience, and you may find informal advice just as useful as the official guidance.

Finally, remember that this is your journey. There is no single right way to become a parent while working here. The most important thing is that you feel supported, informed, and able to make the choices that are right for your family.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, remembers that her own return to work felt smoothest when she had agreed on a gradual reintroduction with her team. She now encourages everyone on her team to think about what would help them feel comfortable coming back, and to say so openly.

We wish you and your growing family all the best.
