# Working Time & Overtime Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software GmbH, we build route-optimization software that helps logistics operators run their fleets efficiently. To do that well, we need a team that is focused, rested, and able to plan their days with clarity. This policy explains how we think about working time and overtime, and what you can expect from us in return.

We work across different countries and legal systems. This policy sets a common baseline for everyone at Kranich, regardless of where you are employed. Where local law or your employment contract imposes stricter rules, those rules take precedence over this policy. If you are ever unsure which applies to you, please reach out to People & Culture.

We trust you to manage your own schedule. This policy is not about counting minutes. It is about making sure that we share the same expectations, protect your wellbeing, and stay compliant with the legal framework in each country where we operate.

## 2. Working time

Kranich is a remote-first company. Most of us work from home within our country of employment — Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom. We also have offices in Berlin (Ritterstraße) and Lisbon (Rua do Alecrim) for those who prefer to work on-site, and we use Perch to book desks and meeting rooms when you visit.

Your working time is the time you spend performing work-related duties for Kranich. This includes your regular daily work, participation in meetings with colleagues or clients, and any other tasks that are part of your role. It does not include breaks, commuting, or voluntary activities outside of work.

We expect you to organise your working time so that you can fulfil the responsibilities of your role while maintaining a healthy balance. For most roles, this means a standard full-time schedule distributed across the working days of the week. Part-time arrangements are possible and should be discussed with your manager and recorded in Fern.

As a general rule, working time may not exceed 44 hours in any week. This limit applies to all employees, regardless of whether the work is performed during regular hours or as overtime. We set this limit to protect your health and to ensure that you have sufficient time for rest and recovery between working periods.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, sometimes needs to coordinate with colleagues in different time zones. She plans her week so that her total working time stays within the limit, even on weeks when an early-morning call with a client in Poland shifts her schedule.

If you find that your workload regularly requires you to approach the weekly limit, please speak with your manager. That is a signal that we need to redistribute work or adjust priorities, not a sign that you should simply work longer hours.

## 3. Overtime and compensation

Overtime is work performed beyond your regular scheduled working time, at the request of your manager or with their approval. Occasional overtime can be necessary — for example, when we are preparing a major product release, supporting a client during a critical rollout, or resolving an incident that affects service availability.

We do not expect overtime to be a regular part of your routine. If you are consistently working overtime, something is wrong with our planning, and we want to fix that together. Please raise it with your manager or with People & Culture.

Overtime is compensated with time off within 18 weeks. This means that when you work additional hours, you accrue an equivalent amount of time off that you can take at a later date, provided the time off is taken within the period stated. The intention is to give you flexibility: you can use the time off to recover shortly after a busy period, or you can save it for a moment that suits you better, as long as it is used within the stated window.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, supports a client during a system migration over a weekend. She records the extra hours she worked. A few weeks later, when the migration is complete and the client is stable, she takes a couple of days off to compensate for that weekend work.

To take time off as compensation for overtime, you should submit a request through Fern, just as you would for any other leave. Your manager will approve the request, taking into account the needs of the team and your own preference for when to take the time off. We encourage you to take this time off rather than letting it accumulate.

Overtime compensation is not a substitute for your regular leave entitlement. Your annual leave is separate and should be planned and taken in accordance with the leave policy. Time off in lieu of overtime is an additional benefit that recognises the extra effort you put in.

If your role involves working with clients in different time zones, as is the case for some of our field solutions engineers, occasional out-of-hours work may be necessary. Please agree on the expected pattern with your manager in advance, and record any resulting overtime so that it can be compensated.

## 4. Time tracking

We track working time to ensure that we comply with legal requirements in the countries where we operate, and to protect your wellbeing. Time tracking is not a performance management tool, and we do not use it to monitor your productivity or to judge how you spend your day.

You are responsible for recording your working time accurately. This includes your regular working hours, any overtime you work, and the periods of time off you take as compensation for overtime. You should record your time in Fern, which is our system of record for HR-related data.

Record your time on a regular basis — ideally at the end of each working day or at least weekly. Do not wait until the end of the month to reconstruct your hours from memory; that is error-prone and creates unnecessary stress. If you are unsure how to record a particular type of time, such as a partial day of work or a day when you work from the office, please ask People & Culture or check the guidance on Grove.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, often travels to client sites. He records his travel time as working time when the travel occurs during his regular working hours, and he records any out-of-hours travel separately so that it is visible to his manager.

Your manager is responsible for reviewing the time records of their team members on a regular basis. If they notice patterns that warrant attention — for example, recurring overtime or gaps in recording — they will discuss this with you directly. The goal of these conversations is to support you, not to discipline you.

If you believe that your time records are incorrect, or if you notice an error in how your overtime has been recorded, please correct it promptly or contact People & Culture for assistance. Accurate records are the foundation of fair compensation, and we want to get this right.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. We are happy to explain how the policy applies to your specific situation, including any interaction with local law in your country of employment.

For questions about how to record your time in Fern, or if you experience technical issues with the system, please contact IT support at it-help@kranich.example. They can help you with system access and functionality.

If you have concerns about your workload, your working hours, or your ability to take the time off you are entitled to, please speak with your manager first. If you do not feel comfortable raising the issue with your manager, or if you have raised it and the situation has not improved, you can contact People & Culture or use the confidential channels described in our internal policies.

We also encourage you to familiarise yourself with related policies that may be relevant to your situation, including the leave policy and the policy on flexible working arrangements. Together, these documents describe how we support you in managing your time at Kranich.

Finally, remember that this policy is a starting point, not a ceiling. If you have ideas for how we can improve the way we think about working time — for example, by changing how we schedule meetings or how we plan for busy periods — we would love to hear them. You can share your suggestions with your manager, with People & Culture, or through the feedback channels on Grove.
