# Meeting Room Guide

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains how to find, book, and use meeting rooms at our Berlin and Lisbon offices. We want every meeting to start smoothly, whether you are hosting a client workshop, a team sync, or a quiet focus session.

We keep this guide short on purpose. For questions not covered here, reach out to Workplace & Operations via the help desk alias.

## 2. Rooms

Both offices offer a mix of room sizes and layouts. You will find small huddle spaces for calls, standard meeting rooms with screens, and larger rooms for workshops or all-hands gatherings.

Every room is equipped with a screen, a cable for common laptop models, and a whiteboard. Some rooms have video-conferencing cameras; these are marked in Perch so you can pick the right one for hybrid meetings.

A few practical notes:

- Rooms are open for use during office hours. Outside those hours, access may be limited.
- If you move furniture, please put it back when you are done.
- Please leave the room tidy and switch off the screen when you leave.

If you notice something missing or broken, report it through the help desk alias so we can fix it promptly.

## 3. Booking

Use Perch to book a room. You will find the room layout, photos, and equipment details there. Booking a room is required, even for short, informal meetings. This helps colleagues find each other and avoids double-bookings.

When you book, keep these points in mind:

- Book only the time you need. If your meeting ends early, release the room so others can use it.
- If you are running late, hold the room for a short grace period. After that, the room becomes available to others.
- For recurring meetings, check the recurring-booking cap in POL-028 §3. That policy explains how far ahead and how often you may reserve a recurring slot.
- For larger or sensitive sessions, consider a room with a closed door rather than an open huddle space.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, needs a quiet room for a design review with her team in Berlin. She checks Perch, picks a room with a good camera, and books a slot that works for both time zones. When the review finishes early, she releases the room so a colleague can take it for an afternoon workshop.

If no room is available, try a different time or a different office floor. You can also check whether a colleague has booked a room they no longer need. When all else fails, Perch shows you which rooms are free on the day, so you can improvise.

## 4. Questions

For any question about meeting rooms, facilities, or booking, contact Workplace & Operations through the help desk alias. We usually reply within a few days.

For questions about Perch access or technical issues with the booking tool, the help desk can also point you in the right direction.

If you have ideas for improving our meeting spaces, we would love to hear them. Send a note to Workplace & Operations, and we will consider it for the next office refresh.
