# Performance Review Process Guide

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains how performance reviews work at Kranich Software GmbH. We run reviews to support your growth, align your work with team goals, and make sure everyone receives useful feedback — not to create paperwork for its own sake.

A good review should leave you with a clear sense of what is going well, where you can develop, and what to focus on next. It should also give your manager and team a fuller picture of your contributions. We want the process to feel fair, transparent, and genuinely useful.

This document is the companion to the formal performance policy, which is POL-004. Where anything here seems unclear, POL-004 takes precedence.

## 2. Who this is for

This guide is for every employee at Kranich Software GmbH, regardless of team, location, or seniority. That includes:

- Individual contributors across Routing Core, Fleet Insights, and Platform & Infrastructure
- Customer-facing colleagues in Customer Success and Go-to-Market
- Engineering managers and team leads
- People working remotely from Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, or the United Kingdom
- People working from our offices in Berlin (Ritterstraße) or Lisbon (Rua do Alecrim)

If you joined Kranich recently, your onboarding plan includes an introductory conversation about how reviews work. If you have questions at any point, reach out to your manager or to People & Culture via people@kranich.example.

## 3. Process

The review cycle has several stages. Each stage is designed to be lightweight and practical.

**Self-reflection.** You begin by reflecting on your own work since the last review. Consider what you accomplished, what you learned, and where you struggled. Write this down in the review form provided by your manager. There is no prescribed length — a few thoughtful paragraphs are usually enough.

**Manager assessment.** Your manager reviews your self-reflection and prepares their own assessment. They consider the quality of your work, how you collaborate with others, and how you have progressed against the goals you set previously. Managers are encouraged to gather input from colleagues you work with regularly, including people in other teams.

**Review conversation.** You and your manager meet to discuss both perspectives. This is a two-way dialogue. You should leave the conversation knowing what is expected of you and how your manager sees your strengths and areas for development. If you disagree with something, say so — the conversation is meant to be honest.

**Written summary.** After the conversation, your manager writes a short summary of what was discussed, including any agreed actions. You receive a copy and have the opportunity to correct factual errors or add your own comments. The summary is stored in Fern, our HRIS, and is visible only to you, your manager, and People & Culture.

**Goal setting.** Together, you set a small number of goals for the coming period. Goals should be specific enough to be meaningful but not so detailed that they become bureaucratic. You will revisit these goals at your next review.

Throughout the year, we encourage informal check-ins. A review should never be the first time you hear about a problem. If something is not working, raise it promptly with your manager rather than waiting for the formal cycle.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, noticed that her project had fallen behind schedule. She mentioned this to her manager, Ines Duarte, during a regular check-in. Together they adjusted priorities, and by the time the review came around, Anna's summary reflected the realistic picture rather than a surprise.

## 4. Timelines

The review cycle follows the calendar year. The People & Culture team announces the opening of the review period well in advance, so you have time to prepare.

- **Self-reflection window.** You receive an invitation to complete your self-reflection. You will have a few weeks to finish it. People & Culture sends a reminder before the window closes.
- **Manager preparation.** Managers have time after the self-reflection deadline to gather input and write their assessments.
- **Conversation period.** Review conversations take place over several weeks. Managers schedule these directly with you, at a time that works for both of you. If you are remote, the conversation happens over video call.
- **Summary and goals.** Written summaries and agreed goals are completed shortly after the conversation. People & Culture reviews a sample of summaries to ensure consistency across teams.

If you are on leave during part of the cycle, your manager will adjust the timeline so you are not disadvantaged. If you have just joined and have not been with Kranich for a full period, you still participate, but the focus is on your early progress and settling-in rather than a full assessment.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, was travelling for work during the conversation period. Her manager scheduled her review as soon as she returned, and she completed the self-reflection from the road. Nothing was rushed.

## 5. Questions

If you are unsure about any part of the process, start with your manager. They can answer most questions about timing, format, and expectations.

For questions about how reviews relate to compensation, role changes, or other people processes, contact People & Culture via people@kranich.example. For questions about how review data is stored or protected, contact the Information Security team via security@kranich.example.

If you feel your review was not conducted fairly, you can raise a concern with People & Culture. We take all concerns seriously and will handle them confidentially. You can also speak to Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture, directly.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, was unsure whether feedback from a client workshop should be included in his review. He asked his manager, who confirmed that client feedback is valuable and encouraged Marek to include it in his self-reflection.

We want every review to leave you feeling more confident about your work and clearer about your future at Kranich. If the process ever feels unclear or unfair, please tell us — we review our own process every year and we listen.
