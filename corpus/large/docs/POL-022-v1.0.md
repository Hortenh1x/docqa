# External Communications & Social Media

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich, we are proud of the work we do and the way we do it. Many of us share our professional experiences online, speak at industry events, or talk with journalists and analysts. This policy helps you do that confidently, while protecting Kranich's reputation and our customers' trust.

It applies to everyone who works at Kranich, whether you are an employee, a contractor, or an intern. It covers communications you make on behalf of Kranich, as well as your personal use of social media when it relates to your work or our industry.

We want you to be an ambassador for Kranich. We also want you to be yourself online. This policy is not about silencing you. It is about being thoughtful about what you share, and about knowing when to ask for help.

## 2. Speaking for Kranich

Only designated people may speak publicly on behalf of Kranich. This includes talking to journalists, posting on Kranich's official social media channels, writing blog posts on the Kranich company blog, or representing Kranich at conferences and industry panels.

Unless you have been explicitly asked to do so, you are not speaking for Kranich. When you speak in a professional context, make clear that your views are your own. A simple line in your bio or at the start of a talk — "Views are my own" — is usually enough. This distinction matters because customers and partners may assume that anyone from Kranich speaks with authority about our products and roadmap.

If a journalist contacts you about Kranich, do not respond directly. Instead, forward the request to Go-to-Market. They will coordinate a response and identify the right spokesperson. This applies to all media, from trade publications to podcasts to newsletters. Even a seemingly casual comment can be quoted out of context.

When you speak at a conference or appear on a panel about your area of expertise, you do not need prior approval. We encourage this kind of thought leadership. However, if your talk will mention Kranich products, customers, or strategy in any detail, review your materials with Go-to-Market first. They can help you avoid accidentally disclosing information that is not yet public.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, was invited to speak at a logistics meetup about route optimization trends. He planned to show a slide with aggregate performance data from several Kranich customers. Before the event, he sent his slide deck to Go-to-Market, who asked him to replace the customer data with anonymized illustrations. Marek gave the talk as planned, and his audience still found it valuable.

## 3. Personal accounts

You are free to maintain personal social media accounts and to post about your work, your team, and the logistics industry. We ask that you follow a few common-sense guidelines.

Never share confidential information. This includes unreleased product features, customer names or data, internal financial results, security details, and anything marked internal or confidential. When in doubt, leave it out, or ask your manager or Go-to-Market.

Be respectful. Do not attack competitors, customers, partners, or former colleagues. Do not post anything that could be seen as harassing, discriminatory, or defamatory. Remember that your posts are public and permanent, even if you delete them later.

Do not use Kranich logos or trademarks on personal accounts in a way that suggests your account is official. If you list Kranich as your employer, that is fine. If you use the Kranich logo as your profile picture, that is not.

Do not engage in arguments about Kranich online. If you see a post that criticizes Kranich or spreads misinformation, you do not need to respond. If you feel you must respond, do so calmly and factually, and never escalate. For anything beyond a simple clarification, suggest the person contact us through official channels.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, noticed a LinkedIn post complaining about one of our features. She replied briefly, acknowledged the user's frustration, and pointed them to our support channels. She did not discuss the specifics of the account or promise a fix. That is the right level of engagement.

If you are unsure whether something you want to post is appropriate, wait a day and re-read it. If it still feels fine, and it does not involve confidential information, you are likely good to go.

## 4. Approval of public content

Some types of public content require approval before they are published. This is not about slowing you down. It is about making sure we present a consistent, accurate picture of Kranich to the world.

The following require approval from Go-to-Market:

- Blog posts or articles published under a Kranich byline, or on the Kranich company blog.
- Social media posts on Kranich's official accounts.
- Press releases or statements to the media.
- Presentations, white papers, or case studies that include Kranich data, customer references, or product roadmap details.
- Content that quotes or attributes statements to Kranich leadership.

To request approval, send your draft to the Go-to-Market team. They will review it for accuracy, confidentiality, and tone. Go-to-Market approves public content within 1 business day. If your content is time-sensitive, let them know when you submit it, and they will prioritize accordingly.

For personal content that is clearly your own opinion and does not mention Kranich, no approval is needed. For personal content that mentions Kranich in a factual way — for example, "I work on the Routing Core team at Kranich" — no approval is needed either. The requirement applies when you are creating content that represents Kranich or that could reasonably be seen as official.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wrote a technical blog post about a routing algorithm she had implemented. The post did not mention Kranich or any customer data — it was purely about the algorithm. She published it on her personal blog without approval. That is fine. If she had wanted to publish it on the Kranich engineering blog, she would have sent it to Go-to-Market first.

If Go-to-Market requests changes, work with them to revise the content. They will not change your technical substance without discussing it with you. Their goal is to protect Kranich, not to dilute your ideas.

## 5. Questions

If you have a question about this policy, or about a specific communication you are planning, there are several ways to get help.

For questions about media relations, official Kranich communications, or approval of public content, contact Go-to-Market. They are happy to review drafts, discuss whether approval is needed, and advise on messaging.

For questions about what information is confidential, or about security-related disclosures, contact security@kranich.example. This is especially relevant if you are unsure whether something touches on customer data or our security posture.

For questions about how this policy applies to your role or your personal situation, talk to your manager or to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. They can help you interpret the policy in context.

Finally, if you see something that concerns you — a colleague's post, a news article, or a social media thread about Kranich — please flag it. You can send it to Go-to-Market or to security@kranich.example. We would rather hear about it from you than read about it later.

We trust your judgment. This policy exists to support you, not to restrict you. When you are in doubt, ask. A quick conversation now can prevent a misunderstanding later. Thank you for representing Kranich well, both online and offline.
