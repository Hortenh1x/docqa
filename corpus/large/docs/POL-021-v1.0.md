# Contractor Engagement Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how we engage independent contractors at Kranich Software GmbH. It sets out when a contractor relationship is appropriate, how long an engagement may last, and what access and equipment a contractor receives.

This version supersedes version 0.9. It reflects our commitment to clear, fair and compliant working arrangements for everyone who contributes to Kranich, whether as an employee or as an external partner.

We value the flexibility and specialist skills that contractors bring. At the same time, we must protect the legal distinction between employees and contractors. Misclassification can create risk for the company and for the individual. This policy helps managers, teams and People & Culture make consistent decisions.

If you are considering engaging a contractor, read this policy first. If you have questions, contact Legal or People & Culture before you make an offer.

## 2. When to use a contractor

Use a contractor when you need a specific skill or capacity for a defined piece of work, and the person will operate with a degree of independence. A contractor typically brings their own methods, may work for other clients, and is responsible for their own taxes and social contributions.

A contractor relationship is not appropriate when the work is ongoing, closely supervised, or an integral part of our core operations. If the person will work set hours at our direction, use our tools as their primary workspace, and perform the same role as an employee, they should be hired as an employee instead.

Examples of suitable contractor engagements include:

- A specialist who joins a project for a defined phase, such as a data migration or a security audit.
- An expert who provides guidance on a particular technical topic without joining a team on a permanent basis.
- A consultant who delivers a specific deliverable, such as a market analysis or a training programme.

Before engaging a contractor, discuss the plan with your manager and, where relevant, your team lead. People & Culture can help you assess whether the role fits a contractor classification. Legal can review the engagement if there is any doubt.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer, works with a client in Poland who needs help integrating our route-optimisation API into their warehouse system. The client asks Marek to recommend an independent consultant who can build a custom connector. Marek refers the client to our Customer Success team, who follow this policy before engaging the consultant.

Contractors are engaged through Kranich Software GmbH, not through individual teams. All contractor engagements must be documented in a written agreement. The agreement must state the scope of work, the expected deliverables, and the commercial terms. Do not start work before the agreement is signed.

If you already work with someone who provides services to Kranich on a regular basis, review the arrangement with People & Culture at least once per year. This helps us confirm that the relationship still meets the contractor criteria.

## 3. Duration and renewal

A contractor engagement may not exceed 9 months without a Legal review. This applies to the total length of the working relationship with the same contractor, not to individual statements of work. If you expect the work to continue beyond this period, you must initiate a Legal review before the engagement reaches the limit.

The Legal review considers the nature of the work, the degree of control we exercise, and whether the contractor has become integrated into our operations. Legal may approve an extension, recommend a transition to employment, or suggest changes to the working arrangement.

When an engagement is nearing its end, plan ahead. Discuss with the contractor whether the work is complete or whether an extension is needed. If the work is complete, ensure the contractor returns any company property and that their access is revoked promptly. If an extension is needed, start the review process in good time so there is no gap in coverage.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, works with a contractor who was engaged to help refactor a legacy service in the Routing Core team. The contractor has been with the team for several months and the work is nearly done. Anna realises the contractor will reach the engagement limit before the refactor is complete. She speaks with Ines Duarte, her engineering manager, who contacts Legal to request a review. Legal reviews the scope and approves a short extension so the contractor can finish the work.

Renewals are not automatic. Each renewal requires a fresh assessment of the scope and the contractor relationship. A contractor who has been engaged continuously for a long period may no longer be appropriately classified as a contractor. In that case, People & Culture will discuss options with the hiring manager.

## 4. Access and equipment

Contractors receive access to the Kranich systems they need to perform their work. Access is granted on a least-privilege basis and is limited to the duration of the engagement. The hiring manager is responsible for requesting access through Platform & Infrastructure, following the standard process.

Contractors use their own equipment unless the engagement requires otherwise. If a contractor needs a Kranich-managed device to access certain systems securely, Platform & Infrastructure will provide one. The contractor must return the device when the engagement ends. If a contractor uses their own device, they must comply with our security requirements for personal devices.

Contractors do not receive an internal email address. They communicate with teams through external email or through the collaboration tools that Platform & Infrastructure approves for external users. Contractors may be invited to specific meetings and channels as needed, but they do not appear in the internal org chart.

Contractors must follow the same security policies as employees. They must use the password manager we provide for business credentials, and they must report any security incident promptly to security@kranich.example. Contractors do not have access to internal policy documents on Grove unless the information is necessary for their work. If a contractor needs to read a policy, the hiring manager can share the relevant section.

When the engagement ends, the hiring manager must notify Platform & Infrastructure so that access is revoked on the same day. The contractor should also be reminded of their confidentiality obligations, which continue after the engagement ends.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, works with a contractor who helps prepare training materials for a new client. The contractor needs access to a shared document folder and to the client-facing knowledge base. Deniz requests access through Platform & Infrastructure, specifying which folders the contractor may view. The contractor uses their own laptop and signs in with a temporary account. When the project finishes, Deniz confirms that the contractor has completed their deliverables and requests that access be removed the same day.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, contact People & Culture in the first instance. They can help you understand the contractor criteria and the process for engaging a contractor.

For questions about the legal classification of a specific engagement, or about the review process described in §3, contact Legal. For questions about access, equipment or security requirements, contact it-help@kranich.example.

If you believe a current contractor arrangement may not comply with this policy, raise it with your manager or People & Culture. We would rather address a concern early than discover a problem later. Speaking up helps us keep our practices fair and compliant for everyone.

For related guidance on how we work with external parties, refer to the security policy and the procurement policy in the policy library on Grove. People & Culture can also point you to the relevant sections of the employee handbook if you are comparing contractor and employee arrangements.

We appreciate the contributions that contractors make to Kranich. This policy exists to make those contributions clear, compliant and rewarding for all involved.
