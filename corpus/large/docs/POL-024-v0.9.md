# Vendor & SaaS Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software relies on a growing number of external vendors and software-as-a-service tools to run our business. From cloud infrastructure and communication platforms to specialized routing analytics, these tools help our teams work efficiently and serve our customers well. At the same time, every vendor relationship carries cost, security, and operational considerations that we need to manage thoughtfully.

This policy explains how we evaluate, approve, renew, and retire vendor relationships and SaaS subscriptions. It applies to all employees, contractors, and anyone else who procures or manages tools on behalf of Kranich. Finance owns this policy and coordinates with the Platform & Infrastructure team, Information Security, and Workplace & Operations as needed.

We want to make it easy for you to get the tools you need. This policy is meant to provide clarity, not bureaucracy. If you are unsure whether a tool needs review, please reach out to Finance or the IT help alias before you sign up.

## 2. Adding a tool

Before you purchase or subscribe to a new vendor tool or SaaS product, you must complete the internal review process. This applies whether the tool is free, has a trial period, or costs money. A tool is considered "new" if your team has not used it before, even if other teams already do.

Start by discussing the need with your manager and, where relevant, your team lead. Then submit a request through the standard procurement channel. Finance will coordinate the review with Information Security and Platform & Infrastructure.

The review considers a few key questions. First, does the tool solve a real need that cannot be met with tools we already have? Second, does it meet our security and data-protection standards? Third, is the pricing structure transparent and aligned with our budget cycle? Finally, does the vendor provide adequate support and a clear contractual framework?

For tools that process customer data or other sensitive information, Information Security will perform a more detailed assessment. Please allow enough time for this step. If you need a tool for a client engagement, involve Customer Success or the field solutions team early so they can flag any contractual constraints.

Once approved, Finance or Workplace & Operations will set up the subscription, and you will receive onboarding guidance. Do not purchase tools with a personal credit card or expense them through Ledgerly before approval. If you already use a tool that has not been reviewed, please flag it to Finance so we can bring it into compliance.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, wants to try a new analytics dashboard for client reports. She discusses it with her manager, submits a request through the standard channel, and waits for the security review before signing up. This way, the tool is properly vetted and the subscription is tracked from day one.

## 3. Renewals and notice

Vendor contracts and SaaS subscriptions rarely expire automatically in a way that is convenient for us. To avoid unintended renewals, missed cancellations, or lapses in critical services, we manage renewals centrally.

Finance maintains a register of all active vendor contracts and subscriptions, including renewal dates and notice periods. The register is shared with the relevant budget owners before each renewal cycle. Renewals must be reviewed at least 60 days before the notice deadline. This gives us time to evaluate whether the tool is still needed, whether usage justifies the cost, and whether alternatives have emerged.

As part of the review, the budget owner should confirm that the tool is actively used and that the license count matches current headcount. If a team has shrunk or the tool is only used by a handful of people, consider reducing the subscription size or moving to a lower tier.

For contracts with automatic renewal clauses, the notice period is especially important. If we miss the deadline, we may be locked in for another term. Finance tracks these dates carefully, but budget owners are also responsible for knowing the terms of the tools they use. If you receive a renewal notice or invoice directly from a vendor, forward it to Finance promptly.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, receives a renewal notice for a mapping tool his team uses. He forwards it to Finance a few weeks after receiving it. Finance checks the register and notes that the notice deadline is approaching, so they accelerate the review and confirm whether the tool is still needed for upcoming client projects.

## 4. Offboarding a vendor

When a tool is no longer needed, or when we decide to replace it with a different solution, we must offboard the vendor in a controlled way. This prevents unnecessary costs, reduces security risks, and ensures a clean transition.

The process begins with a decision to retire the tool, made by the budget owner in consultation with Finance and the relevant team. Notify Finance as soon as the decision is made, even if the contract still has time remaining. Finance will handle the formal termination notice according to the contract terms.

Before the subscription ends, make sure that all data held in the tool is exported or migrated as needed. This is particularly important for tools that store customer data, project documents, or configuration settings. Platform & Infrastructure can help with data migration to a replacement tool or to internal storage.

Coordinate with Information Security to revoke access for all users and to confirm that no credentials or API keys remain active. If the vendor hosts any of our data, confirm in writing that the data will be deleted or returned according to the contract and our data-protection obligations.

Finally, update the internal register and any relevant documentation, such as the tool inventory on Grove. Inform the affected teams that the tool is being retired and point them to the replacement, if there is one. Remove the tool from onboarding materials and from any internal guides.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, decides that her team's legacy reporting tool is no longer needed because the new analytics platform covers all use cases. She coordinates with Finance to send the termination notice, works with Platform & Infrastructure to export historical reports, and asks Information Security to revoke access. Within a short time, the tool is fully retired and removed from the internal tool list.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, the procurement process, or a specific vendor contract, please reach out to Finance or the IT help alias. For security-related concerns about a vendor or tool, contact Information Security directly at the security alias.

For questions about how tools are used within a specific team, start with your manager or the relevant team lead. They can help you determine whether a new tool is needed or whether an existing one can be adapted.

We also encourage you to share feedback about tools that work well or tools that are causing friction. Your input helps us make better decisions about what we adopt, renew, or retire. Thank you for helping us keep our vendor landscape lean, secure, and aligned with how we work.
