# Speak-Up & Anti-Harassment Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH is committed to a work environment where everyone feels respected, safe, and able to do their best work. This policy explains what we mean by harassment, how you can raise a concern, and what you can expect after you do.

We believe that speaking up is a sign of strength, not weakness. Whether you have experienced something yourself or witnessed something that did not feel right, we want you to know where to turn and that you will be heard.

This policy applies to all employees, regardless of where they work — in the Berlin or Lisbon offices, at a client site, or remotely from home. It also applies to contractors, interns, and anyone else who works with us. It covers conduct at work-related events, in digital channels, and in any setting where your relationship with Kranich is a factor.

## 2. What we do not tolerate

We do not tolerate harassment, bullying, or discrimination of any kind. This includes conduct directed at you because of your gender, age, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sexual orientation, disability, or any other characteristic protected by law.

Harassment can take many forms. It may be verbal, such as offensive jokes, slurs, or unwanted comments about a person's appearance or personal life. It may be physical, such as unwanted touching or intimidation. It may also be digital, such as offensive messages in chat channels, demeaning comments in code reviews, or sharing inappropriate images.

Bullying is also covered by this policy. Bullying includes repeated, unreasonable behaviour that humiliates, undermines, or isolates a person. It can come from a manager, a peer, or someone more junior — we take it seriously regardless of the seniority of the people involved.

Sexual harassment is never acceptable. This includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favours, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature. A single incident can be enough to constitute harassment — it does not need to be repeated.

We also do not tolerate retaliation. If you raise a concern in good faith, participate in an investigation, or support a colleague who has spoken up, you will not face negative consequences for doing so. Retaliation against someone who speaks up is itself a violation of this policy.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, noticed that a colleague from another team repeatedly made dismissive comments about a Polish client's English during a video call. Deniz felt uncomfortable and worried the client might overhear. Under this policy, Deniz can raise this concern without fear of being blamed or punished.

## 3. How to raise a concern

If you experience or witness behaviour that may violate this policy, we encourage you to speak up. You can choose the channel that feels most comfortable for you.

**Talk to someone you trust.** You can raise a concern with your manager, with any member of the People & Culture team, or with the VP People & Culture, Aylin Demir. You can also speak to the Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar, if your concern involves the use of company systems.

**Use the People team directly.** You can email people@kranich.example. Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, will acknowledge your message and help you decide on the next steps. You do not need to provide all the details in your first message — a brief description is enough to get started.

**Contact security if appropriate.** If your concern involves a potential breach of company systems or data, you can email security@kranich.example instead. The security team will coordinate with People & Culture to make sure your concern is handled properly.

You may raise a concern anonymously if you prefer. We cannot guarantee anonymity in every situation, but we will do our best to protect your identity while still conducting a fair process. If you choose to remain anonymous, please provide as much detail as possible so that we can follow up effectively.

You do not need to be certain that a violation occurred. If something feels wrong to you, that is enough reason to raise it. We would rather look into a concern that turns out to be unfounded than miss a situation where someone needs help.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, overheard a remark during a project meeting that he found demeaning. He was not sure whether the speaker intended harm, but he emailed people@kranich.example the same day to describe what he had heard. The People team thanked him and explained what would happen next.

## 4. What happens next

Once you raise a concern, People & Culture will review it promptly. Every concern receives a first response within 7 business days. This first response will confirm that we have received your concern and outline the steps we will take.

The response may come from People & Culture, or from another person who is best placed to handle the situation. In most cases, we will begin by gathering information. We will speak with you to understand what happened, and we may speak with the person involved and any witnesses you identify.

We will treat all information as confidentially as possible. Only those who need to know about the concern will be involved in the process. We will ask everyone we speak with to respect the confidentiality of the matter.

Depending on what we find, we may take a range of actions. These can include coaching, a formal warning, changes to team structures, or — in serious cases — termination of employment. We will also consider whether any changes to our processes or culture are needed to prevent similar situations in the future.

We will keep you informed of progress where we can. In some situations, we may not be able to share every detail of the outcome due to privacy obligations to others. We will always explain what we can, and we will let you know when a matter has been resolved.

If you are not satisfied with how your concern was handled, you can escalate it to Aylin Demir, VP People & Culture. If your concern involves Aylin herself, you can raise it directly with the CEO, Marta Lindqvist.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, raised a concern about a comment made during a distributed team meeting. Within the stated timeframe, she received a response from People & Culture confirming that the matter would be reviewed. A few days later, she was invited to a short conversation to provide additional context. The matter was resolved with a conversation between the parties involved, and Anna confirmed that she felt comfortable moving forward.

## 5. Questions

If you have any questions about this policy, how to raise a concern, or what behaviour is acceptable, please reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example. You can also speak to your manager or to any member of the People team in confidence.

We also encourage you to review related policies in the Grove policy library, including POL-004, which covers respectful communication in digital channels. If you are unsure whether a particular situation falls under this policy, ask us — we would rather clarify than have you stay silent.

Speaking up helps us build the kind of company we all want to work for. Thank you for doing your part.
