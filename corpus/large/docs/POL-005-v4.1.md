# IT & Information Security Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how we protect Kranich's information, our customers' data, and the tools we rely on every day. We all share responsibility for keeping our systems secure, whether we work from the Berlin or Lisbon office or remotely from home.

This version supersedes version 3.0 of this policy. It reflects how we work today, with clearer guidance on accounts, devices, and incident reporting. The policy applies to every employee, contractor, and intern, as well as to anyone else who accesses Kranich systems.

We keep the rules practical. Security should not get in the way of doing great work for our customers; it should make that work safe and reliable. If a rule feels unclear or impractical in your role, reach out to the Information Security team rather than quietly working around it.

## 2. Accounts and passwords

Every person who needs access to Kranich systems gets an individual account through our identity management process. Sharing accounts is never permitted, even temporarily. Your account represents you, and any action taken under it is your responsibility.

Passwords must be at least 16 characters long. We recommend using a passphrase, such as a short sentence or a combination of words that is easy for you to remember but hard for others to guess. Do not reuse passwords from personal accounts or from previous employers.

We provide 1Password as our password manager. Store all work-related credentials there, and use its generator to create unique passwords for every service. If you ever suspect that one of your passwords has been compromised, change it immediately and report the incident as described in §5.

Multi-factor authentication is required on all systems that support it. Use an authenticator app rather than SMS where possible. If you lose the device that carries your authenticator app, contact it-help@kranich.example promptly so we can help you regain access securely.

When you change roles within Kranich, your access rights are reviewed and adjusted. When you leave the company, your access is revoked on your last working day. Managers should notify People & Culture of departures promptly so this happens on time.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, joined a new project team and needed access to an additional internal tool. Her manager requested the access through the standard process, and the Platform & Infrastructure team granted it for the duration of the project only.

## 3. Security training

Security awareness is a continuous practice, not a one-time event. We want everyone to recognize common threats and know what to do when something feels off.

New hires complete the mandatory security training within 14 days of their start date. The training covers phishing, password hygiene, data classification, and incident reporting. You will receive an invitation by email on your first day; if you do not see it, contact it-help@kranich.example.

All employees complete a short refresher training each calendar year. The refresher keeps you up to date on emerging threats and any changes to our tools or processes. It takes about the same time as a typical team meeting and can be completed in one sitting.

Leaders are expected to encourage their teams to make time for training. If you manage someone who has not completed the required training, follow up with them personally. Completion status is visible in Fern, our HRIS.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, completed his mandatory training during his first week. A few months later, he received a suspicious email that looked like it came from a client. Because of the training, he checked the sender address carefully, did not click the link, and reported it to the security team.

## 4. Devices

Kranich provides you with the equipment you need to do your job. Company-issued laptops are configured with our standard security settings, including full-disk encryption and automatic updates. Do not disable or alter these settings.

Keep your operating system and applications up to date. Automatic updates are enabled on company devices; if you see a prompt to restart for an update, do so at the end of your working day or whenever you can take a short break.

Use the company-provided virtual private network when connecting to internal systems from any network that is not your home or office network. This includes cafés, co-working spaces, hotels, and client sites.

Lock your screen whenever you step away from your device, even for a moment. Use a screen lock that activates automatically after a short period of inactivity.

Do not install unauthorized software on company devices. If you need a tool that is not in our standard catalog, request it through it-help@kranich.example. The Platform & Infrastructure team will assess it for security and compatibility.

Physical security matters too. Do not leave laptops or phones unattended in public places. When traveling, keep your device with you as carry-on luggage. If a device is lost or stolen, report it immediately as described in §5.

Personal devices are not permitted to access Kranich internal systems unless they are enrolled in our mobile device management program. If you use a personal phone to read work email, it must be enrolled and protected by a passcode or biometric lock.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, sometimes works from a co-working space near her home. She always connects through the virtual private network and keeps her laptop with her when she goes to get coffee, even if she only steps away for a minute.

## 5. Reporting incidents

If you suspect a security incident, report it promptly. Do not wait to be sure, and do not try to investigate on your own. Early reporting helps us contain damage and protect our customers' data.

Examples of incidents include: a phishing email you clicked on, a lost or stolen device, unusual account activity, a suspected malware infection, or a data breach involving customer information.

Report incidents by emailing security@kranich.example. Include what happened, when you noticed it, and any steps you have already taken. If you are unsure whether something counts as an incident, report it anyway.

After you report, the Information Security team will acknowledge receipt promptly and guide you through next steps. You will not be blamed for reporting a genuine mistake. We would rather hear about a hundred false alarms than miss one real incident.

If you lose a device that contains customer data, also inform your manager right away. The manager will help coordinate with Customer Success if any client communication is needed.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, received a message on a messaging platform that appeared to come from our CEO asking her to buy gift cards. She recognized the signs of a social engineering attempt, did not respond, and forwarded the message to security@kranich.example.

## 6. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, contact the Information Security team at security@kranich.example. For questions about how this policy applies to your specific role or a tool you use, start with your manager or your team's lead.

You can also find practical guides and frequently asked questions on Grove, our intranet. The Information Security team updates those pages whenever processes change.

If you believe a rule in this policy creates an unnecessary obstacle to your work, tell us. We review this policy regularly and welcome suggestions for improvement. Security works best when it is a shared effort, and your experience in daily work is valuable input.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, had a question about how to handle a contractor's access when a project ended. She found the answer on Grove and, when the guide was not fully clear, emailed security@kranich.example for clarification. The team updated the guide the same week.
