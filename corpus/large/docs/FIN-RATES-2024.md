# Expense Rates & Per-Diems 2024

## 1. Purpose

This document sets the per-diem rates that apply to business travel in 2024. Use these rates when you submit an expense report in Ledgerly for a trip taken between January 1 and December 31, 2024.

The rates cover meals and incidental expenses for full travel days. They are meant to keep things simple: instead of collecting individual receipts for every coffee or snack, you claim the daily rate for the country where you are traveling. Accommodation and transport are handled separately through Ledgerly and are not part of this sheet.

If your role involves regular travel, please review this sheet before your first trip of the year so you know what to expect. For questions about how to apply a rate, see §5.

## 2. Per-diem rates by country

The rate applies per full calendar day of travel in the country listed. Choose the rate for the country where you spend the night. If a trip crosses several countries, claim the rate for each day based on where that day ends.

| Country | Rate |
|---|---|
| Germany | €39 per day |
| Portugal | €37 per day |
| Spain | €21 per day |
| Poland | €69 per day |
| France | €50 per day |
| United Kingdom | €47 per day |

These rates apply to all employees regardless of their home office. For example, Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, uses the Portugal rate when she travels within Portugal and the Germany rate when she visits the Berlin office. Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, uses the Poland rate when she spends a day with a client in Warsaw.

## 3. Partial travel days

A full per-diem rate applies only to days that are complete travel days. Trips under eight hours pay 50% of the country rate.

In practice, this means a day trip that starts and ends on the same day and lasts less than eight hours is worth half the daily rate. A day trip of eight hours or more, or any trip that involves an overnight stay, earns the full rate.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer, drives from Berlin to a client site in Poznań, spends the working day there, and returns home the same evening. The whole trip takes about six hours, so he claims 50% of the Poland rate for that day.

For trips that begin or end mid-day because of an early flight or a late meeting, use your judgment: if you were away from home for most of the day, claim the full rate; if you left after lunch or returned before dinner, the 50% rate is more appropriate.

## 4. Meals provided by others

If a meal is provided free of charge — for instance, a client buys you lunch, or breakfast is included in your hotel rate — you do not claim the portion of the per-diem that would have covered that meal. The per-diem is meant to cover costs you actually incur, not to become a bonus on top of hosted meals.

There is no fixed deduction table for this. Use a reasonable approach: if only breakfast is included in your hotel stay, claim the full daily rate minus a modest amount for that meal. If all meals are provided, claim only the incidental portion of the rate.

Example: Sofia, a product manager, attends a two-day workshop in Madrid where the organizer covers lunch and dinner on both days. She claims the Spain rate for those days but reduces it to reflect that her main meals were already paid for.

When you submit your report in Ledgerly, add a short note in the comment field explaining which meals were provided. This helps the Finance team process your claim without follow-up questions.

## 5. Questions

If you are unsure which rate applies to your trip, or how to handle a specific situation, reach out to Finance through Ledgerly before you travel. You can also write to the general help alias and your request will be routed to the right team.

For related policies, see POL-004 for the general expense policy. This sheet is valid for calendar year 2024 only; for travel in later years, refer to the applicable rate sheet.
