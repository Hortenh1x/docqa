# Code of Conduct

## 1. Purpose

Kranich Software GmbH builds route-optimization software for logistics operators. Our success depends on trust — trust from our customers, from our partners, and from each other. This Code of Conduct describes the standards we hold ourselves to in our daily work, whether we are in the office in Berlin or Lisbon, working remotely from home, or meeting with clients on the road.

This policy applies to every employee, contractor, and intern at Kranich. It also applies to members of the Leadership Team. We expect everyone to read this document carefully and to act in line with its principles. If anything in this policy is unclear, or if you face a situation that feels like a gray area, reach out for guidance rather than guessing.

## 2. How we treat each other

We are a remote-first company with people across Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France, and the United Kingdom. Our teams — Routing Core, Fleet Insights, Platform & Infrastructure, Customer Success, and Go-to-Market — work across time zones and cultures. That diversity makes us stronger, and it requires us to be intentional about respect.

Treat every colleague with dignity. Listen actively, assume good intent, and give constructive feedback in a way that helps people grow. When you disagree, disagree with the idea, not the person. We value directness, but directness never justifies rudeness or personal attacks.

We do not tolerate harassment, discrimination, or bullying of any kind. This includes behavior in meetings, in chat channels, at company events, and in any other work-related context. We make decisions about hiring, promotion, and assignments based on merit and business need alone.

We also respect each other's time. Be punctual for meetings, keep your camera on when you can, and communicate clearly about your availability. If you commit to a task, follow through. If you cannot meet a deadline, tell your team early so they can adjust.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, notices that a colleague in the Berlin office repeatedly interrupts Deniz during video calls. Anna raises the concern privately with the colleague afterward, explaining how the interruptions affect the conversation. She then mentions it to her engineering manager, Ines Duarte, who follows up with the colleague's manager. The behavior stops, and the team's collaboration improves.

Confidentiality is part of respect. Do not share personal information about colleagues without their consent. Do not discuss individual performance or compensation with people who do not need to know. Respect customer data and our internal systems at all times.

Finally, be honest. Do not misrepresent your work, your hours, or your results. If you make a mistake, own it promptly. Errors are learning opportunities; concealment is not.

## 3. Gifts and hospitality

Building relationships with clients and partners often involves gestures of appreciation. A modest gift or a business meal can be appropriate. However, gifts and hospitality can also create the appearance of improper influence. We must remain objective in our decisions and avoid any situation that could compromise, or appear to compromise, our judgment.

You may accept or offer customary business meals and small promotional items of nominal value. Gifts and hospitality above €60 in value must be declared. This includes gifts you receive from a client, vendor, or partner, as well as gifts you offer on behalf of Kranich. Declarations are made through your manager and recorded by the Workplace & Operations team.

Do not accept cash or cash equivalents, such as gift cards, under any circumstances. Do not accept gifts that could be seen as an attempt to influence a business decision, such as a contract award or a renewal. If you are unsure whether a gift is appropriate, declare it or decline it.

When traveling for business or meeting with clients, use good judgment about who pays for what. Kranich pays for its own employees' travel and entertainment unless there is a clear business reason for the client to host. Any exception should be discussed with your manager in advance.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, is offered tickets to a sporting event by a logistics operator that is evaluating our software. The tickets are worth more than €60. Marek thanks the client but declines, explaining that our policy requires him to declare gifts above that value and he prefers to avoid the appearance of bias. He mentions the offer to his manager so the team is aware.

## 4. Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest arises when your personal interests could influence, or appear to influence, your decisions at Kranich. We expect you to act in the best interest of the company and to avoid situations where your loyalty is divided.

Disclose any outside employment, consultancy, or business activity that might compete with Kranich or that could affect your objectivity. If you have a close family member or partner who works for a customer, vendor, or competitor, tell your manager or People & Culture. This disclosure does not automatically mean you must change your role; it simply allows us to manage the situation transparently.

Do not use your position at Kranich for personal gain. Do not direct business to a company you or a relative owns without prior approval. Do not take opportunities for yourself that you discover through your work at Kranich, such as a promising business idea that relates to our market.

When you make purchasing decisions on behalf of Kranich, choose vendors based on quality, price, and service. If you have a personal relationship with a vendor representative, disclose it before the decision is made.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, learns that her sister works for a logistics software provider that is a competitor. Deniz informs her manager and the People & Culture team through the HRIS system Fern. Together they agree that Deniz will not participate in any competitive analysis involving that provider. The disclosure protects both Deniz and Kranich.

## 5. Speaking up

We rely on every employee to help us uphold these standards. If you see something that may violate this Code of Conduct, or if you experience behavior that makes you uncomfortable, speak up.

You can raise a concern with your manager, with People & Culture, with the Head of Information Security Priya Nayar for security-related matters, or with any member of the Leadership Team. If you prefer to remain anonymous, you can use the reporting channel described in our internal policy library on Grove. All reports are taken seriously and handled with discretion.

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith is strictly prohibited. We will not tolerate punishment, exclusion, or any negative consequence for speaking up. If you believe you have experienced retaliation, report it immediately.

We also encourage you to raise concerns early, even when you are not sure something is wrong. A question is always welcome. It is better to ask than to let a small issue grow into a larger problem.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, notices that a colleague appears to be claiming reimbursement for expenses that do not match the receipts in Ledgerly. Sofia is uncertain whether it is a mistake or intentional. She reports her observation to People & Culture, who reviews the matter confidentially. It turns out to be an error in data entry, and the colleague is grateful for the correction.

## 6. Questions

This Code of Conduct cannot cover every situation. If you have a question about whether a particular action is appropriate, ask before you act. You can contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example, the IT help desk at it-help@kranich.example, or the security team at security@kranich.example.

For questions about gifts and hospitality, refer to the related expense guidance in our finance documents. For questions about confidentiality and data handling, see the information security policies maintained by the Head of Information Security.

When in doubt, choose the more transparent path. Disclose more rather than less. A brief conversation with your manager or with People & Culture can resolve most uncertainties quickly. We are all responsible for maintaining the culture of trust that makes Kranich a great place to work.
