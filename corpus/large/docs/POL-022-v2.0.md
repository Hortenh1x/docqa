# External Communications & Social Media

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how we communicate externally and how we use social media, both when speaking for Kranich and when posting on personal accounts. It helps us protect our customers, our reputation and each other while staying open and approachable.

This version supersedes version 1.5. We have updated it to reflect how we work across our offices in Berlin and Lisbon and with colleagues in Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom. The policy applies to everyone at Kranich, regardless of team or role.

External communications include interviews, conference talks, blog posts, podcast appearances, comments on professional forums, and any content posted to social media that mentions Kranich. The same principles apply whether you are a member of Routing Core, Fleet Insights, Platform & Infrastructure, Customer Success or Go-to-Market.

We want you to share your expertise and enthusiasm. This policy is not meant to discourage you. It is meant to give you clarity about when you can speak freely and when you should involve Go-to-Market first.

## 2. Speaking for Kranich

You speak for Kranich when you are invited to do so in your professional capacity, when you represent the company at an event, or when you post content on an official Kranich account. In those situations, your words reflect on all of us.

Be honest, accurate and respectful. Do not disclose confidential information about our customers, our technology or our internal plans. If you are unsure whether something is confidential, treat it as confidential and ask before sharing.

Do not comment on legal matters, customer disputes, financial results or personnel issues. These topics require coordination with the responsible teams. Refer such inquiries to Go-to-Market or to the relevant owner, such as Priya Nayar for security matters.

If a journalist contacts you, you do not need to respond alone. You may acknowledge the inquiry, but please loop in Go-to-Market before providing substantive answers. Go-to-Market will coordinate with the appropriate people, including Marta Lindqvist, Jonas Weber or Tomasz Zieliński when needed.

When you speak at a conference or write an article as a Kranich representative, you are expected to follow our general conduct standards. Be mindful that your audience may include customers, prospects and competitors. Frame your remarks constructively and avoid disparaging others in our industry.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, is invited to speak at a regional logistics meetup. He accepts the invitation and informs Go-to-Market beforehand. In his talk, he describes how route optimization works in general terms and shares lessons from his own experience, without naming specific customers or revealing internal benchmarks.

## 3. Personal accounts

On your personal social media accounts, you are free to express your opinions. Kranich does not monitor or restrict your personal posts, as long as they do not create a conflict with your work or misrepresent the company.

We ask you to keep a few things in mind. If you identify yourself as a Kranich employee in your profile or posts, add a simple disclaimer, such as "Views are my own." This helps readers understand that you are not speaking on behalf of the company.

Do not post anything that could reasonably be seen as confidential company information, even on a personal account. This includes customer names, internal tool details, unreleased product plans and discussions from internal meetings. It also includes screenshots of our systems, such as Perch, Ledgerly or Grove, unless you have approval.

Be thoughtful about connecting your personal opinions to your role. You may advocate for causes you care about, but avoid suggesting that Kranich endorses those causes. Do not use Kranich branding, logos or materials on personal accounts without permission.

If you post about your work life, you may share general experiences. For example, you can say that you enjoy collaborating with colleagues across Europe or that you appreciate our remote-first setup. Please do not post about specific customers, internal projects or details from our offices in Berlin or Lisbon.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, posts on a professional network about a conference she attended. She mentions that she works at Kranich and adds that the views are her own. She does not mention any customer conversations or product feedback she heard during the event.

If you are unsure whether a personal post crosses a line, wait before publishing. You can always ask Go-to-Market or check with your manager. A short pause is better than a post you cannot take back.

## 4. Approval of public content

Some external content requires approval before it is published. This applies to content created in your capacity as a Kranich representative, including blog posts on company channels, official social media posts, press releases, webinar presentations and speaking abstracts.

Content that does not require approval includes routine replies on official accounts handled by Go-to-Market, internal communications, and personal posts that do not mention Kranich.

To request approval, send your draft to Go-to-Market through the usual channel on Grove. Include the intended platform or outlet, the target audience and a short summary of the message. Go-to-Market reviews the draft for accuracy, confidentiality and alignment with our brand voice.

Public content is approved by Go-to-Market within 1 business day. If you submit a draft and do not hear back within that time, follow up with the Go-to-Market team. Approval is not automatic; Go-to-Market may request changes or decline to approve content that does not fit our guidelines.

If your content involves technical details about Routing Core or Fleet Insights, Go-to-Market may consult with the relevant engineering lead, such as Ines Duarte for Routing Core. If your content involves financial information, Go-to-Market will involve Tomasz Zieliński. Plan for this extra step when you prepare time-sensitive content.

Example: Sofia, a product manager for Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, writes a blog post about trends in fleet telematics. She submits her draft to Go-to-Market on a Tuesday morning. Go-to-Market reviews it, asks Sofia to remove a reference to an unnamed customer's deployment, and returns the approved version the same day. Sofia publishes the post on the company blog.

Once content is approved, publish it as planned. If you make significant changes after approval, submit the revised version for a new review. Minor edits for grammar or formatting do not require re-approval.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, start with your manager or the Go-to-Market team. They can help you interpret the guidelines for your specific situation.

For questions about confidentiality or security aspects of external communications, contact security@kranich.example. For questions about internal tools mentioned in this policy, such as Grove or Perch, contact it-help@kranich.example. For general people-related questions, contact people@kranich.example.

If you see an external post about Kranich that seems inaccurate or problematic, do not respond to it directly. Forward the link to Go-to-Market. They will decide whether a response is appropriate and, if so, who should provide it.

Remember that related guidance exists in other documents. For example, POL-004 covers confidentiality expectations more broadly, and FIN-RATES-2026 addresses how we communicate about pricing and rates. When in doubt, refer to those documents or ask.

We trust you to use good judgment. This policy exists to help you share your work confidently and to protect Kranich and our customers. When you are unsure, ask. A quick conversation with Go-to-Market or your manager will usually resolve the question.
