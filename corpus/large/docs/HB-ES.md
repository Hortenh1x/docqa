# Employee Handbook — Spain

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Kranich Software GmbH. We are glad you are part of the team. This handbook explains how employment works for our colleagues in Spain. It covers the essentials: vacation, probation, notice periods, working hours, benefits and more. It is written for you, not for lawyers. If something is unclear, reach out to People & Culture at people@kranich.example.

We are a remote-first company. Most of us work from home within our country of employment. We have offices in Berlin and Lisbon, but you are not required to be there unless your role says otherwise. What matters is that we do great work together and treat each other with respect.

Kranich Route Cloud helps mid-size logistics operators plan better routes. Our teams — Routing Core, Fleet Insights, Platform & Infrastructure, Customer Success and Go-to-Market — build and support that product across Europe. As part of that effort, you will find that we value clarity, kindness and a healthy balance between work and life.

This handbook is one of several country-specific handbooks. The structure is the same across countries, but the numbers differ. Always refer to the handbook for your country of employment.

## 2. Your employment in Spain

Your employment with Kranich Software GmbH is governed by Spanish law and by your individual employment contract. Your contract states your role, your location and the specific terms that apply to you. If there is ever a conflict between this handbook and your contract, your contract takes precedence. If there is a conflict between this handbook and Spanish law, the law takes precedence.

You are employed through our Spanish entity. Your workplace is considered to be your home in Spain, unless your role requires travel or work at a client site. We are remote-first, and we trust you to manage your time responsibly.

Your personal data is handled in line with our privacy practices. For any questions about your data, or about the tools you use, contact it-help@kranich.example. For questions about your contract or personal circumstances, contact people@kranich.example.

We expect all employees to follow our internal policies. You can find them in Grove, our intranet and policy library. The policies most relevant to your daily work include POL-007 on sick leave, POL-004 on information security, and the finance-related policies such as FIN-RATES-2026. Please familiarise yourself with them.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, often helps new starters in Spain find the right policy in Grove. If you cannot find something, she will point you in the right direction.

## 3. Vacation

Employees in Spain receive 23 days of statutory-plus-Kranich vacation per year. This is your paid time off for the calendar year, which runs from January to December.

You accrue vacation from the start of your employment. You are encouraged to take your vacation throughout the year rather than saving it all for the end. We believe rest makes us better at work.

You request vacation through Fern, our HRIS. Submit your request in advance so your team can plan around your absence. Your manager will approve or decline the request. Declines are rare and usually happen when too many team members are away at the same time.

Vacation days that are not used by the end of the year may be lost, unless Spanish law requires otherwise. We encourage you to plan carefully. If you have questions about your balance, check Fern or ask People & Culture.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, plans her vacation around the quieter periods in the logistics calendar. She submits her requests early so her colleagues can cover for her.

## 4. Probation

The probation period in Spain is 3 months. During this time, both you and Kranich can assess whether the role is a good fit.

Probation works like any other period of employment. You have the same rights and responsibilities as after probation, including vacation accrual and access to our tools. The main difference is that either side may end the employment with less notice during probation, as provided by Spanish law.

You will have regular check-ins with your manager during probation. These are opportunities to give and receive feedback. You will also be introduced to the tools you need: Perch for meeting-room and desk booking, 1Password for passwords, Fern for HR matters, Ledgerly for expenses and Grove for policies.

If you feel uncertain about anything during probation, speak up. Your manager, your team and People & Culture are there to help you succeed.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, spent his probation learning the product and meeting the Customer Success team. He found the regular check-ins helpful for understanding expectations.

## 5. Notice periods

The contractual notice period in Spain is 9 weeks. This applies when you resign or when Kranich terminates your employment, unless Spanish law provides for a different period in specific circumstances.

You must give notice in writing. Send your resignation to your manager and to People & Culture. The notice period starts on the day we receive your written notice, as specified in your contract.

During the notice period, you continue to work normally. You remain entitled to your salary and benefits. You also remain bound by your confidentiality obligations and by our internal policies.

If Kranich terminates your employment, we will follow the same notice period unless the circumstances require otherwise under Spanish law. We will always treat you with respect and dignity.

If you have questions about notice, or about how to resign, contact people@kranich.example. We would rather have an open conversation than have you worry in silence.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, once wondered how much notice she would need to give if she ever moved on. She asked People & Culture and received a clear explanation within a few days.

## 6. Public holidays

Employees in Spain observe 9 days of public holidays in the reference year. These are national holidays recognised across Spain. In addition, some holidays are set at the regional or local level. Your specific holiday calendar depends on where you live in Spain.

Public holidays are paid days off. If a public holiday falls on a weekend, the rules for compensation vary by region. Kranich follows the local rules that apply to your workplace.

You do not need to request public holidays through Fern. They are already accounted for in your calendar. If you are unsure whether a particular day is a holiday in your region, check with your manager or People & Culture.

Public holidays are separate from your vacation days. Taking a public holiday does not reduce your vacation balance.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, checks her regional holiday calendar at the start of the year. She plans her vacation around the holidays so she can take longer breaks without using too many days.

## 7. Working hours

The standard working week in Spain is 39.0 hours per week. This is the reference for full-time employment. Your contract may specify how these hours are distributed across the week.

We are a remote-first company. We do not track your hours minute by minute. Instead, we agree on goals and outcomes. You are expected to be available during core collaboration hours, which your team will communicate. Outside those hours, you have flexibility in how you structure your day.

You should take breaks during the working day. Spanish law provides for rest periods, and we encourage you to step away from your screen regularly. If you work from home, set clear boundaries between work and personal time.

If your role requires you to work outside your standard hours occasionally — for example, to support a client — discuss this with your manager in advance. We do not expect regular overtime. If overtime does occur, your manager will work with you to adjust your schedule.

Use Fern to record any leave or absences. Use Ledgerly to record any work-related expenses. If you have questions about your working time, talk to your manager or People & Culture.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, structures her day around client calls in the morning and quiet work in the afternoon. She makes sure to take a proper lunch break every day.

## 8. Sick leave basics

If you are unwell and cannot work, let your manager know as soon as you can. You do not need to provide details of your illness — just tell them you are unwell and roughly when you expect to return.

For absences due to illness, you must follow the sick-note rules in POL-007. That policy explains when you need a medical certificate and how to submit it. Please read POL-007 carefully so you know what is expected.

While you are on sick leave, you continue to receive your salary according to Spanish law and our internal practices. Your vacation days continue to accrue during sick leave.

If you are sick for a longer period, stay in touch with your manager and with People & Culture. We will support your recovery and your return to work. We understand that health comes first.

If you need time off to care for a sick family member, check with People & Culture. Spanish law provides for certain care-related leave, and we will follow the applicable rules.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer in Poland, once caught a bad flu. He messaged his manager in the morning, followed the sick-note rules in POL-007, and rested. When he returned, his team briefed him on what he had missed.

## 9. Remote work in Spain

You are employed to work remotely from Spain. This is your default arrangement. You do not need to ask for permission to work from home.

You may occasionally work from a different location within Spain for a short period. If you plan to do so, let your manager know in advance. This helps your team coordinate.

If you wish to work from another country temporarily, even within the European Union, you must seek approval first. Cross-border remote work can affect payroll, taxes and social security. People & Culture and Finance will guide you through the process. Do not assume it is fine just because the country is nearby.

Your home office should be a place where you can work productively. Kranich provides the equipment you need. If you need something for your workspace, discuss it with your manager and use Ledgerly for any approved expenses.

You are responsible for ensuring that your home workspace is safe and suitable. We trust you to set it up well. If you have concerns about your setup, raise them with your manager.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, sometimes works from a friend's apartment in Porto for a week. She tells her manager in advance and makes sure she has a reliable internet connection.

## 10. Benefits in Spain

Kranich offers a benefits package to employees in Spain. The details are described in your contract and in our internal documentation. Benefits may change from year to year, and we will communicate any changes clearly.

You have access to the standard benefits required by Spanish law, including social security coverage. In addition, Kranich provides private health insurance for you and your family. Details are in your contract.

You also have access to our internal tools. Perch lets you book meeting rooms and desks when you visit an office. 1Password keeps your passwords safe. Fern is where you manage leave and personal data. Ledgerly is where you submit expenses and book travel. Grove is where you find policies and company news.

We offer learning and development opportunities through our internal programmes. If you want to grow your skills, talk to your manager. We value curiosity and continuous improvement.

Benefits are the same for all employees in Spain, regardless of where they live in the country. If you have questions about a specific benefit, contact people@kranich.example.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights who works remotely from Spain, uses her private health insurance for an annual check-up. She finds the process straightforward.

## 11. Payroll and taxes

Your salary is paid monthly by bank transfer. The payment date is stated in your contract. You will receive a payslip each month that shows your gross salary, deductions and net pay.

Kranich handles payroll through our Spanish payroll provider. Taxes and social security contributions are deducted at source, as required by Spanish law. You do not need to do anything for these deductions — they happen automatically.

You are responsible for filing your personal income tax return in Spain. Kranich provides the annual tax summary you need for your filing. People & Culture will let you know when the summary is available.

If your personal circumstances change — for example, if you marry, have a child or move within Spain — please update your details in Fern. Some changes may affect your tax withholding. People & Culture can advise you on what to do.

If you have questions about your payslip, your taxes or your payroll deductions, contact people@kranich.example. For questions about expenses or travel, use Ledgerly and refer to FIN-RATES-2026.

Example: Lea, a people operations specialist in Berlin, helps employees in Spain understand their annual tax summary each year. She sends a reminder when the documents are ready.
