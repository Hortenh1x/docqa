# IT & Information Security Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how we keep Kranich Route Cloud, our internal systems, and the data we handle safe and secure. It applies to everyone at Kranich Software GmbH, whether you work from the Ritterstraße office in Berlin, the Rua do Alecrim office in Lisbon, or remotely from home in any of the countries where we employ people.

Information security is a shared responsibility. Our customers — mid-size logistics operators — trust us with sensitive route and fleet data. A single careless action can compromise that trust. This policy sets clear expectations so that we all know what to do and what to avoid.

If you are ever unsure whether an action is safe or permitted, err on the side of caution and ask. The Information Security team is here to help, not to punish honest mistakes. Reach us at security@kranich.example.

## 2. Accounts and passwords

Every employee receives a Kranich account when they join. Use that account only for work purposes and never share it with anyone, including colleagues. If someone else needs access, they should request their own account through the normal onboarding process.

Passwords must be at least 10 characters long. Choose a passphrase that is easy for you to remember but hard for others to guess — for example, a combination of words that are meaningful to you but not publicly associated with you. Do not reuse passwords from personal accounts.

Use the password manager 1Password to store all your work passwords. Never write passwords down on paper, store them in plain text files, or send them over email or chat. If you suspect that your password has been compromised, change it immediately and report the incident as described in §5 of this policy.

Where multi-factor authentication is available, you must enable it. This adds a second layer of protection beyond your password and significantly reduces the risk of unauthorized access.

When you leave Kranich or change roles, your access will be adjusted accordingly. You are responsible for the activity that happens under your account, so always lock your screen when you step away from your device, even for a moment.

## 3. Security training

Security awareness is a skill that improves with practice. New hires complete the mandatory security training within 14 days of their start date. The training covers recognizing phishing attempts, handling customer data responsibly, and reporting suspicious activity.

The training is delivered through Grove, our intranet and policy library. You will receive an invitation with a link to the course on your first day. The course takes roughly an hour and can be completed in one sitting or in shorter sessions.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, joined Kranich in the middle of a busy sprint. She completed the security training over two evenings, finishing well within the required window. Since then, she has learned to spot phishing emails that pretend to be from internal tools.

All employees, regardless of seniority, must refresh their security knowledge periodically. The Information Security team sends short reminders and updates through Grove. Pay attention to these — they often contain examples of real threats we have observed.

If you feel that the training does not address a situation you face in your daily work, tell your manager or the Information Security team. We continuously improve the curriculum based on feedback from people like you.

## 4. Devices

Kranich provides company devices for your work. These devices are configured with security controls that protect both you and our customers. Do not remove, disable, or tamper with these controls.

Company devices are for work use. Occasional personal use, such as checking your calendar or reading the news, is acceptable as long as it does not interfere with your duties or expose company data to risk. Do not install unauthorized software, browser extensions, or applications on company devices. If you need a tool that is not in the standard catalog, request it through it-help@kranich.example.

Keep your operating system and applications up to date. Automatic updates are enabled on company devices; if you see a prompt to restart for an update, do so promptly. Delaying updates leaves known vulnerabilities open.

When working remotely, use only trusted networks. Public Wi-Fi in cafés, airports, or hotels is not secure. If you must connect through such a network, use the company virtual private network. Never handle customer data over an unsecured connection.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, often travels to customer sites. He always carries his company laptop and uses the virtual private network when connecting from hotel Wi-Fi. He never plugs unknown USB drives into his device, even when a client offers one with route data — he requests files through secure channels instead.

Physically protect your devices. Never leave a laptop unattended in a public space, even for a short time. When traveling, keep devices with you as carry-on luggage rather than in checked baggage. If a device is lost or stolen, report it immediately as described in §5.

## 5. Reporting incidents

An information security incident is any event that could compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of our systems or data. This includes lost or stolen devices, suspected phishing, unauthorized access to accounts, malware infections, and accidental exposure of customer data.

If you notice something suspicious, report it promptly — do not wait to confirm that it is a real threat. Early reporting helps us contain damage and protect others. You can report an incident by emailing security@kranich.example or by contacting the Information Security team directly through internal chat.

When reporting, include as much detail as you can: what happened, when you noticed it, and what you were doing at the time. Do not attempt to investigate or fix the issue yourself beyond basic steps like disconnecting from the network. Your safety and the integrity of the evidence matter more than speed.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, received an email that looked like it came from a customer asking for route data. Something felt off — the sender address was slightly different from the usual one. She forwarded the email to security@kranich.example without clicking any links. The Information Security team confirmed it was a phishing attempt and blocked the sender.

There is no blame for reporting incidents in good faith. We would rather receive ten false alarms than miss one real threat. Concealing an incident, whether accidental or intentional, is a serious matter and may lead to disciplinary action.

If you are unsure whether something counts as an incident, report it anyway. The Information Security team will triage and advise you on next steps.

## 6. Questions

If you have questions about this policy or about information security in general, there are several ways to get help.

- For technical issues with devices, accounts, or access, contact it-help@kranich.example.
- For security-specific concerns or to report an incident, contact security@kranich.example.
- For questions about how this policy relates to your employment, contact people@kranich.example or your manager.

You can also reach out directly to Priya Nayar, Head of Information Security, or to any member of the Information Security team. We are happy to explain the reasoning behind a rule or to help you find a secure way to do your work.

Remember that security is not a one-time task but an ongoing practice. Stay curious, stay vigilant, and speak up when something seems wrong. Together, we keep Kranich Software GmbH and the customers who rely on us safe.
