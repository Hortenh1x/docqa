# Performance Review Policy

## 1. Purpose

At Kranich Software, we want every employee to understand how their work contributes to our mission and to have a clear picture of where they stand. Performance reviews are one of the ways we make that happen. They give us a shared moment to step back from day-to-day work, reflect on achievements and challenges, and agree on what comes next.

This policy describes how performance reviews work at Kranich: when they happen, what ratings mean, and how we make sure the process is fair across teams and offices. It applies to all employees in Germany, Portugal, Spain, Poland, France and the United Kingdom, regardless of whether you work from home or from one of our offices in Berlin or Lisbon.

The review process is a conversation first and a form second. Managers and employees should treat it as an opportunity for honest, constructive dialogue. The written record that comes out of a review helps with decisions about development and career progression, but it is not meant to be a surprise — you should never hear something in a review that you have not heard before.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, finds her review most useful when her manager points to specific client conversations from the past year. That kind of concrete feedback is what we encourage across the company.

## 2. Review cycle

The performance review cycle runs once per calendar year. The People & Culture team opens the cycle in the first quarter, and all steps — self-review, manager review, calibration and the review conversation — are completed within the cycle period.

The cycle begins when People & Culture announces it through Grove and by email. At that point, every employee receives a task in Fern with instructions for their self-review. Self-reviews are due within 7 working days of the cycle opening. This gives everyone enough time to reflect without dragging the process out.

After the self-review deadline, managers have a window to prepare their own assessments. Managers should review the employee's self-reflection, gather input from colleagues where relevant, and look back at notes from one-on-one meetings throughout the year. We encourage managers to keep informal notes after each one-on-one so that the annual review is based on the whole year, not just the most recent weeks.

Once manager assessments are complete, the review conversation takes place. This is a dedicated meeting between the employee and their manager, separate from regular one-on-ones. The conversation should cover the employee's self-reflection, the manager's assessment, and jointly agreed goals for the coming year. After the conversation, the manager records the final outcome in Fern, and the employee receives a written summary.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer who works with clients in Poland, travels frequently and sometimes misses internal announcements. When the cycle opens, his manager reminds him personally during their one-on-one, and Marek completes his self-review on time from his home office.

## 3. Ratings

Every employee receives a single overall rating for the review cycle. The rating reflects performance over the entire period, not isolated events. Ratings are described in words, and each rating has a clear meaning:

- **Exceeds expectations** — the employee consistently delivered above what their role requires, took initiative beyond their core remit, and had a measurable impact on their team or the company.
- **Meets expectations** — the employee did what their role requires, delivered solid work, and met the goals agreed in the previous cycle. This is the expected outcome for most employees in most years.
- **Partially meets expectations** — the employee met some goals but fell short in meaningful areas. This rating prompts a development conversation and a clear plan for improvement.
- **Does not meet expectations** — the employee fell significantly short of what their role requires. This rating triggers a structured performance improvement conversation between the employee, their manager and People & Culture.

The rating is not a score of the person. It describes how well the employee's work in the review period matched the expectations of their role. A rating of "meets expectations" is a good outcome — it means the employee is doing their job well. We do not force a distribution of ratings across teams, and we do not expect a certain share of employees to receive any particular rating.

Ratings are recorded in Fern and become part of the employee's personnel file. They inform decisions about salary adjustments and role changes, but no decision is made on ratings alone. Managers should always consider the full context of an employee's situation, including any personal circumstances that may have affected performance during the year.

Example: Sofia, a product manager in Fleet Insights working remotely from Spain, received "exceeds expectations" after leading a major feature release. Her manager based the rating on documented feedback from engineering and customer success, not on Sofia's own account alone.

## 4. Calibration

Calibration is the step where managers come together to review proposed ratings before they are final. Its purpose is fairness: we want an employee doing similar work in Lisbon and Berlin to be assessed against similar standards, and we want to avoid one manager being systematically stricter or more generous than another.

Calibration happens in sessions facilitated by People & Culture. Managers from related teams meet to discuss the ratings they have proposed for their direct reports. Each manager presents a short summary of the employee's work and the proposed rating. Colleagues can ask questions, offer additional context, and — where there is good reason — suggest a different rating. The final decision rests with the employee's own manager, but the discussion should be genuine. If a manager disagrees with the group's feedback, they should be able to explain why.

Participation in calibration is mandatory for all managers with direct reports. Sessions are scheduled during the review cycle and announced in advance. Managers should come prepared with notes from the review period. Calibration discussions are confidential; what is said in the room stays in the room.

Calibration also serves a second purpose. It gives managers a chance to spot patterns across teams — for example, where goals were set too ambitiously or too loosely at the start of the year. Those insights feed back into how we set goals for the next cycle.

Example: Ines Duarte, an engineering manager in Berlin, proposed a rating of "partially meets expectations" for one of her reports. During calibration, a peer manager pointed out that the employee had been covering for a vacant role for several months. After discussion, Ines revised the rating to "meets expectations" with a note about the context.

## 5. Questions

If you have questions about this policy, the review cycle, or your own review, there are several places to turn.

Start with your manager. They are the best person to explain how the process applies to you and what you can expect. If you do not feel comfortable raising something with your manager, or if you have already done so and still have concerns, contact People & Culture at people@kranich.example. Lea, our people operations specialist in Berlin, coordinates the review process and can help with practical questions about Fern, timelines and documentation.

For questions about how ratings affect compensation, refer to the compensation framework described in POL-004. The Finance team handles questions about payroll and benefits through Ledgerly; you can reach them through the usual expense and HR channels.

If you believe your review was not conducted fairly, or if you experienced bias during the process, you can raise a concern with People & Culture or, for matters involving confidentiality or data handling, with Information Security at security@kranich.example. Priya Nayar, our Head of Information Security, oversees how review data is stored and accessed in Fern.

We also encourage you to give feedback about the process itself. The review cycle should improve over time, and your experience is the best source of insight. People & Culture will send a short feedback request after the cycle closes; you can also share thoughts at any time through Grove.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, wonders whether the rating scale applies the same way to engineers as to people in client-facing roles. She writes to people@kranich.example, and Lea explains that the same scale applies to all roles, though the expectations themselves differ by function and seniority.

We believe that fair, transparent reviews make Kranich a better place to work. Thank you for taking the process seriously and for contributing to the company's growth.
