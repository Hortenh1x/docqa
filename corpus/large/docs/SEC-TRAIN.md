# Security Awareness Programme

## 1. Purpose

Security is everyone's responsibility at Kranich Software GmbH. Our clients trust us with sensitive route and fleet data, and a single careless action can undo the work of our entire Platform & Infrastructure team. This programme ensures that every employee understands the threats we face, recognises risky situations, and knows how to respond.

The programme is not about testing or punishing people. It is about building good habits together. We want you to feel confident spotting a phishing email, handling a lost device, or choosing a strong passphrase — without having to think twice.

This document describes the learning modules we offer, how often you will engage with them, and where to go if you have questions. The People & Culture team and Information Security work together to keep the content current and relevant to how we actually work.

## 2. Modules

The Security Awareness Programme consists of several short modules. Each module focuses on one topic and includes realistic examples drawn from our daily work at Kranich.

- **Phishing and social engineering.** How to recognise suspicious messages, what to do when something feels off, and how to report it to security@kranich.example.
- **Password and account hygiene.** Choosing strong passphrases, using 1Password correctly, and enabling multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.
- **Device and workspace security.** Locking your screen, protecting your laptop when travelling, and what to do if a device is lost or stolen.
- **Data handling and classification.** Understanding which information is internal, confidential, or public, and how to share data safely with clients and partners.
- **Remote work awareness.** Securing your home network, avoiding shoulder-surfers in public places, and safeguarding printed materials.

Each module takes only a few minutes to complete. We keep the content practical. For example, rather than explaining phishing in theory, we show you a mock email that looks like it comes from our expense tool Ledgerly and ask you to spot the warning signs.

Example: Marek, a field solutions engineer working with clients in Poland, often checks work email from his phone while travelling. After completing the device security module, he set up remote wipe on his phone and now knows exactly whom to contact at it-help@kranich.example if the device ever goes missing.

## 3. Cadence

New joiners complete the foundational module during their onboarding. The deadline for this is set out in POL-005 §3; please check that document for details. Your manager will remind you, and the People & Culture team tracks completion.

After the initial training, all employees complete a short refresher module on a regular cycle. These refreshers rotate through the topics listed in §2 so that no single subject becomes stale. We also run occasional, unannounced simulated phishing campaigns. These simulations are educational, not punitive. If you fall for one, you will receive a brief explanation and a link to the relevant module — nothing more.

When a new threat emerges that affects how we work, Information Security may publish an ad-hoc alert through Grove. These alerts are short and actionable. You should read them promptly, even if your regular refresher is not due yet.

Example: Deniz, a customer success manager in Berlin, received a simulated phishing email that appeared to come from our HRIS Fern. She clicked the link. The next day, she found a friendly note in her inbox explaining what she had missed and pointing her to the phishing module. She completed it during her lunch break and has not clicked a suspicious link since.

## 4. Questions

If you have a question about the programme, your completion status, or any security topic, you have several options.

- For technical questions about a specific email, link, or device, contact it-help@kranich.example.
- To report a suspected security incident, write to security@kranich.example. You can also report anonymously through the channel described in our internal policy library.
- For questions about training logistics, deadlines, or completion records, contact people@kranich.example.

Your managers and team leads are also happy to help. They receive a summary of which modules their team members have completed, so they can support you if you are struggling to find time.

If you are unsure whether something is a security issue, err on the side of asking. There is no such thing as a silly question when it comes to protecting our clients' data and our own systems.

Example: Anna, a backend engineer in Lisbon, once received an unusual request from a person claiming to be a new client. She was not sure whether to share a sample dataset. Instead of guessing, she forwarded the request to security@kranich.example and asked her engineering manager, Ines Duarte, for advice. Within a few hours, she had a clear answer and a new rule to follow.

We review this programme regularly. If you have ideas for making the modules more engaging or more relevant to your role, please share them with Information Security or People & Culture. Security awareness is a continuous conversation, and your input makes us all safer.
