# IT & Information Security Policy

## 1. Purpose

Our customers trust us with the data that runs their logistics. This policy defines the security baseline every employee follows — devices, credentials, data handling, and what to do when something looks wrong. It is deliberately short; the point is that you actually read it.

## 2. Devices

Work happens on company-managed devices only. IT enrolls every laptop in device management before handover: full-disk encryption, automatic updates, and remote wipe are non-negotiable defaults. Personal devices may access the company calendar and chat, but never source code, customer data, or production systems.

Screens lock automatically after five minutes; lock yours manually whenever you step away, at home exactly as in a café.

## 3. Security training

Every new hire must complete the mandatory security training within 14 days of their start date. The training is assigned automatically in Fern on day one, takes about ninety minutes, and ends with a short quiz. Annual refreshers are assigned each autumn to everyone.

Managers can see completion status for their team in Fern; overdue trainings escalate to the Head of Information Security.

## 4. Passwords and authentication

Kranich provides 1Password to every employee, and using it is mandatory: every work credential lives in the company vault, no exceptions and no browser password stores. Multi-factor authentication is enforced on all company systems that support it. Never reuse a work password anywhere else.

Example: when Anna joined Routing Core, her first task was importing the team's shared service credentials into the correct 1Password vault — nothing is documented in wikis or chat.

## 5. Data handling

Customer data stays in production systems and approved analytics tools. Do not export customer datasets to laptops, personal cloud storage, or spreadsheets shared by link. Anonymized samples for debugging are created through the process described in the Internal Data Protection Policy (POL-010).

## 6. Network

The company VPN is required on any network you do not control — hotels, cafés, airports, and during workations. Home networks should use WPA2 or better with a non-default router password.

## 7. Reporting security incidents

If you suspect a security incident — a phishing click, a lost device, an odd login notification — report it to security@kranich.example within 24 hours of noticing it. Faster is better, and reporting a false alarm is always the right call. The response process itself is described in the Security Incident Response Runbook (SEC-001).

## 8. Enforcement

Deliberate circumvention of this policy is treated as a serious matter under the Code of Conduct (POL-009). Honest mistakes, promptly reported, are treated as learning opportunities — that is a promise from the Head of Information Security, Priya Nayar.
