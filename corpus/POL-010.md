# Internal Data Protection Policy

## 1. Purpose

Kranich processes two kinds of sensitive data: our customers' operational data (routes, depots, drivers' schedules) and our colleagues' personal data. This policy translates GDPR obligations into everyday rules that fit on one page of habits.

## 2. Ground rules

- **Purpose first.** Personal data is used only for the purpose it was collected for. New purposes need a new assessment — ask the privacy team before building.
- **Least access.** You get access to the data your role needs, nothing more. Access reviews run quarterly per system owner.
- **Shortest life.** Data we no longer need is deleted on schedule, not "kept just in case". Retention schedules live in Grove.

## 3. Customer data

Customer operational data stays inside production systems and the approved analytics stack. Debugging with realistic data uses the anonymized-sample process: request a sample in Grove, and the platform team generates one with identifiers removed. Exporting raw customer data to laptops or personal storage is never acceptable (see also POL-005).

## 4. Employee data

Employee personal data lives in Fern with role-based access. Managers see what they operationally need — absence dates, not medical certificates; salary bands during reviews, not payroll files. People & Culture and payroll providers process the rest under contracts that mirror this policy.

## 5. Sharing with third parties

Any new vendor that touches personal data goes through the privacy review in Grove before a contract is signed: what data, what purpose, where processed, how deleted. The privacy team keeps the processing register current — if your tool is not in the register, it is not approved.

## 6. Data subject requests

Requests from customers' employees or our own colleagues (access, correction, deletion) go to privacy@kranich.example immediately. Statutory deadlines are short; never sit on such a request, even over a weekend.

## 7. Breaches

A suspected personal-data breach is a security incident first: follow the Security Incident Response Runbook (SEC-001) and the reporting duty in POL-005. The privacy team handles regulator and data-subject notifications where required — that assessment is theirs, not yours.

## 8. Training and questions

Data-protection basics are part of the mandatory security training for new hires and the annual refresher. Everyday questions: privacy@kranich.example or #security. When unsure, ask before processing — a two-line question beats a breach report every time.
