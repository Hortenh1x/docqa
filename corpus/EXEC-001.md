# Compensation Bands (Berlin)

Access: Leadership only

## 1. Purpose

Salary bands give leadership a shared reference for offers, promotions and the annual review, so that pay decisions are consistent across teams rather than negotiated case by case. This document holds the current bands for the Berlin roles that account for most of our hiring. It is maintained by the leadership team with People & Culture and is deliberately not in the policy library in Grove: managers receive the band for a specific role from their People partner, not from this document.

## 2. Bands

All figures are annual base salary for full-time employment in Berlin, before bonus and benefits. Bands for Lisbon and for remote contracts in other countries are derived from these on request from People & Culture.

| Role | Annual base salary (Berlin) |
| --- | --- |
| Senior Engineer | €78,000–€96,000 |
| Engineering Manager | €92,000–€115,000 |
| Product Manager | €70,000–€88,000 |

Within a band, the lower part is for colleagues new to the role, the middle for established performance, and the upper part for people who shape how the whole team works. A promotion moves the person into the new role's band, not automatically to its top.

## 3. Annual review budget

The compensation rounds that follow the review cycles (see §5 of HR-003) are funded from a single company-wide pool. The annual salary review budget is 3.5% of total payroll. Leadership allocates the pool across teams based on calibration outcomes and band position, not evenly per head: a colleague sitting low in the band with a strong review takes priority over one already near the top. Promotions agreed in calibration are funded from the same pool. The CFO, Tomasz Zieliński, confirms the pool against the approved budget before the round opens; the VP People & Culture, Aylin Demir, runs the mechanics in Fern.

## 4. Handling

These bands are confidential to the leadership team. They are not shared in full with managers, not stored in Grove or on shared drives, and not quoted in offer letters or interviews — a candidate hears an offer, not a band. Leadership members do not forward this document by email; discussion happens in the leadership meeting.

The bands are reviewed once a year, using market data obtained by People & Culture. An individual offer outside a band needs agreement from the CEO, Marta Lindqvist, and is documented with the reasoning. Example: when Routing Core hired a senior engineer whose expectations exceeded the band, Jonas Weber took the case to Marta rather than stretching the offer quietly; the answer was a clearer path to the next role instead of an off-band salary.
