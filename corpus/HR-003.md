# Performance Review Process

## 1. Purpose

Reviews at Kranich answer three questions: how did the last period go, where are you heading, and what support do you need. This document describes the cadence, the inputs, and what happens with the results — so the process is predictable rather than mysterious.

## 2. Cadence

Performance reviews run twice a year, in April and October. Each cycle takes about three weeks from self-review to the closing conversation. Between cycles, feedback stays continuous — the review is a summary, never a surprise.

## 3. The inputs

- **Self-review.** You describe outcomes, not activity: what changed because of your work. Fern provides the (short) template.
- **Peer feedback.** You nominate two to four colleagues; your manager may add one. Peers answer three questions, fifteen minutes total.
- **Manager assessment.** Written against your role profile and the goals agreed in the previous cycle.

## 4. The conversation

The review conversation is an hour, held after you have read the written assessment — no live reveals. It ends with agreed goals for the next period and, where relevant, a development plan: skills, scope, or the path toward a role change.

## 5. Outcomes

Review outcomes feed the compensation round that follows each cycle; salary decisions are made by calibration across teams, not by individual managers alone. Promotions are proposed in the same calibration with written cases.

Example: Anna's case for senior engineer was written in October, calibrated in November, and effective in January — the timeline every promotion follows.

## 6. If the review is difficult

When performance is below expectations, the review says so plainly, together with a concrete support plan and a follow-up date. Nobody is managed out by silence at Kranich; that is a People & Culture commitment.

## 7. Probation check-ins

During the probation period, lighter check-ins replace the full cycle: end of month one with your manager, mid-probation with People & Culture. The full review rhythm starts with the first regular cycle after confirmation.

## 9. Calibration guidance for managers

Access: Managers only

Calibration makes individual assessments consistent across teams. Each manager brings proposed ratings and a short written case per person; People & Culture chairs, and a manager from another team acts as challenger. The bar is the same everywhere.

To keep the top rating meaningful, no more than 20% of a team's ratings may be "exceeds expectations". Above that share, calibration will ask you to separate genuinely exceptional outcomes from a strong year — both are good news, but only the first is the top rating. There is no floor for the lower ratings; nobody is pushed down to fill a curve.

Example: Ines Duarte, engineering manager for Routing Core in Berlin, arrived with more top ratings than the share allows; after the discussion, the strongest cases kept it and the others received "meets expectations" with a note on what would make the difference next cycle. Proposed ratings stay confidential until the review conversation (see §4).
